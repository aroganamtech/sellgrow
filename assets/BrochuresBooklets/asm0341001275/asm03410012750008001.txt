TWENTY-FIVE thousand miles of airways, to speed United States mail and commerce to 32 countries, form America’s international air transport system. These great trunk airlines of the Pan American Airways System give the same unparalleled speed advantages to business and pleasure travel. In five short years they have become the standard air transport system of the world. Their record for on-time dependability is matched by few railroads.
Operating through 110 airports, a private radio network of 59 stations, as many weather bureaus, and a fleet of 64 multi-engined airliners, the "Flying Clipper Ships” of America’s "Aerial Merchant Marine,” Pan American is by far the largest of airways systems.
More than 165,000 passengers have flown 51 million miles with Pan American—9 million pounds of air mail and express have been carried—with the highest rated efficiency—99.81%—of any airline in the world.
