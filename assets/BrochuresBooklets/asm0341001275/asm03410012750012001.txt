IVE brief hours from Brownsville, Texas,
western terminal of America’s great interna-
tional airways . . . along the Gulf to Tampico . . . over foliage-covered mountains to Mexico City . . . one of the world’s most interesting capitals . . . Aztec mementos . . . gracious old Spanish cathedrals, palaces, courtyards, magnificent boulevards . . . interesting, hospitable people, in colorful native costume . . . shops brimful of lovely Indian artistry . . . the bull fight . . . age-old gardens through which ply picturesque gondolas, music makers, flower girls. . . .
Beyond, intriguing countryside . . . pyramids older than Egypt’s . . . fiestas, music, dances. . . .
Mexico, still unspoiled by hordes of tourists . . . uncrowded, unhurried . . . spring-like days, refreshingly cool evenings . . . perfect attractions for a winter holiday of a week—or a season. . . .
Five Hours, now, from Brownsville ... or two days by connecting train or plane from the Middle West, the East, New England. . . . Two days from Miami, also, by way of Cuba ... or Florida —Cuba—Mexico City—Texas in three days of striking scenic contrasts.
