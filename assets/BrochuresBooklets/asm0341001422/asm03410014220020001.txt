DOES PAN AMERICAN HAVE COMPETITION?
Pan American is engaged in two kinds of airline busine ss--quite different from each other-=both highly competitive.
The service to the United States Territories is within the domestic market, protected against foreign-flag competition. The carriers on these routes earn their revenues in United States dollars, have about the same wage scales, and are subject to a single regulatory agency, the United States Civil Aeronautics Board.. The traffic density on most of these routes, however, is low compared to that in the Continental United States.
Each of Pan American's services to the Territories is paralleled by one or more U. S. -flag carriers; to Puerto Rico, by Eastern Air Lines; to Hawaii, by United Air Lines and Northwest Airlines; to the Panama Canal Zone, by Braniff Airways; to Alaska by Northwest Airlines, Pacific Northern Airlines and Alaska Airlines.
The situation on the international routes is entirely different from that on these territorial routes. Revenues must be accepted in a multitude of foreign currencies, many of them fluctuating in value and not readily convertible into dollars. On all these routes there are foreign-flag competitors--usually a number of them. As shown subsequently, the wage rates paid by these foreign-flag competitors are far below those of the U. S. -flag carriers. Instead of a single regulatory agency, each foreign government on the route has sovereign power.
