HOW MUCH DOES THE GOVERNMENT PAY PAN AMERICAN IN SUBSIDY AND WHAT DOES IT GET IN RETURN?
The Civil Aeronautics Board had estimated that subsidy to Pan American for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1955 would be $18, 728, 000. This would represent some 8. 3% of Pan American’s total revenues.
Pan American provides transportation for 28. 2% of all passengers traveling between the United States and its overseas territories and foreign countries (except Canada and Mexico) by all means, both air and sea. It carries 42. 8% of those traveling by air and 62. 2% of the U. S. airmail to the territories and foreign countries. It keeps in readiness 62,751 miles of routes and 93 overseas stations. Its fleet of 109 four-engined, long-range transports, along with the crews ready to man them and the mechanics ready to maintain them, could provide the capacity in a sudden emergency to move, as an example, from the United States to Europe, 7, 600 troops or 1,750, 000 pounds of cargo in one day. The cost to the Government of maintaining these aircraft and the flight crews alone would amount to $83, 000, 000 a year.
Pan American participates in an arrangement with the United State Government for a Civil Reserve Air Fleet whereby a large amount of overseas airlift will be available to the Armed Forces on 48 hours' notice, in the event of national emergency. Approximately 45 four-engine long range, modern passenger and cargo transports have been committed by Pan Ameri can to this program; this constitutes about 40% of the Company's productive capacity.
