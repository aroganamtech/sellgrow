WHY SHOULD THE UNITED STATES SUBSIDIZE U. S. -FLAG AIR TRANSPORTATION TO ITS TERRITORIES AND FOREIGN COUNTRIES IF FOREIGN LINES WOULD RENDER THIS SERVICE WITHOUT SUBSIDY?
Air transportation has become the principal, and in some instances, the only method of passenger transportation between the United States and its overseas territories. The United States could no more rely on foreign operators for this essential service than it could for air transportation between New York and California.
It would likewise be unthinkable that the United States, the leader of the Free World, should not be a leader in air transportation. The United States airlines which link our own with foreign countries are an example of the success of the American system and are an essential element in maintaining national prestige. Experience in maritime transportation and in communications has shown that trade follows the flag.
Pan American's services over the international trade routes of the air have opened the door to many new markets for American business; have expedited the mails and, specifically, business documents and communications; and have promoted, through speed and ease of delivery, trade in goods between the United States and foreign countries.
From the standpoint of national defense, Pan American maintains a network of strategic air routes and a fleet of aircraft and crews available for instantaneous use in the event of emergency. The United States cannot rely for this on the airlines of even the friendliest foreign countries, which are subject to enemy seizure in the event of war.
