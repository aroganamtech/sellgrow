An important source of an airline's revenue is the carriage of mail for its own government. Most of the large foreign-flag competitors receive from their government rates two or three times greater than those which the U* S< -flag carriers receive for the carriage of United States mail. Financial comparisons are badly distorted by this discrepancy» Foreign-flag competition is constantly on the increase. For example, in 1947, foreign carriers had only 27% of the passengers between the United States and Europe; they carried some 48% in 1954.
