New Zealand
WHAT’S SPECIAL
New Zealand is made up of two islands that have some of the most magnificent and dramatic natural features in the world: the Alpine peaks of Mount Cook and its neighbors (all over 10,000 feet), the towering walls of majestic fjords, and a thermal region displaying bursting geysers, bubbling mud pools, and boiling lakes.
New Zealand has been discovered and rediscovered—first by the Maoris, who called the land Aotearoa—(long white cloud) and settled in the North Island, with their own language, music and culture. Then in 1642 the white men— the pakehas to the Maoris—arrived. Tasman, their leader, called the land New Zealand after his own province in Holland. But he did not settle, nor did Captain Cook who came briefly more than 100 years later. Not until 1840 did the British arrive to stay, and they drew up the Treaty of Waitangi to share rights with the Maoris. New Zealand has kept close ties with Britain—the institutions and way of life are British, and everywhere there is an agreeable and informal friendliness that makes the visitor welcome.
