GETTING AROUND...
For the visitor to London there are three main ways of travelling around the city—taxi, underground and bus. Unless you are going to explore well outside the city it is probably not worthwhile hiring a car, London's roads can get very crowded.
Taxis—Taxis are ideal for short trips, particularly when shopping. The very distinctive, usually black, 'hansome cab' as it is called, is easy to spot. Every taxi carries a lit sign and you can hail them when the orange 'For Hire' sign shows. The doormap or receptionist of your hotel will call a taxi for you or you can telephone one of the following taxi-ranks if you are in central London:
Albert Embankment SE1 735 7837 Kensington High Street W8 937 0736 London Bridge Station SE1 407 1183 Moorgate EC2 606 4526 Russell Square WC1 636 1247 Sloane Square SW1 730 2664.
Taxi drivers are a mine of information about what is where in London. Tip roughly 7’/2p for each 25p of the fare.
Incidentally, until recently every taxi had, by law, to carry a bale of hay in the trunk, to feed the now non-existent horse.
Buses—London's red double decker buses are known throughout the world. They provide not only transport but also an excellent way of seeing the sights of London in comfort. Smoking is only allowed on the upper deck, and not at all on single deck buses. It is a recommended way of getting around the city, except during rush hours when workers are either going to, or coming from the office.

Maps of bus routes, and information about tours can be found at underground stations, free of charge. A Go-As-You-Please ticket will allow you to travel on London Transport for 4 or 7 days and you get a free 20 mile sightseeing trip thrown in.
Green Line or London Country Buses will get you out of the city to the beautiful country-side and historic towns outside London. The enquiry office is at Ecclestone Bridge, Victoria, SW1 834 6563.
Underground—often known as 'The Tube'. A quick way to get around London. Free maps are available from underground stations. Avoid the underground during the rush hour —unless you like crowds.
The Tourist Ticket Office, St James's Park, Station, or one of the London Transport Travel Enquiry Offices at Victoria, Oxford Circus, Piccadilly Circus, King's Cross and Euston Stations, open 8.00—1800 daily, can give you details of special trips and tickets.
Tours—A variety of organised tours, which often include meals and guides in the total cost, are available. The following firms run regular tours by coach or private car.
Thos Cook Et Son Ltd., 45 Berkley Street, W1. 499 4000. Evan Evan's Tours, 74 Russell Square, WC1. 637 4825. Frames Tours Ltd., 25 Tavistock Place WC1. 387 3488.
Other sources of travel information in and around London are the British Rail Travel Centre, Lower Regent Street, SW1, 839 4343, and London Transport, 55 Broadway, SW1, 222 1234.
