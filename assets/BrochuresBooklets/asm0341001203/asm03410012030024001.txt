SIGHTSEEING
If this is your first visit to Britain’s-beautiful and historic capital (or even a return trip), you're probably planning to do some sightseeing while you’re here. The big problem with a city as vast and varied as London is —where do you start? Friends who’ve been here before may have given you a list of places you simply mustn't miss. Or perhaps there’s somewhere you’ve always promised yourself you'd visit. So a word of advice—you’ll find your sightseeing trips much more rewarding if you decide in advance to visit, the places that are really going to interest you, not these you feel you have to see. You’ll go home much more enriched mentally—and a lot less tired physically!
Probably the most painless way of seeing
as many of London’s sights is to book up for one of London Transport’s special sightseeing bus tours. You’ll get a brief glimpse of a wide variety of places and you can go back and take a closer look at what you fancy if you have time later on.
But you may already have a good idea of the sort of things you want to see: old churches, like St. Paul’s Cathedral and Westminster Abbey; historic buildings, like Buckingham Palace and The Law Courts; bustling street markets, like Smithfield and Petticoat Lane; romantic parks, like St. James’s and Regent's Park; world-famous areas like Trafalgar Square and Piccadilly Circus.
