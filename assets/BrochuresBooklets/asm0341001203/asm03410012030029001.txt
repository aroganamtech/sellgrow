BUCKINGHAM PALACE St James's Park SW1 The permanent London Palace of the reigning Sovereign. BURLINGTON ARCADE Piccadilly W1. 1819 Regency shopping promenade with original shop windows. BURLINGTON HOUSE Piccadilly W1. Houses the Royal Academy and various Royal Societies.
THE CENOTAPH Whitehall SW1. To honour the dead of World War I.
CHARING CROSS WC2. The Charing Cross was the last of the stone crosses set up by Edward I to mark the funeral resting places of Queen Eleanor's body on its way to Westminster Abbey.
CHELSEA ROYAL HOSPITAL Chelsea Embankment SW3 A hospital for old soldiers.
CLARENCE HOUSE Stable Yard Gate SW1. Now the home of the Queen Mother.
CLEOPATRA'S NEEDLE Victoria Embankment SW1 Presented by Egypt and set up by the Thames, 1878. COVENT GARDEN MARKET WC2 The market buildings were erected In 1831. The market moves to Nine Elms in 1974 and the consequent redevelopment will drastically change the character of that area.
HMS 'DISCOVERY' Victoria Embankment WC2. Captain Scott relics. Other moored ships are nearby.
DOCKS The Port of London. Stretch from the Tower to Tilbury on the north bank of the Thames.
DOWNING STREET SW1 17th-cent. street houses built by Sir George Downing. No. 1 0 is the official residence of the Prime Minister, No. 11 of the Chancellor of the Exchequer. FLEET STREET EC4 Has been associated with printing since the days of Caxton. All national and most provincial newspapers have their offices In or near it.
GOLDSMITHS' HALL Foster Lane EC2. Occasional exhibitions.
GRAY'S INN Holborn WC1. Entrance from passage next to 22 High Holborn. An inn of Court since 14th century. GREENWICH: OLD ROYAL OBSERVATORY Greenwich Park SE10. Formerly the Greenwich Observatory. Time and astronomical instruments.

HC_BORN VIADUCT. EC1. William Haywood, 1869. Fine example of Victorian cast iron, and a pioneer traffic improvement scheme. «

KENWOOD HOUSE (Iveagh Bequest), Hampstead Lane, NW3. English 18th century paintings and furniture.
LAMBETH PALACE, Lambeth Palace Road, SW1. The London residence of the Archbishop of Canterbury.
LAW COURTS, Strand, WC2. Massive Victorian-Gothic buildings, housing the Royal Courts of Justice. LEADENHALL MARKET, Gracechurch Street, EC3. Victorian glass and iron hall housing the poultry market. LINCOLN'S INN, Lincoln's Inn WC2. An Inn of Court. 17th century.
LINCOLN'S INN FIELDS, WC2. 7 acres of gardens laid out by Inigo Jones 1618. Now office workers play competition games.
‘LITTLE VENICE', Harrow Road, W2. Artists, writers, converted barges and the Grand Union Canal.

SMITHFIELD EC1. Once 'Smooth Field’. Historical site of tournaments, public executions, cattle market and the famous Bartholomew Fair. Smithfield Market is the largest meat market In the world (10 acres).
SOHO. An area bounded by Regent Street. Oxford Street and Charing Cross Road. Lively and notorious but per-fectly safe.
SOMERSET HOUSE, Strand, WC2. Houses the register of births, marriages and deaths in England and Wales, and Inland Revenue.
SPITALFIELDS. This centre of silk weaving in England was established by the Influx of Flemish and French weavers in the 16th and 1 7th century.
STOCK EXCHANGE, Throgmorton Street, EC2. Entrance In Tower Block, Old Broad Street to public gallery high above the turmoil of 3,000 soberly dressed members In milling activity below.
STRAND, WC2. Once a 'strant'—-a walk along the river bordered in Stuart times with mansions and gardens down to the river.
THE TEMPLE. An extensive area of courtyards, alleys, gardens and warm brick buildings.
TEMPLE OF MITHRAS, Queen Victoria Street, EC4. Originally found 18 ft. underground THE TOWER OF LONDON. Tower Hill, EC3. A keep, a prison and still a fortress.
TRAFALGAR SQUARE WC2. Laid out by Sir Charles Barry 1829. Famous for its pigeons and political demonstrations.
WHITEHALL SW1. Wide thoroughfare used for ceremonial and State processions.
