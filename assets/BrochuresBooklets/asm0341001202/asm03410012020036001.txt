Use of coupons, in actual practice, is very simple. With a little care you can avoid the nuisance of too heavy refunds in Italian money, which you’ll want to convert when you are leaving. Also if at any time coupons buy more gas than your tank will hold, be sure the station attendant gives you cash for the difference. Obviously, gas coupons are highly negotiable — safeguard them as you do your money.
In many places, oil is sold in 2-litre cans. But it comes in a can with a screw-top. If your car does not need two litres, screw on the cap and put it in your trunk.
HOW DO YOU HANDLE MONEY?
Travelers Checks are the most convenient way of carrying money for the motoring traveler. Letters of credit, too, if you like — but Travelers Checks eliminate dependence upon accessibility of banks. Have a good number of small denominations as well as large, so that you can whittle down on local currency as you approach a border. A few dollar bills are useful for this same purpose. There are facilities for converting one type of currency to another at border-crossing stations, however.
On leaving the U. S. take with you enough currency of the country in which you will land to cover two or three days’ cash expenses. You can obtain this at your bank.
Be sure to include among your “travel papers” a good currency conversion guide. Any Pan American office will supply an excellent Currency Converter.
HOW COMPLICATED ARE BORDER CROSSINGS?
Not complicated at all! Remember that as a tourist you are a welcome visitor. The necessary formalities are kept to a minimum. You have your documents, passport, and insurance papers in a carefully-guarded pocket (tip: have your tailor install a button on an inside coat pocket).
