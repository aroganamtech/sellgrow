European busses make it a rule to sound a most musical horn (a scale of notes) on every corner; remember, please, that you are expected to answer — a safety measure you will come to admire.
Bear in mind that the best time for European driving is off-season, in spring and fall, when the countryside is at its prettiest. It is at these times, too, when road traffic is lightest and driving easiest.
WHAT ABOUT LANGUAGE?
It is natural for the non-linguist, contemplating striking out on his own in a foreign land, to be concerned about how he will communicate with people. Once again, there is no cause for concern.
For one thing, you will be surprised to find that most of the people who serve you in hotels, inns and restaurants, speak several languages — including English. Almost always there will be someone on the management staff who speaks some English.
But even when you encounter situations where interchange of conversation is impossible because of language lack on both sides, you will find that somehow, and a little miraculously, you can communicate, one with the other.
