WHAT ARE EUROPEAN DRIVERS LIKE?
Europeans take uncommon pride in their cars. They also do a great deal of driving. As a result, the chances are good that you will find far fewer “cowboy” operators than in the U. S. You will be struck by a seeming absence of auto junk yards — possible testimony to the skill of foreign drivers. In most countries, speed limits in the open country are imposed only on weekends (80 kilometers, or 50 miles per hour).
Only places where you need to be en garde are in major cities such as Paris, Rome and London. Yet here is an interesting thing; traffic in these cities seems much more hectic when you are walking, or riding in a taxi. Behind the wheel of your own car, it is not really bad at all. Within a very short time you get the knack of driving in these congested cities — not pressing your advantage too far, making way for the other fellow, a give-and-take technique that makes you an expert. Always, there are beaming policemen to help the visiting American momentarily lost in city traffic.
