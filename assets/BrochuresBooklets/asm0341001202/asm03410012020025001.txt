You will see ancient apple trees espaliered against houses as high as the roof peaks, acres of orchards with trees interwoven in espalier designs. The fall air is crisp and clean, the countryside brilliant. In both seasons, most roads have few cars on them. Accommodations, too, are no problem, and you avoid high-season rates.
In the big cities such as Paris, London and Rome, living costs are comparable with similar accommodations in the U.S. But on the road, you can count on living costs being considerably lower. In excellent hotels in many of Europe’s famous resorts during most of the year, you can get room accommodations for two at rates lower than U.S. hotels. $6 to $8 per day, double, is not at all unusual. Many times, the cost of your meal is more than that of your room — and both are low.
Motels (as yet not too numerous) are surprisingly cheap; there’s one in Interlaken facing on the water, with swans swimming by your window and the towering heights of Grindel-wald as a backdrop, where you can stay for $5 or $6 per day. Even the world-famous Gritti Palace Hotel, facing the Grand Canal in Venice, will lodge you in luxury, stuff you with a breakfast and dinner as fine as you’ll ever eat, for little more than $22 a day for two. The view alone is worth all of this, and more.
