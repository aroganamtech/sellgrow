To sum up: relax about the language problem. It doesn’t exist in any serious way.
WHAT ABOUT GASOLINE AND MECHANICAL SERVICE?
Forget any worries about availability of gasoline and mechanical service. There are stations everywhere — and you will encounter many of the familiar friendly trademarks of the brands you use at home. Service and courtesy in many of these stations is remarkable. You’ll get your side windows washed, as well as front and back — and don’t be surprised if the owner shakes your hand on leave-taking. These are nice people, and you’ll love it.
Garages are plainly marked to indicate makes of cars serviced. Your Michelin Guide will also steer you.
On the Continent, gasoline is sold by the litre, rather than by the gallon (3.75 litres equal a gallon), and by the Imperial gallon (equivalent of 1.2 U. S. gallons) in Great Britain. Gasoline is expensive — but this is balanced by the fact that foreign cars consume very little gas. You can drive great distances before needing to refill.
In Italy, as a visiting motorist, you are entitled to sizeable savings through the purchase of gas coupons, which you then use in lieu of cash. The saving is approximately 30%. A daily allotment of 30 litres (7.5 gallons) is allowed for 10 days. This 10-day allotment may then be repeated at 10-day intervals for a period up to 90 days. Unused coupons must be surrendered before leaving Italy, and refund is given in Italian currency. Gasoline coupons are purchased and refunded at Italian Auto Clubs and at all border stations. (Gas coupons are not issued for cars rented in Italy.)
