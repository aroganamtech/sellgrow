In many big cities special parking places — marked by signs — are provided for visitors.
Finally, you will find a refreshing spirit of helpfulness among Europeans toward the motorist, most especially if you are an American sitting behind the wheel of a parked car with a small frown of bewilderment on your face. More often than not a friendly cyclist or passerby will stop to help, and so great will be his concern you would think he had nothing else to do. These fleeting friends are all a part of your joyous experience.
WHAT ABOUT GUIDE BOOKS?
Europe is good to the American traveler insofar as guide books are concerned. Europeans themselves are great travelers (in off-season, European sight-seers far outnumber Americans). As a result, there are guides galore, covering not only driving routes but hotels, inns, restaurants, and points of interest as well.
It is possible to have too many references — so boil them down to a few. The following are recommended as most helpful:
Pan American's Own "New Horizons World Guide"— thoroughly covers in handy reference form 119 lands round the world, 27 in Europe. Contains monthly weather charts and practical information on dozens of subjects such as local customs, climate, hotels and restaurants with current prices, tipping, points of interest, shopping, entertainment, sports, clothing. Invaluable while planning as well as a useful take-along guide. Has sold over a million copies.
