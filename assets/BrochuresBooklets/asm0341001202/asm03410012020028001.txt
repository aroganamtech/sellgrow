WHAT ARE EUROPEAN ROADS LIKE?
This is a question that concerns most people.
Yet it is the easiest to answer — and in the answering, to give complete assurance. Most roads in Europe are good. The “Autoroutes” of France the “Autostrades” of Italy, the “Autobahnen” of Germany, compare most favorably with our superhighways. Other main roads are every bit as good as the U. S., and even secondary roads pose no driving problems.
Many would-be road travelers look at a topographical map of Switzerland or Austria and shudder at the thought. Nothing could be more foolish. Most of the roads in these countries run along level valley floors, by rivers and lakes, with the mountains that looked so awesome on the map delighting your eye on either side. In mountainous country, you will at times drive through high passes — but you will find that these roads are built for driving, with graded corners, sometimes surfaced with cobblestones, always well marked. Ordinary driving ability is all that is needed. Your driving guides will tell you when certain mountain passes are closed because of snow.
The only countries where driving is done on the left side of the road are Great Britain, Ireland (and Sweden, prior to September, 1967).
Don’t worry — you’ll get used to it quickly.
