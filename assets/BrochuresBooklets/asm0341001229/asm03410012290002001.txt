ALASKA
Years before Lewis and Clark reached the Pacific, Yankee seafarers bound for China were being entertained by hospitable Russians at Sitka, capital of what was then the Czar’s Russian America. Secretary of State William H. Seward in 1867 negotiated the purchase of Alaska for $7,200,000 (about two cents an acre) then considered highly extravagant.
When the steamer "Portland” docked at Seattle, "a ton of gold from Alaska in her hold,” the word flashed by telegraph. The gold rush was on; eyes turned to Alaska.
In fish, furs, and minerals, Alaska has, in the 77 years it has belonged to the U. S., yielded more than $2,000,000,000 — three hundred times its purchase price. The future of this great Territory is assured.
Not least among Alaska’s valuable resources are her hardy citizens, including thousands of hard-working Indians and Eskimos.
