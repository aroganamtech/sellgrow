As countries which were once considered remote and far distant are bound together by jet transport the peoples of these countries will come to know each other better and the commercial jet transport will become a new and effective aid to world brotherhood and understanding.
The new jets not only will be faster than present airline transports, they will be larger. While flight times are cut nearly in half the capacity of international air fleets will be almost doubled.
In the history of transportation increased capacity and better, faster service have always resulted in increased business for the carrier operating the new service. It is anticipated that jet transports will prove no exception to this rule.
A large portion of the vastly increased capacity of the international airlines will be utilized by a new type of air traveler. This new air traveler will be the school teacher taking advantage of jet speed to see the historic sights of Europe; the businessman who once took his vacation in Miami will travel to Honolulu; and, the rancher who fished the streams of his own state will cast for trout in the Andes.
Jet transports are the true mass transportation airplanes. One Boeing 707, because of its great capacity—almost double that of the DC-7C—and its 575 mile-per-hour cruising speed will carry nearly as many transatlantic passengers in a year as the Queen Mary.
