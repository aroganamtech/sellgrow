The Jet Passenger
Pan American’s president, Juan T. Trippe, as long ago as 1944, advocated air transportation within the price range of the average man when he said:	. . air transport
does have the choice—the very clear choice— of becoming a luxury service to carry the well-to-do at high prices or to carry the average man at prices he can afford to pay. Pan American has chosen the latter course.”
In the postwar years Pan American was in the forefront of the campaign to bring international air fares within the reach of the average traveler. In 1952 transatlantic tourist-class fares went into effect culminating a long effort by Pan American in the International Air Transport Association for low-cost air transportation. Tourist-class fares resulted in the reduction of the New York-Lon-don roundtrip fare from $711 to $486.
Again in April, 1958, Pan American made another vital contribution to the average air traveler when Economy service was introduced. This provided a saving of $113 on a New York-London roundtrip as compared to tourist-class fare.
These low-cost fares combined with the speed and capacity of the Jet Clippers will add a new dimension to travel opportunity. Although few places in the world are more than a day away from the United States on present airline schedules, for most travelers the distance to Europe is still measured in miles rather than time.
