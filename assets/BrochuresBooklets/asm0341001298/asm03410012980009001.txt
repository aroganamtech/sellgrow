An innovation in the Boeing 707 Jet Clipper which will add to passenger comfort is a passenger service unit above each seat. This unit contains reading light, seat belt sign, fresh air unit, public address speaker, and a stewardess call button.
Unlike arrangements in current aircraft the passenger service unit is movable so that when changes are made in cabin configuration—from four abreast to five or six abreast —each seat will have its own reading light and other service facilities perfectly aligned for the passenger’s convenience.
The Pan Am Jet Fleet
Pan American’s first scheduled transatlantic jet flight will be operated with the most thoroughly tested new aircraft in aviation history. The prototype Boeing 707 has been flying since July, 1954, and has logged more than 1,000 hours air time, including a record breaking transcontinental flight from Seattle to Baltimore in 3 hours and 48 minutes.
Before the B-707 goes into transatlantic passenger service it is expected that it will have operated a scheduled cargo service on a daily basis.
The Pan American Jet Clipper will show clearly the lines of its jet ancestors. The concept of hanging the jet powerplants in pods under the wings, a feature of American-built jet aircraft, has been proved the most practical design in the manufacturer’s long experience with multi-engined jet aircraft.
