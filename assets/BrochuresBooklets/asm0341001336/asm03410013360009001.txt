1975 — A Year of Challenge and Change
1975 will be a year of tremendous challenge and change for Pan Am. Crucial decisions will be made during the year that will have major impact on the Company’s future direction.
In general, the environment in which Pan Am will be operating will be fraught with economic problems. Inflation rates will remain high in the industrialized countries. In addition, recessionary trends will be experienced in many of the developed nations. Although fuel availability will most likely not present a problem, the price of this important commodity will remain high in 1975.
Industry scheduled traffic over Pan Am sectors is forecasted to decline slightly in 1975 while Pan Am traffic may show a modest growth. This positive growth for Pan Am reflects strong opportunities in the Pacific and in Central and South America.
The overall slump in international travel is a result of the poor economic conditions mentioned above as well as the increase in air fares that occurred throughout 1974 in an attempt to recapture fuel cost escalations. International pleasure-related trips have and will continue to suffer the most from the fare increases whereas business travel in 1975 will tend to remain constant.
Many international carriers recorded significant losses for 1974. This situation has brought new pressures to bear on airlines and government agencies to introduce new concepts which can be generative in nature yet are related to the cost of providing the service.
