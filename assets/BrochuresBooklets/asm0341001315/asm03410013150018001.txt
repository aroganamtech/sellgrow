Children Like to Fly
Air transportation is for everybody. The airlines now have tourist services at low costs which make it possible for anyone to use airplanes if he wishes.
Children have traveled in airplanes with their parents from the early days of airlines. An employee of Pan American World Airways who was transferred three times within a year, took his baby daughter along with him each time. Before she was a year old, this little girl had flown 30,000 miles.
The children in this picture are playing a racing game about air travel around the world. They are just as comfortable as if they were at home.
The airlines like to have everyone fly, no matter how young or old he may be.
THINGS TO DO by yourself with your parents, or friends
1.	Make an airplane game board on cardboard or a large sheet of wrapping
paper. Using the map in front of this book, copy names of the cities on the cardboard or paper, numbering each of them in order. Using small plastic airplanes for counters and dice, have a race “around the world”.
2.	Put a jig-saw puzzle of the world together.
3.	Make a list of all the new words you have learned in this book. Look in a dictionary for their pronunciation and meanings.
THINGS TO DO in school
1.	Plan a “quiz show”, asking some important questions about aviation and the countries you have been studying.
2.	Plan and construct an airline ticket office or counter in the classroom. Make timetables, air route maps, money, tickets and supply travel folders and real timetables from travel bureaus or airline offices to help “furnish” the ticket office. Choose ticket agents and passengers to sell and purchase tickets and plan air trips to different countries.
3.	Have your classmates, teachers, and friends tell about airplane trips they have made.
