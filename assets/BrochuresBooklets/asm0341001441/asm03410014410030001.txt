Airborne radar was another project. During the year the company ordered enough to equip its entire fleet of DC-6B Clippers, as used in Alaska, and the 33 new Douglas DC-7C Clippers, many of which were scheduled for use in the Pacific in 1936.
The airline drew on Pacific-Alaska division personnel during 1955 for a program in Pakistan, to train Pakistanis in modern techniques of running an international airline. Captain Henry C. Kristofferson, of the division, was chosen to head the 24-man PAA team for this three-year project.
1955 also marked an innovation on PAA's Pacific flights. A group of 20 Nisei stewardesses (American girls of Japanese ancestry) was hired to serve aboard the airline's Tokyo flights, where their fluency in Japanese could serve non-English-speaking passengers.
In 1955, PAA's Pacific-Alaska Division was employing a total of 4,003 employees with an annual payroll of $21,725,000.
Such was the development from the days of 1935 when a handful of dedicated employees and several aircraft launched the world's first commercial air service over a major ocean.
The Division could look back with pride over two decades of Pacific flight, from the pioneering period when it operated two flight frequencies per month between Alameda and the Orient in 120-mph flying boats to the modern flight age with 300-mph land planes operating 30 flight frequencies weekly from three West Coast gateways to virtually every port of call in the Pacific basin and thence around the world.
