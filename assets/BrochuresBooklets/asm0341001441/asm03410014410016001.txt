On the 10th of February, a Pan American DC-4 Clipper departed from San Francisco and arrived in Manila 47 hours later. The new flight schedule shaved 26 hours from previous travel time between the United States and the Orient. The "cannonball” flights, set up on a thrice-weekly schedule, eliminated overnight stops. Leaving San Francisco at 10:00 p.m., the Clippers landed at Honolulu the next morning and took off again at noon after taking on fuel and a new crew. Midway Island, formerly an overnight stop, was by-passed except as a "flag stop" and the Clippers flew directly to Wake for a one-hour refueling and crew change stop. The next stop was Guam where crews were again changed and more fuel taken aboard.
Direct Clipper service between the United States and Shanghai, bringing China's metropolis within approximately 50 hours travel time of San Francisco, was inaugurated June 3, 1947, reopening the air route pioneered 10 years earlier.
