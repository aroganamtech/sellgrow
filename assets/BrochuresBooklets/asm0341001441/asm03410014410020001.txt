Boeing B-314, famous flying-boat Clipper used from 1940 through 1946, that made Pan American’s over-ocean flying comfort known throughout the world.
By the beginning of 1950, Honolulu had become an aerial "Times Square" of the Pacific, with fast Clippers criss-crossing the Pacific East, West and South.
It was interesting to recall that 15 years before, there was no such thing as scheduled flying to Hawaii or to the Orient. Only daring flights by individual pilots in special planes had crossed the Pacific and several of those had ended tragically.
In the early thirties, even, concrete plans for such a venture of flying the long Pacific miles existed only in the mind of one man - Juan T. Trippe, President of Pan American Airways. The brain that had foreseen a brilliant future for international flying as early as the mid-twenties, had envisioned today's scheduled flights over the Pacific before airplanes that could cross the vast spaces were on engineers' drawing boards.
By 1950 Mr. Trippe's words at the Board meeting of 1934, "We are getting ready to fly the Pacific", had been translated into 300-mile-an-hour Strato Clippers, flying the ocean from the West Coast to Hawaii and Hawaii to the Orient in times that only "dreamers" like Juan Trippe foresaw.
