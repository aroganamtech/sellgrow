In April, 1950, the Pacific-Alaska Division announced that the historic island of Midway had been dropped as a stopping point on its Pacific routes. Modern land* based airplanes had made Midway unessential as a stepping stone. This dot on the ocean * just 850 acres in all * has had a history out of all proportion to its size. Pan American started using Midway as a stopping point in 1935, and continued until April 15, 1950.
All other flight matters took a back seat when war broke out in Korea at the middle of 1950. The Pacific-Alaska Division was suddenly thrown into a furore of activity to help the military air lift to Korea, run by the Military Air Transport Service of the U.S. Air Force.
In dramatic fashion the airline proved the statement by its President a few months before that "America's merchant air fleet is a vital second line of national defense."
Within four days of June 25 when the tanks and armed columns of the North Korean forces slashed across the 38th parallel, Pan American had organized and put into operation, under contract to MATS, a trans-Pacific airlift to rush key military men and vital supplies across the Pacific to Japan, where they were flown on to Korea.
