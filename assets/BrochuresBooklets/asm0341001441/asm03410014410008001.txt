The S-42 was delivered to Pan American Airways in December 1934. Tests had revealed that by stripping its passenger accommodations and devoting its entire useful load to fuel, its range could be extended to about 2540 miles and its endurance to about 18.5 hours. It was never expected that this plane would be capable of passenger, mail and express service across the California-Honolulu sector of the Pacific route. Its task was to be the preliminary surveys to provide the data necessary for the operation of the M-130 aircraft in passenger service. Since the Martin factory had indicated that delivery of the M-130s would be delayed, Pan American Airways returned the S-42 to the Sikorsky plant for modification. Endurance was increased to 21.5 hours and range just under 3000 statute miles. The modified aircraft was termed the S-42B.
With the modified S-42B, the following surveys were completed: On April 16,
1935 it departed from Alameda for Honolulu and returned April 23, 1935. On June 12, 1935, it departed from Alameda and completed the survey of the Honolulu-Midway sector, returning to San Francisco on June 21, 1935. On August 9, 1935, it departed from Alameda and completed the Midway-Wake survey returning to San Francisco on August 28, 1935. On October 5, 1935, it departed from San Francisco for the Wake-Guam sector, returning to San Francisco on October 24, 1935.
November 11, 1935, the first M-130, NC14716, was received by the Pacific Division.
It was christened the "China Clipper” and on November 22, 1935, departed on the first airmail flight across the Pacific. Governor Frank L. Merriam of California had proclaimed the day officially as "Pan American Airways Day." Postmaster General James A. Farley came from Washington, D.C. to witness what he characterized as "the beginning of the greatest and most significant achievement in the marvelous and fascinating development of air transportation”.
Code signals were flashed from Pan American Airways' bases at Honolulu, Midway, Wake, Guam and Manila stating that all was in readiness.
