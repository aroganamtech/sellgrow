With the interior Alaskan operations integrated, Pan American moved into the next stage of its development program - an extension to southeastern Alaska,
After a series of survey flights, a route from Fairbanks to Juneau via Whitehorse, Yukon Territory, was selected and authorization was obtained from the Canadian government for landing at Whitehorse. Meanwhile, in 1934 PAA had purchased the assets of Alaska Southern Airways, which had been operating in the southern part of the territory, with occasional charter flights to the U.S., thus acquiring three small aircraft, miscellaneous ground facilities and, most important, considerable operating experience along the coastal area.
A highlight of PAA's operations came on April 1, 1935, with the inauguration of regular service between Juneau and Fairbanks with Lockheed Electras - fast, twin-engine monoplanes which were considered among the most advanced aircraft of their day. The following year the company replaced the old Fairchild 71s with Fairchild Pilgrims 100-As which were equipped for Arctic operations over the mail routes in the interior.

Communications and meteorological services. In 1934 and 1935, PAA undertook to construct and operate the first complete and integrated radio network in Alaska suitable for point to point and plane to ground communications and for weather reporting. Eleven radio stations, in addition to one already operating at Fairbanks, were established. All were equipped with meteorological facilities and personnel were trained to make accurate weather observations. These services were made available to the Army and Navy.
