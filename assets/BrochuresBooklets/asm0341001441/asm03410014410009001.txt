On December 6, the Clipper was back at Alameda and the first round-trip flight over the Pacific was completed in her log book. Outward-bound from San Francisco the Clipper carried 110,865 letters weighing 1,837 pounds. Returning from Manila her mail load consisted of 98,480 letters weighing 1,789 pounds.
Passenger service between San Francisco, Honolulu and Manila was inaugurated on October 21, 1936.
Three months previously Pan American Airways had contracted for the delivery of six Boeing B-314 four-engined flying boats, the next in the line of flying Clippers to pioneer the air routes of the Pacific
The company turned its attention to the development of a South Pacific route to Australia and New Zealand via Kingman Reef and Pago Pago. Landing rights at Auckland were granted by the New Zealand government November, 1935, with service to begin by December 31, 1936. Plans were being pushed to put the new service into operation, but the lack of adequate facilities along the route made it necessary for PAA to apply for an extension of the inaugural date.
