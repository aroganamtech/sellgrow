Australia is more than worth coming so far to see. And although you may be surprised to find very sizeable modern cities such as Sydney and Melbourne, the main show is out-of-doors.
Australia, for instance, is famous for bathing and surf beaches, and for swank beach resorts. Australians, among the most likeable people on earth, are great enthusiasts for all sports — swimming, sailing, golf, tennis, horse-racing, and team games. You’ll join in on all this as a welcome visitor, and have the time of your life. Field sportsmen will find fine shooting and fresh-water angling — and for followers of the deep, there’s really exciting salt-water fishing.
But you don’t have to be a sportsman to have a good time. There are astounding things to see. You can be an explorer and see with your own eyes the painted “black-fellow” bushmen, so expert with the boomerang. You
can pick up blood-red stones on a one-time ocean floor at Gibber Plains, gasp at great round rocks, of many tons, known as the “Devil’s Marbles”.
Expect other big things of nature, too. You’ll sniff thousands of flowers with names and beauty that are new. There’s everything from lush jungle growth to pines. In Canberra there’s an avenue flanked by 20,000 rose bushes. In the fauna division, Australia is most amazing. Kangaroos, with progeny in their pockets, leaping twenty feet; the ridiculous duck-billed platypus and the fast-running emu whom you’ve met before only in crosswords; lizards that bark, and fish that can live out of water. The chap you’ll love is the Koala Bear — for all the world like a child’s toy come to life. But don’t go to Koala Park near Sydney before 2:30 in the afternoon. That’s when the bears wake up, and that’s when the park opens.
