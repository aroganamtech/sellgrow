COCKTAILS
The cocktail hour in Paris starts around 6:30 in the evening and follows the tea hour by only fifteen minutes. At this time the bars and cafés start filling with French people and tourists, the former ordering apéritifs and the latter whisky sours and dry Martinis. French gin is awful, and if you ever expect to get a good Martini you should specify an English or American brand. French bartenders usually use more vermouth than Americans prefer, so you must insist on an extra dry Martini if you want it to come out right. Some of the better bars in town serving American drinks are at the Hotels Crillon, Meurice, Ritz, Prince de Galles, Claridge, Plaza-Athénée, Lotti and California.
The Crillon Bar, located across the street from the American Embassy, is a favorite of the diplomatic crowd. It serves one of the best Tom Collins in Paris, and most of the mixed drinks are made in American style.
The Ritz Bar, one of the oldest and most famous drinking institutions in France, is a hangout for outer fringes of royalty
