Blood-soaked butchers carry quarters of beef into the stalls and broad-shouldered porters backs while commissionnaires check each shipment, with care. Greenhouse growers arrange their carnations, tulips and lilacs on the long benches and farmers pile mountains of vegetables neatly in the streets.
The people that work in Les Halles have strong arms, strong voices, and stronger appetites. It was they who popularized eating onion soup early in the morning and it is to them that the market owes its reputation for good restaurants.
Four that we would like to mention here are : Au Pied de Cochon (The Pig’s Foot), Aux Mareyeurs (At the Fishmongers), Au Pere Tranquille (freely translated as the Quiet Old Man) and Au Chien qui Fume (The Smoking Dog.)
Au Pied de Cochon, at & Rue Coquilliere, is famous for its onion soup and grilled pig’s feet. The upstairs restaurant is more expensive and the downstairs brasserie more fun.
You will find Aux Marayeurs, at 98 Rue Rambuteau. Mme Rochet has been overseeing the making of the onion soup for twenty years. The restaurant also likes to brag about the size of its escargots (snails) and lobsters.
