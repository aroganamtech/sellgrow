Au Pere Tranquille has rested on the corner of 16 Rue Pierre-Lescot for over a century. It used to be as famous for its cabaret as it was for its food, but now the nightclub is closed and you will have to eat downstairs.
The oldest and most picturesque of the onion soup stops is Au Chien qui Fume, at 33 Rue du Pont-Neuf, which was founded in the 1790’s. There are several pictures of dogs with pipes and cigars in their mouths on the walls. The upstairs part of the restaurant is a cabaret with a two-man orchestra. Anyone who wishes to sober up fast should go there to listen to the music. One of the men plays an accordion with his hands and beats a drum with a foot pedal. His partner strums a banjo and passes the hat after four or five numbers. At any other hour this would be difficult to take, but four o’clock in the morning is a tolerant time and the music seems to fit the atmosphere of the place.
After your onion soup you can wander among the stalls and purchase flowers at fantastically low prices, and observe the inside workings of “the belly of Paris.”
