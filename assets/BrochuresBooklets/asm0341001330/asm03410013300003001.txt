At ten o’clock on the morning of March 17, 1853, the Clipper ship SOVEREIGN OF THE SEAS was knifing her way through enormous, combing waves in the South Pacific. And, something was happening that had never happened in the world before. All through the night the ship had roared along under her royals, defying rain squalls which a few years earlier would have worried any skipper afloat, filled him with fear that a sudden gust would drive his ship beneath a wild mountain of water like a sounding whale. But, this captain, Captain Lauchlan McKay, had a new kind of sailing craft under his heels, a ship that rode the waves more steadily and remained drier the faster she drove. He had command of a Clipper.
At ten, and during the day, speed soundings were taken, and each time the mate called out, “Nineteen knots again, sir!” The ship was tearing along hour after hour at nineteen nautical miles per hour, and a miracle was in the making. As far as anyone on board knew, passengers and crew alike, nobody had ever sailed at that speed before. And, when six bells had been struck the miracle was made. For the first time in the history of the world, a ship had sailed more than 400 sea miles in 24 hours.
America and its people were elated. And with good reason. Every sea-going country was locked in a struggle for world trade, and for a young nation, is was a struggle almost for survival. Boldly her shipbuilders had junked most of the accumulated ideas of nations that had been building ships for thousands of years, and they had produced the Clipper. In less than ten years, these Yankee shipbuilders had proven their new ideas right by building scores of ships that were better, went faster, and carried more trade, blazed more briney trails than the world had dreamed of before.
