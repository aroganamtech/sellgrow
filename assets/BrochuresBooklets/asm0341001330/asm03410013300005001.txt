Clippers had speed! Whipping across the Atlantic in 14 days, they were a far cry from the tiny ships of Columbus that had consumed three months in the crossing. From New York to San Francisco, around Cape Horn, is a long and perilous voyage, but the fastest Clippers made it in 89 days, a record never beaten by any other type of sailing vessel since. The Clippers coursed the seas like nimble hares. Who could help but stand in awe of the Clippers whose masts stood three feet thick at the deck, with a great rake aft that made them appear to be in swift flight across the waves even when only riding calmly at anchor.
Undoubtedly the fastest and most famous sailing ship was the Clipper FLYING CLOUD. Designed by Donald McKay and built in East Boston in 1851, she was most appropriately named. 235 feet overall, she carried a great expanse of canvas that made her into a living, racing creature, truly a white winged wonder of the sea. She was sold to her first owners while still on the stocks, and for a price reported to be twice that of her entire cost. So proud were her owners of her maiden voyage speed record (89 days from New York to San Francisco) that they published her log on silk with letters of gold.
There were Clippers and Clippers, and still more Clippers, as the living legend caught the imagination of shipbuilders. Down the greased shipways came the RAINBOW, the WEST WIND, the AMERICAN EAGLE, and the RAVEN. They came fast. The keel of the JOHN BERTRAM was laid in October, 1850, and in just 91 days she was anchored in Boston, fully laden with cargo and white sails set for San Francisco.
