From the tiny mail-carrying aircraft of 1927 to the great flashing Jet Clippers of Pan American’s fleet today has been a long haul, but as romantic a history as can be told. Just like Captain Samuels of the DREADNOUGHT, Pan American’s early pilots had their weather difficulties, too. Due to the smallness of the lightly powered aircraft they flew in the early days, they sometimes found a surprise or two even in the upper reaches of the sky.
Preserved for us is the tale of one veteran Pan American pilot, who flew 80 miles an hour over the Gulf of Mexico. Just out of Key West the plane ran into a tropical hurricane with headwinds of 90 miles. Unable to proceed, the pilot was about to give himself up for lost when he found he had been blown right back over the airport he had left an hour before, and made a safe landing.
Juan Terry Trippe pioneered the multiple engine aircraft of the 1930’s and 1940’s, and the long-range ocean-spanning Flying Clippers of that era. Flying Clippers! One of the most famous names in aviation, given to the flying boats that were at home in the sky and landing in the water. The work they did, save that they moved through the air instead of on the sea, resembled that of the graceful, tall-masted sailing vessels of 100 years ago. Many of the flight paths of the original Flying Clippers were based on navigational clues that originated in the Old Clipper logbooks.
