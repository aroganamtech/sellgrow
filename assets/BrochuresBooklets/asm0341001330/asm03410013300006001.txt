The legend grew with such Clippers as the DREADNOUGHT, named after the famous vessel in Admiral Nelson’s fleet. Under the command of Captain Samuels, she earned the nickname “Wild Boat
of the Atlantic.” On one historic voyage, a huge sea put the captain out of action. The wheel was ignored and the ship was soon helpless. A jury rudder failed and it was impossible to put the ship about. But, the crew managed to get her main and mizzen yards braced, and she slowly gathered sternway and sailed backwards in a howling gale for 280 miles!
Just how or when the famous name “Clipper” was coined isn’t really clear. Some say it was because they used to course along at a good clip, for speed was their chief claim to fame. Then, again, they clipped days off long standing speed records. Designed and built in response to the need for more and speedier trade between the United States and foreign lands, the Clippers transported fortune-seekers by the thousands when the California and Australia gold rushes touched off a huge immigration to both countries.
