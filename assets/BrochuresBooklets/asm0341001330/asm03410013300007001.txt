Today, in the middle of another century, the sounds which recapture for us the spirit of such achievement are the high-pitched whine of the turbine engine, the steady hum of electronic gear, and, on occasion, the incessant patter of rain lashing against an aluminum hull.
On October 28,1927, a FOKKERF-7, piloted by Captain Hugh Wells, powered by three Wright 220-horse-power engines, took off on an historic 90 mile flight from Key West,
Florida to Havana, Cuba.
Again, something was happening that had never happened in the world before. A great international airline was being born, an airline that would ensure America’s having its rightful place in world air transportation, an airline that would immortalize the name “Clipper” for the future, with a fleet that would be as famed throughout the world as were those memorable, sleek sovereigns of the sea.
It would become known as PAN AMERICAN AIRWAYS.
Pan American started its present global flight operations with a short 90-mile flight to Havana, Cuba, in 1927, and then extended its airways to Central and South America.
