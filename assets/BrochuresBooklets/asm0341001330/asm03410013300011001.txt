What about the future?
In the years to come, boarding Pan American passengers will continue to see the name of their aircraft at the entrance, and many will be the name of one of those graceful Clippers of the sea, but within a decade it will read a little differently: SUPERSONIC CLIPPER FLYING CLOUD . . . SUPERSONIC CLIPPER RAINBOW . . . SUPERSONIC CLIPPER CATHAY.
Pan American is the first U. S. airline scheduled to receive delivery of the CONCORDE, a supersonic transport now being built jointly by England and France. And, Pan American has requested delivery schedules on 15 of the larger, faster U. S. version now in the planning stage, as an initial addition to the Jet Clipper fleet.
Little more than a century since the sleek Clippers were astonishing the world with their nautical speed records, Pan Am passengers will be traveling in aircraft that measures speed not in knots or in miles but in Mach numbers — relationship to speed of sound. So smooth, so quiet, so comfortable, the Supersonic passenger will find it hard to believe his Clipper has flown him from New York to London in less than three hours.
It will be a great new fleet — Pan American’s Supersonic Clipper Fleet — with a magnificent tradition.
