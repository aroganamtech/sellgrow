Just as the ports of China, Australia and Africa knew the Clipper ships, so did Lisbon and Southhampton come to know the giant flying boats of Pan American. And when the Flying Clippers put down their bulbous noses, with the Stars and Stripes painted broadly over them, in far off Manila, Hong Kong and Auckland, over 8,000 miles from their homes, there were Americans long absent from their homeland who wept at the sight.
The established Clipper airlanes were invaluable when a smoldering World War II broke into flame. It was the DIXIE CLIPPER with Captains Vinal and Cone that flew President Roosevelt to Africa for one of the most momentous conferences of the war. Great accomplishments were part of the Clipper wartime story. Such is the now legendary round-the-world flight of the PACIFIC CLIPPER captained by Robert Ford. The aircraft with its ten man crew left San Francisco on December 2, 1941, and its destination was Auckland, New Zealand. Normal schedule called for the plane to return to San Francisco in two weeks, but December 7th and Pearl Harbor intervened. It was a year and five months before the PACIFIC CLIPPER returned to base, and in that time she completed an history-making circle of the world.
“Clipper” is a trade-name of Pan American Airways, and has been ever since the AMERICAN CLIPPER was launched and christened in Washington in 1931. She was named by Mr. Trippe himself.
