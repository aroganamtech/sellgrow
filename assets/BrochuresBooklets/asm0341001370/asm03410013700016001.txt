Etiquette for small fry
If your child is walking and the least bit inquisitive,
he’ll be very tempted to run up and down the aisles. Because there is always a danger of his stumbling and falling it is not permissible for him to have free run of the aisles. As a matter of fact, if he does undertake a 10-yard dash, the stewardess will probably caution you against this form of entertainment. Naturally, a child will become restless. In this case it is perfectly proper to take him for a walk the length of the aircraft. After meal service and after the stewardesses have finished tidying up the galley (kitchen) you may want to visit this part of the aircraft. There will be standing room there and your child may enjoy “talking” with the stewardesses, receiving a glass of water or soft drink.
The stewardess will be happy to give your child a coloring book, crayons and Walt Disney comic books. An older child will have a natural curiosity about the trip, the Jet Clipper, and where he is going. Make it a point to buy a child’s book about the country you will visit or at least a story about that country. Also encourage him to keep a “log” of his trip. He may even be encouraged to write a letter to his friends or schoolmates about his Jet Clipper flight. This can be done “in-flight” and the stewardess will supply him with Pan Am stationery.
