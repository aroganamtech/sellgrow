With the Pacific and Atlantic conquered, the dream of ’Round the World service, connecting Pan Am’s eastern and western routes, became closer to being a reality.
On June 17, 1947, Captain Hugh H. Gordon, piloting a Pan Am Lockheed “Constellation,” inaugurated the first ’Round-the-World civil air service from LaGuardia Airport, New York.
Pan Am continued to pioneer. In 1955, Pan Am opened the way into the commercial jet age by placing the first order for American-built jet airliners. On October 26, 1958, Captain Samuel H. Miller guided his Boeing 707 Jet Clipper down the runway at IdlewiId Airport, and Pan American inaugurated American flag jet servicewith a flight from New York to Paris. Flying at speeds close to 600 miles per hour,the newJet Clippers shrunk the world to half its size...bringing continents and people closer together.
Jet travel has changed the face of the world. As stated by Juan Trippe, “Mass travel by air-made possible by the Jet Age—may prove to be more significant to world destiny than the atom bomb. For there can be no atom bomb more powerful than the air tourist, charged with curiosity, enthusiasm and good will, who can roam the four corners of the world, meeting in friendship and understanding the people of other nations and races.”
Pan Am’s new 747 will dwarf the present 707 and yet be able to take off and land on present day runways. It will be quieter and more comfortable for its 362 passengers.
