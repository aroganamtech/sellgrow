THE PAN AM STORY
In 1927, a 28 year old Yale graduate named Juan T. Trippe, equipped with a love of flying and five years of aviation experience in a new and growing industry, founded an airline—Pan American Airways. Pan Am®—now the world’s most experienced airline—consisted of just two new Fokker F-7 three-engined airplanes and 24 loyal employees. On October 28, 1927, Pan Am Flight No. 1 taxied down a runway in Key West, Florida, bound for Havana, Cuba...a 90 mile hop, in an aircraft that had a maximum speed of 90 miles per hour. This was the first United States International Air Service in scheduled operation.
Today, giant Pan Am Jet Clippers®, able to fly at speeds up to 600 miles an hour, cruise along a route network of more than 81,000 miles ...linking 119 cities in 81 lands on all six continents. But it was the Caribbean that served as a laboratory for Pan Am’s present trans-Atlantic, trans-Pacific, and ’round-the-world Jet Clipper network.
Aviation’s dynamic growth and today’s jet age aircraft make the Caribbean look small on the world globe...yet it presented to Pan Am’s pioneers all the problems that an aspiring world air operator could meet.
