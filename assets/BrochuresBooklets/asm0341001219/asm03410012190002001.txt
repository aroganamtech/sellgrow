Leave your hotel in the morning by limousine. Drive through downtown Honolulu, passing the Arts, Civic and Territorial buildings and the statue of King Kamehameha. Beyond the city, through beautiful tropical country, the highway enters Nuuanu Valley to famous Nuuanu Pali, an observation point on a high cliff, with a breath-taking view. KOKO HEAD —KAILUA TOUR
Leave your hotel for a fascinating half-day drive by automobile, circling the eastern end of Oahu Island. Starting out through the old volcanic lava flows of Koko Crater, the first stop is at famous Diamond Head, the mountainous point of land you have so often seen in pictures. Close by is the lighthouse used as the finish line for Trans-Pacific Yacht. Races. From here, the drive continues through line residential districts to Koko Head and delightful Hanauma Bay. Here lava flows reach into the ocean. You’ll also see the “Blow Hole”, a fast-repeating Old Faithful that, in this case, shoots great geysers of salt water with each incoming wave. Next stop is Makapuu Light, the world’s most powerful. There's a sporting spectacle on the beach at the foot of this lighthouse. Expert swimmers gather here to ride the surf — planing with their bodies and without benefit of surfboards. Return trip to Honolulu and Waikiki is made over the scenically sensational Nuuanu Pali Road. On the way, you drive t hrough Kailua and Lanikai, thriving suburbs of Honolulu, once a cocoanut plantation. Here, too, are excellent swimming beaches and attractive beach homes.

MOUNT TANTALUS DRIVE
COST ACCORDING TO TYPE OF HOTEL ACCOMMODATIONS
This half-day scenic drive starts from your hotel and climbs the slopes of tropical Mount Tantalus. From this height you will get, a never-to-be-forgotten view of Honolulu, from Pearl Harbor to Diamond Head. You’ll be treated to the rare and delicious Macadamia nuts which grow on this mountainside. A stop is made at the Punchbowl — not a roadside inn, but, a 500-foot high extinct volcano crater that rises sharply almost in downtown Honolulu. You will see the exotic night-blooming cereus growing on the walls of the Punahou School. A stop is made at the University of Hawaii, The Honolulu Academy of Arts with its exhibits of Polynesian and Oriental cultures, and Waikiki Aquarium where you’ll find the little fish with the big name.
