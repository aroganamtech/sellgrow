Pursers, Stewards
Now that dinner is finished, let’s go to the lounge and sit there for a while. Miss Stanley will join us, and tell us how it feels to be a Stewardess . . .
Oh, Hello, Miss Stanley. Tell us, won’t you, what is the difference between a purser and a stewardess?
MISS STANLEY:
“Well, the purser is the steward or stewardess in charge.
“On some flights, we have six stewards or stewardesses in a transatlantic flight. On others, we have four, one to serve from each of the four galley units. It depends on whether or not the flight is The President Special, President, Rainbow or Thrift, or a combination of these services.”
“We are the flight service crew, and One of us is in charge. The senior steward, or stewardess, has that duty and is called the purser.

And the purser has to keep the records, too. We have a passenger manifest showing who is aboard, and we have a chart showing where everyone is sitting. We see that you get a form to fill out for the Immigration people, that’s your landing card, and another to fill out for your customs declaration, and we help you with these forms if you want us to. We have to file reports on what goes on during the flight. . .
“How did I get to be a stewardess?
“First I went to college and graduated. That’s not required, but it is required that we have either tivo years of college, or else two years of experience in business. We have to be twenty-one. And we have to have a foreign language — that’s a must, because we have so many passengers who do not speak English.
