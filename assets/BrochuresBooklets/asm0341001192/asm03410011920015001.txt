Nearly all of Britain’s 1,200 golf courses, which are among the world’s best, are open to non-member visitors; take an open letter of introduction from your club at home. The season is March to November, and green fees are very low. In Scotland, home of golf, are such revered courses as the Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St. Andrews, Fife; the Troon, Turnberry and Prestwick in Ayrshire; Gleneagles and Blairgowrie, Perthshire; Carnoustie, Angus; Gullane, North Berwick and Muirfield in East Lothian.
Tennis, usually played on grass courts, is popular all over the country.
Streams such as the Itchem, Frome and Axe have made English trout fishing famous. Dry-fly fishing here ranks with the best in the world. The moor country in western England offers the wet-fly fisherman superb casting for salmon and trout. Fishing is also marvelous all over Scotland, but most salmon rivers in the Highlands and on the east coast are privately owned. For brown trout fishing there are innumerable hotels which own fishing streams for their guests. For full free information see Scotland for Fishing, published by the Scottish Tourist Board.
England has good hunting for deer, hare and other game, pheasant, partridge, wild goose and duck. Game licenses, for a shotgun and hunting permit, are $2.40 for 2 weeks, $4.80 for 6 months, $7.20 for a year. Most of the shooting in Scotland is done on private preserves.
