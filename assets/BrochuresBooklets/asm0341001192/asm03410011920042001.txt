Lincoln, Lincolnshire (C-4) pop. 77,200 . . . The Lincoln Cathedral, which can be seen for miles, is one of Europe’s important Gothic buildings, minutely decorated with carved wood and stone creatures, real and imaginary, that add unexpected humor to the over-all majesty. The building was erected largely between 1186 and 1761. About 6 miles to the southwest is stately Dodington Hall. Eastgate (M), White HartQA).
Liverpool, Lancashire (B-3) pop. 729,140 . . .The newest landmark in this seaport city is the Roman Catholic Cathedral of Christ the King. Ultramodern in every detail, with a circular nave for mass-in-the-round, the cathedral was built between 1960 and 1967. At the other end of Hope Street is the neo-Gothic Anglican Cathedral, the largest in England, begun in 1904 and the scheduled date for completion is not until 1975. Other Liverpool attractions are the Philharmonic Hall, Walker’s Art Gallery and, of course, the Cavern Club on Mathew Street, where it all began for The Beatles. The Grand National Steeplechase, one of the world’s roughest races, is held at Aintree every spring. Adelphi (M), Exchange (M), Shaftesbury (M), Stork (M).
