Hogarth’s House, Hogarth Lane, Chiswick, W.4.
Horse Guards Parade, Whitehall, S.W.l. The Changing of the Guard delights photographers every weekday morning at 11; at 10 on Sundays.
Houses of Parliament, Westminster, W.C.l. Meticulously lovely Gothic styling presides over Britain’s law-makers. When the House of Commons is in session, Americans can get tickets from the American Embassy to attend. No tickets needed for House of Lords; just queue up at St. Stephen’s Entrance on afternoons that debates are scheduled.
Hyde Park. 341 acres in the heart of London. Speakers’ Corner, where any crackpot can mount a soapbox and harangue anyone willing to listen, is near the triple-arched entrance at Knightsbridge and Wellington Place. The roadway here skirts the eastern edge of the park to Marble Arch (John Nash, 1827).
Inns of the Court, Strand, W.C.2. In Britain, the only four colleges which can “call lawyers to the bar” are Lincoln’s Inn, Gray’s Inn, the Middle and the Inner Temple. The great hall of Middle Temple, opened by Elizabeth I in 1572, is the finest example of Elizabethan design in Britain.
Dr. Johnson’s House, 17 Gough St., E.C.4.
Lambeth Palace, Lambeth Palace Rd., S.E.l. London residence of the Archbishops of Canterbury.
Old Bailey, Newgate St., E.C.4. Where all the “best” criminals stand trial.
Old Curiosity Shop, Portsmouth St., Kingsway, W.C.2.
