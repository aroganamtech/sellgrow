North of town is the palatial Dodington House designed by James Watt in Regency style, with gardens laid out in 1764 by Capability Brown. The famous Longleat House, home of the “Mad” Marquess of Bath (28 miles south of the city) stands in a great park full of African lions, chimps and other unlikely beasts. Top Bath hotels: Cliffe (M, 3V2 miles out), Francis (M), Lansdown Grove (M), Pratt’s (B), Royal York (B).
Birmingham, Warwickshire (C-4) pop. 1,115,630 . . . Midlands home of leading industrialists, inventors and scientists. Great orchestras and famous artists often perform in the Town Hall, and there is an outstanding display of pre-Raphaelite paintings and English water colors, as well as a huge Shakespeare collection, in the city’s Museum and Art Gallery. Royal Angus (L), Albany (M), Grand (M), Midland (M), Excelsior (M; at the airport).
Blackpool, Lancashire (B-3) pop. 150,000 . . . The city has a superb sandy beach, 7 miles of promenade, golf courses, swimming pools, a huge ballroom, a vast amusement park, and spectacular illuminations on autumn evenings. Cliffs (M), Clifton (M), Imperial (M), Savoy (M).
Bournemouth, Hampshire (C-5) pop. 154,300 . . . A resort city with everything to attract the vacationer, including concerts by the renowned Bournemouth Symphony Orchestra. Carlton (L), Royal Bath (L), Branksome Towers (M), East Cliff Court (M), Highcliff (M), Marsham Court (M), Palace Court (M).
