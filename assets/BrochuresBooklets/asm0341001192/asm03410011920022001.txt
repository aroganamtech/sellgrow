tours of the city. Some famous places:
Albert Memorial, Kensington Gardens, S.E.ll. Dedicated to Victoria’s Prince Consort (d. 1861).
Bank of England, Threadneedle St., E.C.2. founded in 1694; windowless for security.
Banqueting House, Whitehall, S.W.l. By Inigo Jones, completed in 1622. The magnificent ceiling was painted by Peter Paul Rubens in 1634.
Billingsgate Market, E.C.3. London’s major fish market for hundreds of years; Sir Christopher Wren’s 202-foot memorial to the Great Fire of 1666.
Buckingham Palace, The Mall, S.W.l. London home of the Royal Family. Changing of the Guard ceremony is at 11:30 daily in summer.
Carlton House Terrace, St. James’s Park, S.W.l. Beautiful buildings designed by John Nash in 1831.
Thomas Carlyle’s House, 24 Cheyne Row, Chelsea, S.W.3.
Chelsea Hospital, Dovehouse St., S.W.3. One of Sir Christopher Wren’s most charming buildings; the home of old soldiers since the days of Charles II.
Cleopatra’s Needle, Embankment, W.C.2. First erected at Heliopolis, Egypt c. 1450 b.c.
Covent Garden, W.C.2. Enormous wholesale fruit, vegetable and flower market; at its liveliest 6-8 a.m., except Saturday.
Charles Dickens House, 48 Doughty St., W.C.l.
10 Downing St., Whitehall, S.W.l. Official residence of Britain’s Prime Ministers for over 200 years.
George Inn, Borough High St., Southwark, S.E.l. Standing since 1676, this is London’s last galleried inn, its low timbered rooms the haunt of Dickens, Dr. Johnson and similar notables.
