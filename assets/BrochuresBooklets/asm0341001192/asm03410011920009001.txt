Entertainment: So far as Americans are concerned, Britain is the Entertainment Capital of Europe because everything’s in English and admission prices are far lower than we’re accustomed to at home. Your travel agent can book you a seat in advance if you know which plays, concerts, festivals or other events you want to attend, but it is very easy to book through one of the many booking agencies in Britain, often in your hotel. What’s On (weekly), the Evening Standard daily newspaper or This Month in London (7!/ip, 180) will tell you everything that’s going on in the capital while you’re there. A copy of In Britain, which describes coming events, will help you do some longer-range planning before you leave home; it’s available from the British Tourist Authority (see Information Centers below) and lists special events from sheep dog trials in Scotland and folk festivals in Wales to Royal Philharmonic concerts in London. Food and Drinks: Breakfast is a monumental triumph in Britain and deserves whole-hearted participation. It costs extra in expensive hotels, but is included in the rates of more modest ones. Tea, about 4 to 5 in the afternoon, is another big event of the day. British baked goods are exceptionally tasty and varied, and a good tea will even tide you over until supper after the theater. For an exhaustive coverage of the best places to eat in Britain, buy Egon Ronay’s Guide to Hotels and Restaurants, or Raymond Postgate’s Good Food Guide. A “Fully Licensed” sign on a restaurant means it can serve drinks, and applies even to dining rooms in some of the big department stores.
