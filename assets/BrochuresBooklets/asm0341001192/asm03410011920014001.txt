Car-renting agencies (both British and American companies) abound. It is inadvisable for anyone, arriving in England for the first time, to get into a self-drive car at the airport and try to adjust, all at once, to both the horrendous London traffic and driving on the left. A car is a nuisance in London, anyway. Arrange to have one delivered when you’re ready to leave the city, or take a train to some outlying town and pick up a car there. Self-drive cars rent in summer for an average of $57 a week, including insurance and unlimited mileage (cheaper in winter). Gas (petrol) is 740 to 800 per Imperial gallon (1 Ys U.S. gallons). Motor caravans, the equivalent of U.S. camper-trailers, can also be rented fully equipped for living. Dealers can help make arrangements to take your rental car or caravan across the Channel for a tour of Europe.
London bookstores are packed with maps and guide books to just about every inch of Britain. Even tiny rural towns are minutely documented in small illustrated guides sold in their newsshops.
Sports: Horse racing goes on all year in England. Flat racing lasts from March to December, steeplechasing is best from December to March. The English Derby is run at Epsom Downs during the first week in June, a gorgeous panoply of royalty and fashion show, gypsies and carnival types. Bets for races all over the country can be placed with off-track bookmakers.
