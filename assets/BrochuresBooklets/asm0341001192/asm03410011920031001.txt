III. Touring England
Here are just a few of the hundreds of delightful towns and villages you’ll want to explore. Hotels listed are those with the most rooms with private baths. See rate codes, page 8. Letters and figures in parentheses refer to towns’ locations on cover maps.
Aldeburgh, Suffolk (D-4) pop. 3,000 .. . Popular vacation spot with golf, tennis, yachting. Picturesque 16th century Moot Hall. The composer Benjamin Britten inaugurated the Aldeburgh Festival of Music and the Arts which is held here every June in a converted 19th century malt house. Brudenell (M).
Banbury, Oxfordshire (C-4) pop. 23,100 . . . East of town is Sulgrave Manor, from which George Washington’s grandfather emigrated to America in 1657. Nearby too are Compton Wynyates, a romantic Tudor manor; Broughton Castle, with an elaborate interior porch; Canons Ashby House, with lovely gardens; Upton House, and others. Whately Hall (M).
Bath, Somerset (B-5) pop. 82,750 . . . The Romans were the first to appreciate the natural hot springs of Bath, and many of their structures are still in evidence. Early in the 18th century, the town fell under the inspired touch of Beau Nash and some of England’s greatest architects, becoming the site of some of the loveliest Georgian buildings ever created: the Royal Crescent of Ionic columned houses, the regal Assembly Rooms, the Pulteney Bridge over the Avon, designed by Robert Adam. The magnificent Abbey, with its huge windows, dates from the 16th century. The Holburne of Menstrie Museum of Art contains exquisite porcelain, silver and furnishings.
