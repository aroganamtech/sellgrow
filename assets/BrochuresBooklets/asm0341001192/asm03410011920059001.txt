Glencoe, Argyll (S of Fort William) pop. 1,130 ... A wild, eerie loneliness hangs over the mountains crowding around the glen where, in 1692, the Campbell clan slaughtered most of the resident MacDonalds. Magnificent scenery for hiking, skiing. King’s House (B).
Grantown-on-Spey, Morayshire (NE of Avie-more) pop. 1,600 . . . Long known as a trout and salmon fishing resort, Grantown now booms in winter also with skiers heading for the Cairngorm Mountains. Pony-trekking, golf, tennis, hiking. Craiglynne
(M)	, Grant Arms (M).
Greenock, Renfrewshire (near Glasgow) pop. 74,500 . . . Clydeside ship-building city and birthplace, in 1736, of inventor James Watt. Tontine (M).
Gullane, East Lothian (SW of North Berwick) pop. 1,480 . .. Three great 18-hole courses make the Muirfield a club well known to golfers. Greywalls (M).
Inverness, Inverness-shire pop. 30,270 . . . The proud little “Capital of the Highlands” lies on both sides of the River Ness that flows north from Loch Ness, of monster fame, into the Moray Firth. Inhabited since prehistoric times, Inverness was the capital of Brude, king of the Piets, whom St. Columba came to convert in about 565 a.d.
