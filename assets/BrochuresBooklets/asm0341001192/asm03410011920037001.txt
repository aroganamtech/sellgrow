Cambridge, Cambridgeshire (D-4) pop. 98,400 ... You can spend days here just admiring the superb architecture of the university. King’s College Chapel is judged by experts to be one of the finest buildings in the world. One of Cambridge’s loveliest spots is The Backs, the tree-shaded lawns and gardens sloping down to the River Cam behind the colleges of Queen’s, King’s, Clare, Trinity, St. John’s and Magdalene. Anglesey Abbey, 6 miles northeast of town, is a recently opened stately home dating from the 13th century. Garden House (M), University Anns (M), Royal Cambridge (B), Blue Boar (B).
Canterbury, Kent (D-5) pop. 32,000 . . . This is probably England’s greatest medieval town. In the great cathedral (begun in 1070) is the “Chair of Augustine,” used during the enthronement ceremonies of every Bishop of Canterbury since the 13th century. Thomas a Becket was murdered in the northwest transept by agents of Henry II in 1170. Behind the altar are the tombs of Henry IV and Queen Joan, and the tomb and accoutrements of Edward, the dashing Black Prince. See also Christ Church, with 800 shields in its vaulting, and the handsome Norman staircase (c. 1160) which leads to the hall of King’s School, oldest boys’ school in Britain. Slatters (M), Abbots Barton (B), Chaucer (B), County (B).
