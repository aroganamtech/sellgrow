Sightseeing: Certain tickets, available in the U.S. but not in Britain, enable visitors from abroad to tour the United Kingdom by train more cheaply than Britons can. Ask your travel agent, or the British Railway International offices at 350 Fifth Ave., New York City 10020, or at 510 West 6th St., Los Angeles 90014. To save money on admission charges to castles, famous homes and monuments in England and Scotland, purchase an “Open to View” ticket before you leave home. Send a check for $3.75 made out to British Rail International to the British Tourist Authority, 680 Fifth Ave., New York, N.Y. 10019.
Or after you get to London, go to the Ministry of Public Buildings and Works, Lambeth Bridge House, S.E.l, and buy a Season Ticket to the History of Britain ($5). This ticket is good for entry to 1,000 places administered by the National Trust in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The ticket is half-price for children under 15 years of age.
Touring Britain by bus, for only two or three cents a mile, is a great way to poke into every nook and cranny of the country, stopping at night in charming, inexpensive inns in tiny villages. (But you’d better phone ahead for a room; some places have beds for only 8 or 10 guests.) To go on a grander scale, take one of the many conducted bus tours offered by travel agencies in London. Consult your own travel agent first because these tours are so popular they must be booked in advance.
