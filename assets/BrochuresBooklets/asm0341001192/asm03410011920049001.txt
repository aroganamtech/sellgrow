From April to September, excursion boats go up and down the Thames between Windsor and Hampton Court, the fabulous palace begun by Cardinal Wolsey and completed by Henry VIII; it is unusual for its intricate maze gardens. In the other direction from Windsor is Stoke Poges, where Thomas Gray wrote Elegy in a Country Churchyard in 1751; and Cliveden, the former Astor mansion now taken over by Stanford University. Old House (M; designed by Wren), Castle (B), Ye Harte and Garter (B).
Worcester, Worcestershire (NW of Cheltenham, B-4) pop. 67,580 . . . An aged city known for glovemaking, porcelain, Worcestershire Sauce and many fine old half-timbered houses. Giffard (M).
York, Yorkshire (C-3) pop. 105,230 . . . York rivals Canterbury as a treasury of medieval architecture. The city walls, topped by a footpath, were built during the reign of Edward III (1327-77), and nightly curfew has rung from St. Michael’s Church since 1066. York Minster, distinguished by its particularly fine glass and graceful proportions, was completed in 1474. Among many other interesting buildings are the 14th century Hall of the Merchant Adventurers and the Castle Folk Museum. About 14 miles northeast of town is Castle Howard, a baroque masterpiece designed by John Vanbrugh. Viking (L), Dean Court (M), Royal Station (M), Chase (B), York Motel (B).
