Rare treasures, at sometimes astonishingly low prices, turn up at the famous Christie’s and Sotheby’s auctions. Open-air markets, where you pay your money and take your chances: Bermondsey (Caledonian), 9-4 on Fridays; Portobello Road, 9-6 on Saturdays; and Petticoat Lane, 9-noon on Sundays.
Transportation: London’s black, boxy, turn-on-a-dime taxis cost only 12.5p for the first % mile, plus 6d or IVip for each succeeding %o mile and 6d for each passenger beyond the first one. A fixed flat fee is charged for luggage. There is a surcharge of 5p between midnight and 6 a.m.
But even strangers in the city can get around efficiently on the facilities of London Transport, which publishes all kinds of maps and guides and issues special economy tickets at its offices at 55 Broadway, S.W.l (Tel. 222-1234). Among many other good guides to London are the supremely convenient, pocket-sized London Man and London Woman, published by Sir Francis Chichester.
Double deckers and red coaches tour central London. Fares are lVip for the first mile, 3V^p for the second, 5p for the third, and lp for each additional mile. Green double decker buses make local stops around the outer periphery of London. Green Line coaches operate between central London and the surrounding Home Counties (Middlesex, Surrey, Kent and Essex).
