Edinburgh, Midlothian pop. 473,300 . . . Scotland’s capital wears an aura of quiet aristocracy, its regal stone buildings blending together in stately harmony. Princes Street is lined with fine buildings on the north side with a great view, across the deep ravine, of the Old Town that culminates in the historic Castle towering over the city from its huge rock. Take a conducted bus tour of the city first. Major points to go back to later: Edinburgh Castle—Norman chapel (1076), Scottish National War Memorial, United Services Museum (Scottish regimental regalia), Great Hall (museum of armor), Royal Apartments (including rooms of Mary Queen of Scots). The Castle is the dramatic background for spotlighted military tattoos and for many events during the annual Edinburgh Festival held in late August and early September . . . The Royal Mile—Many historic buildings on this street and its side streets, especially the Cathedral of St. Giles, Parliament House, John Knox’s house, Canongate Tolbooth, Huntly House (now contains Municipal Museum) . . . Holyrood-house—Official residence of Her Majesty when she is in Edinburgh. Open to the public are the Picture Gallery of Scottish Kings, the State Apartments, Historic Apartments . . . Scottish National Portrait Gallery and National Museum of Antiquities (in one building) . . . Birthplace of Robert Louis Stevenson . . .
