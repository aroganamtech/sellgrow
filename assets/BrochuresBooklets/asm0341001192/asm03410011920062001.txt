Oban, Argyll pop. 6,700 . . . One of the most popular coastal resorts, its yacht-filled harbor sheltered by the offshore Isle of Kerrera. Steamer excursions to Staff a (site of Fingal’s Cave, inspiration for Mendelssohn’s overture); to Iona (where ancient kings are buried in the llth-c. chapel of St. Oran): and to Mull (dramatic cliffs and mountains, legendladen castles, and a Spanish galleon supposedly full of gold at the bottom of Tobermory Bay). Oban also offers swimming, tennis, golf. Great Western (L), Caledonian (M), Park (M), Alexandra (B).
Paisley, Renfrewshire (W of Glasgow) pop. 96,670 . .. See the collection of lovely Paisley shawls in the city museum. Distinctively patterned, 20th-century versions of Victorian era shawls are still made here. Silver Thread (M), Watermill (M).
Perth, Perthshire pop. 41,650 . . . Capital of Scotland until mid-15th c.; just north, at Scone, is where ancient kings were crowned. The church of St. John is where John Knox incited the congregation to demolish Catholic friaries in 1559. But most of Perth is brightly modern with good shops, sports and entertainment. Station (L), Royal George (M), Salutation (M).
Pitlochry, Perthshire pop. 2,500 . . . Noted for its summer-long festival at Hillside Theatre in the Highlands; modern art shows too. Also excellent tennis, golf, fishing, pony-trekking, mountaineering. See the fish ladder for salmon at Pitlochry Dam.
