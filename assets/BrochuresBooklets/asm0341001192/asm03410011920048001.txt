Wells, Somerset (B-5) pop. 7,260 ... At the foot of the Mendip Hills, this town is a veritable jewel of Early English architecture. The cathedral (12th-13th centuries) is especially famed for its West Facade with six tiers of more than 300 statues. On the south side is the bishop’s moated palace where imperious swans ring a bell when they want to be fed. Nearby is Wookey Hole, an immense cave that was inhabited by both men and hyenas (alternately) some 60,000 years ago; excellent museum on the grounds. Swan (M), Crown (B), Red Lion (B), Star (B).
Winchester, Hampshire (C-5) pop. 30,310... This was the capital of Britain during the days of the Romans, Saxons, Danes and Normans. The song-popularized Gothic cathedral is gorgeously furnished and contains many famous shrines. See the local tourist office for information on the many other antiquities here. Wessex (M), Royal (B).
Windermere, Westmorland (B-3) pop. 6,620 . . . A lovely village in wooded hills near the largest lake in England, and a good base for touring the Lake District with its many associations with early-Vic-torian writers and poets. Beech Hill (M), Belsfield (M), Old England (M), Wild Boar (M), Langdale Chase (B), Royal (B).
Windsor, Berkshire (C-5) pop. 29,000 . .. Here is Windsor Castle, indomitable home of British mon-archs for nearly 900 years. When the Queen is not in residence, you can explore the State Apartments. Attend evensong in St. George’s Chapel where the Order of the Garter is bestowed; see the Queen’s Doll House; climb the Round Tower; tour the Great Park in a carriage. Across the Thames from Windsor town is famous Eton College. About 3 miles south
