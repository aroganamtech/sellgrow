Royal Opera House, Covent Garden, W.C.2; Britain’s Royal Opera and Royal Ballet.
Queen Elizabeth Hall, South Bank, S.E.l; huge, ultramodern, for a wide variety of performances; the Purcell Room features small groups and soloists. (Also in the same complex of buildings are the Hayward Art Gallery, and the National Film Theatre with classic movies from many countries.)
Wigmore Hall, Wigmore St., W.l; popular small concert hall; chamber music and recitals.
Theaters: London is the place to see new plays before they reach New York and older plays you maybe missed in New York. The best orchestra seats are only $1.80-$4.20, even for big musical productions; only $1.50-$2 in the upper circle and $1 to $1.50 in the gallery. Tickets, plus service charges, can often be obtained in advance for plays, opera and ballet through travel agents in the States. The famous National Theater appears at the Old Vic on Waterloo Rd., S.E.l. The Royal Shakespeare Company appears at the Aldwych and the Mermaid is at Puddle Dock, Upper Thames St., E.C.4, but most all the other major theaters are midtown in the Piccadilly-Shaftesbury-Strand area (W.l and W.C.2). Regular theaters are closed Sunday, but movie houses (cinemas) are open every day, and buskers still entertain queues lined up waiting to get in.
Landmarks: Among many excellent guides to the city for the diligent sightseer, Visitor’s London, available at London Transport offices, contains probably the most detailed listing of everything to be seen in London, with admission prices, hours open, and how to get there.
