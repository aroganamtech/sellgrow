V. Touring Scotland
The mainland is only 274 miles long and from 30 to 154 miles wide, but Scotland is deeply indented with fiord-like firths and lochs, and corrugated with misty glens and dramatic mountains. Towns and villages are much farther apart than in compact England and Wales. Sundays are much quieter in Scotland than they are farther south, and trains, buses and ferries do not operate in the Western Highlands and Islands. Except for hotel dining rooms, most restaurants and pubs are closed on Sunday. See page 8 for hotel rate codes.
Aberdeen, Aberdeenshire pop. 186,000...Stately silver-grey granite city of towers and spires. See Marischal and King’s colleges; cathedral of St. Machar (1366-1530); Town House; Provost Ross’s House (1593); 14th-c. Brig o’Balgownie over the River Don; Mercat Cross (1686); fascinating fish market. There are some fine old castles in the nearby countryside, and ships in the harbor have scheduled sailings to the Orkney and Shetland Islands. Station (L), Tree Tops (M), Dee Motel (B).
Aberfeldy, Perthshire (SW of Pitlochry) pop. 1,500 . . . Good fishing in the Tay, pony trekking in Highland glens and hills. Castle Menzies is nearby. Dun Aluinn (B).
Aberfoyle, Perthshire pop. 1,450. . . In the heart of Scott’s “Rob Roy” country. Loch and stream fishing, mountain scenery. Forest Hills (M), Bailie Nicol Jarvie (B).
