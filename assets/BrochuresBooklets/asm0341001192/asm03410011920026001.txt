Most discos charge admittance fees on top of membership fees, which are usually higher for foreign visitors than for the locals.
Pubs, being cozy, conversational-type places, traditionally have no entertainment except dart boards and maybe cribbage tables. A few London pubs, however, have jazz combos and poetry readings. Restaurants: All the better hotels have regal dining rooms with absolutely impeccable service. Many hotels and restaurants serve late suppers after the theater because local people are too full of tea, and its myriad accompaniments, to face dinner before the curtain goes up. You can go to a regular restaurant for tea, but hotels usually serve it in a lounge with comfy, chintzy chairs and a homey atmosphere.
Most of the big department stores have very good food to revive shoppers. Sightseers caught in a railway station, art gallery or museum are usually not so lucky, but there is sure to be a good little place not more than a block away. Scattered all over the city are fine moderate-priced chain restaurants: Angus Steak House, London Steak House, Quality Inn and Wheeler’s (seafood). Budget-priced chains include the A.B.C., Express Dairy, Fortes, Fullers, Kardomah, Lyons Corner House and others.
The popular hour for lunch is 1 p.m. in the better places, and 8:30 is the height of the dinner service. Reservations are necessary for those hours but are seldom needed if you eat earlier.
