Carmarthen, Carmarthenshire (A-5) pop. 12,900 . . . The focal point for roads leading up into wild Welsh hills, Carmarthen lies on the River Towy, where fishermen still go out in coracles. The town is associated with Merlin, King Arthur’s magician, and the “Merlin Tree” still stands on Priory Street. 7vy Bush Royal (B).
Llandrindod Wells, Radnorshire (B-4) pop. 3,160 . . . Popular mineral springs health resort in the Cambrian Mountains. Lake boating, golf, tennis, fishing in the nearby River Wye. Metropole (M), Glen Usk (M), Commodore (B).
Llandudno, Caernarvonshire (B-3) pop. 18,500 . . . Breezily situated on a scenic peninsula jutting into Conway Bay, Llandudno has two sandy beaches protected by steep cliffs. On Great Orme are prehistoric ruins, copper mines worked by early Romans, and the 12th-c. church of St. Tudno. Happy Valley Park, swimming, riding, fishing, golf, tennis, plenty of night life, steamer excursions in summer.
