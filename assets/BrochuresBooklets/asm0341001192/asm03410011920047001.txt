April 23, the date of the writer’s death in 1616, and probably also of his birth in 1564, is celebrated with processions. Midwinter is delightful in Stratford, when few tourists visit Shakespeare’s birthplace, Anne Hathaway’s cottage and all the other landmarks, and the theater switches from Shakespearean dramas to opera, ballet and modern plays from London. The town is a good base for tours of the lovely little Cotswolds towns, and to nearby castles and stately homes (be sure to see Compton Wynyates). Alveston Manor (L), Red Horse (L), Welcombe (L), Falcon (M), Shakespeare (M), Swan’s Nest (M).
Tunbridge Wells, Kent (D-5) pop. 41,280 . . . An exquisitely quaint town of handsome villas, mansions and parks. In the nearby rolling countryside are some of England’s grandest stately homes: Knole, Cheven-ing House, Sissinghurst Castle, Chiddingstone Castle, Hever Castle, Scotney Castle and Penshurst Place. At Westerham is Chartwell, home of Sir Winston Churchill, which is now open to the public. Calverly (M), Spa (M), Wellington (B).
Warwick, Warwickshire (C-4) pop. 16,870 ... One of the most romantic storybook spots in all England, the majestic old castle was founded by William the Conqueror. Today it contains a fine collection of armor and paintings. Also see the Lord Leicester Hospital, built by one of Elizabeth I’s favorites; Landor House, home of poet Walter Savage Landor (1775-1864); the parish church with its magnificent tombs; and the Doll Museum. Lord Leycester (B), Tudor House (B).
