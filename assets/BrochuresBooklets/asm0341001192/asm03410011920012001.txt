If a formal occasion arises unexpectedly, you can rent the proper clothes for it from Moss Bros, in London ... If you smoke, take in all the cigars, cigarettes or tobacco that Customs allows (see arriving in Britain). Buying smokes in restaurants is even more expensive than tobacconist shops . . . Some films and camera equipment cost less than in the States; film developing services are good, usually take 48 hours.
Postage: Airmail postage to the U.S. is 71Ap (180) per V2 ounce for letters; 4p (90) for airletters and postcards. A cablegram is 14p (340) per word; 7 words minimum.
Religion: The official religion in England is Protestant Episcopal (Anglican). The official religion in Scotland is Presbyterian. But all sects are represented. Shopping: Most London stores are open weekdays until 5:30 and stay open one night a week until 7 or 8. In smaller cities and towns they close early on one or two afternoons a week. Prices include a purchase tax but for visitors on purchases worth £5 ($12) or more, if they’re delivered to your plane or ship, or sent directly home by the store, a tax remission can be obtained. Antiques (with a certificate proving they are 100 or more years old) and bona fide original works of art enter the U.S. free of customs duties, and Britain is a great place to collect treasures. Rare items, of course, bring a high price.
