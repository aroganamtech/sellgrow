Rye, Sussex (D-5) pop. 4,370 . .. The Ypres Tower has stood since the 12th century, and so has the parish church with a clock pendulum about 20 feet long that has been swinging time away since Elizabeth I. The Mermaid Inn, built in 1420, is riddled with secret staircases and hidden rooms for the protection of smugglers. About 10 miles northwest of town is romantic Bodiam Castle, built in 1386 and surrounded by a wide moat. Mermaid Inn (M).
St. Ives, Cornwall (A-6) pop. 8,680 . . . This exceedingly picturesque old town maintains a raffish atmosphere with its large colony of artists. A fun town with surfing on the Atlantic beach, calm waters on St. Ives Bay. Garrack (M), Porthminster (M), Tregenna Castle (M).
Salisbury, Wiltshire (C-5) pop. 35,800 . . . Salisbury’s majestic cathedral, consecrated in 1258, is one of the most impressive sights in all England and should be visited with a guide. Among Salisbury’s many other old buildings are the beautifully furnished Mompesson House (1701), the House of John A’Port (15th century) and some very old inns and pubs. Just 2XA miles west of town is Wilton House, one of the most stately and most magnificently furnished homes in England. Stonehenge, that mystery-laden circle of gigantic boulders set in place at least 3,000 years ago, rises starkly from Salisbury Plain, 8 miles north of town; summer solstice rites here every June 21 or 22. Rose and Crown (M), Red Lion (M), White Hart (B), County (B).
