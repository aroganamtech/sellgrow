Post Office Tower, Tottenham Court Rd., W.l. A 580-foot novelty on the London skyline; 3 viewing platforms, cocktail lounge and revolving restaurant.
Regent’s Park, N.W.l. London Zoo; open-air Shakespearean performances in summer.
St. Bartholomew the Great, Smithfield, E.C.l. London’s oldest parish church, founded in 1123.
St. James’s Palace, Pall Mall, S.W. 1. Built by Henry VIII, this was the Royal Residence from the 17th century until Queen Victoria appropriated Buckingham Palace. All foreign ambassadors are presented at the Court of St. James.
St. Martin-in-the-Fields, Trafalgar Sq., Spire designed by James Gibbs (b. 1682) admiringly adopted by many American churches.
St. Mary-Le-Bow, Cheapside, E.C.2. Spire by Sir Christopher Wren, 1680. The Bow bells chimes encompass true Cockneys.
St. Paul’s Cathedral, Ludgate Hill, E.C.4. One of Wren’s greatest masterpieces, built from 1675 to 1710; carving on stalls and organ case by Grinling Gibbons (b. 1648).
Tower of London, Tower Hill, E.C.3. More here than just the crown jewels; the Tower is spooky with memories of famous imprisonments and executions in its long history since 1078; Beefeater Guards.
Trafalgar Square, W.C.2. Famous for fountains, pigeons, bronze lions and the Nelson Column. Traffic siphons through Admiralty Arch to The Mall leading to Buckingham Palace.
