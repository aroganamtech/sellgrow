Broadway, Worcestershire (SW of Stratford-Upon-Avon, C-4) pop. 2,725 ... This Cotswold village is without compare for its handsome stone houses and impeccable gardens. It is very old—Lygon Arms dates from the 17th century and St. Eadburgha’s Church was begun in the 12th—but the entire town is as meticulously maintained as if it were a movie set. Lygon Arms (M), Broadway (M).
Brockenhurst, Hampshire (NE of Bournemouth, C-5) pop. 2,660 ... A good home base for expeditions through the lovely New Forest. Fine golf courses here, and a wonderful collection of antique cars and motorcycles in the Montagu Motor Museum at nearby Beaulieu Palace House. Balmer Lawn (M), Carey’s Manor (M).
Bury St. Edmunds, Suffolk (E of Cambridge, D-4) pop. 22,270 ... A cathedral city of great beauty, aristocracy and antiquity. Principal buildings are in the best Georgian style, but there are also many delightful old timbered inns and houses. The Angel Hotel was a coaching inn patronized by Dickens (and Mr. Pickwick). Angel (M), Suffolk (B).
Buxton, Derbyshire (C-4) pop. 19,400 ... A very popular resort in the Peak District, known for its wild natural beauty and thermal springs. This is the setting for an Easter Steeplechase, a May Music Festival and a November Drama Festival. Buxton is near Poole’s Hole, an interesting stalactite cave, and less than 10 miles away is a Bronze Age “henge” of stones. Also in the neighborhood are three of England’s most impressive stately homes: Chatsworth, filled with priceless books and art objects. Haddon
