Brighton and Hove, Essex (C-5) pop. 162,650 ... It was King George IV who turned Brighton into a glitteringly fashionable health and bathing resort. The wildly imaginative Royal Pavilion, designed by John Nash, was completed in 1822. It is regally furnished as it was in George’s day, complete with chi-noserie and a fantastic kitchen. The city’s Philharmonic Orchestra performs in the former royal stables, but Brighton is better known for jazzier music in its many nightclubs. There’s an elaborate gambling casino, a booming fun fair along the seaside promenade, and an annual Veteran Car Rally. The adjoining town of Hove is much quieter, dignified by some unusually fine Regency-style houses. In Brighton: Metropole (L), Bedford (M), Grand (M), Royal Albion (M). In Hove: Courtlands (M), Dudley (M), New Imperial (M), Sackville (M).
Bristol, Gloucestershire (B--5) pop. 434,000 . . . “Shipshape and Bristol fashion” is the theme of this seafaring city where pirates once swigged rum in taverns still lining the quayside. The little Royal Theater, connected by underground passage to the ancient Llandoger Trow tavern, has been in continuous operation longer than any other English theater.
