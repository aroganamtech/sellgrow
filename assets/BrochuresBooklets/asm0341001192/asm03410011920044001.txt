Oxford, Oxfordshire (C-5) pop. 108,880 .. . One of the loveliest and most interesting of cities. Take a conducted tour not only of the university but of the town too. Added to architectural riches are the important Ashmolean Museum, the Museum of the History of Science, and Bodleian, one of the world’s great libraries. The beautiful old buildings are scattered all over the city and it would take days to see them all. Senior of all the ancient pubs is the Bear Inn, opened in 1242. Excelsior Motor Lodge (M), Oxford Motel (B) and the Randolph Hotel (B) offer the most modern accommodations. Ten miles north of town is the magnificent immensity of Blenheim Palace, home of the Dukes of Marlborough since 1722 and birthplace of Sir Winston Churchill. A sound and light performance is held here nightly in summer; the Bear (M) at Woodstock is good and convenient. Sir Winston is buried in the churchyard in the tiny village of Bladon. Farther north, see Clay-don House, its elaborate décor an incredible fantasy of rococo plaster and woodcarving.
Plymouth, Devonshire (B-6) pop. 213,800 .. . Historic port city. Sir John Hawkins lived here; Sir Francis Drake was playing at bowls on the beautiful Hoe esplanade when the Spanish Armada appeared in the Channel; and, of course, this was where the Mayflower sailed from in 1620. Holiday Inn (L).
Portsmouth, Hampshire (C-5) pop. 221,470 . . . Charles Dickens was born here in 1812; the house is now a museum. H.M.S. Victory, on which Lord Nelson met his death at Trafalgar in 1805, is in dry-dock and is quite a tourist attraction. The Victory Museum has Nelson relics.
