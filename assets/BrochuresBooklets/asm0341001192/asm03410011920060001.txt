Try to be in Inverness for the great Highland Games held in July amid skirls of bagpipers. Six miles east of town are the tragic mound graves of clan members who perished at the Battle of Culloden in Bonnie Prince Charlie’s last attempt at freedom for Scotland in 1746. Also here are the Clava Cairns, three great stone rings erected by late Neolithic or early Bronze Age men. At Drumnadrochit, about 10 miles southwest of town, is the 16th-c. Urquhart Castle, one of the country’s largest. Caledonian (L), Station (L), Muirtown Motel (B).
Kelso, Roxburghshire (E of Melrose) pop. 3,900 . . . Historic border town at the juncture of the Teviot and Tweed rivers. The restored abbey was founded in 1128. Ednam House (M).
Kingussie, Inverness-shire pop. 1,000 . . . Neat little village in the Spey Valley. Worth a detour for its excellent Highlands Museum. Good Clan Mac-pherson museum in neighboring Newtonmore. Duke of Gordon (B).
Kirkcudbright, Kircudbrightshire pop. 2,500.
. . . Attractively situated on the Dee estuary, popular with artists. Hornel museum of art, manuscripts and furnishings. Maclellan’s Castle (16th c.). Tolbooth, where John Paul Jones was imprisoned for murder in 1770. Ruins of Dundrennan Abbey (4Vi miles southeast) date from 1142. Selkirk Anns (B).
Kyle of Lochalsh, Ross and Cromarty pop. 1,650 ... A major port for MacBrayne steamers to the Isle of Skye, the Inner and Outer Hebrides, and mainland ports in the Western Highlands. Lochalsh (L).
