It was in 1513 that the Spanish explorer Ponce de Leon landed on the east coast of Florida. When Henry Flagler extended his Florida East Coast Railroad south to Miami in 1896, the area was occupied by only a few farming homesteaders and some rather questionable people who salvaged cargos from ships wrecked on the offshore reefs. When the Florida land boom flared up In the early 1920s, it seemed that everyone in America wanted to own property in Miami.
Miami Beach is on a slim thread of land 3.5 miles east of Miami, between Biscayne Bay and the Atlantic Ocean. Collins Avenue, extends the full length of the beach, for about 8 miles. From 71st Street southward, Collins Avenue has a succession of huge oceanfront hotels, each trying to be more opulent and stunning than the next. Population is 347,618 in Miami proper, and 93,000 in Miami Beach. The Greater Miami area has a total population of over 1,400,000.
WHAT TO SEE: Biscayne Bay, Miamarina, with its splendid yachts, Coconut Grove, Venetian Pool and Hialeah the famous winter horse race track in an exotic setting which also has a flamingo colony. The Museum of Science, the Planetarium, the Wax Museum, the Seaquarium and the 800 year old Spanish Monastery Cloisters. The Vizcaya Palace and the Bayfront Park Rock Garden, the Fairchild Tropical Gardens in Coral Gables and the Japanese Garden In Watson Park.
RESTAURANTS:
A/MIAMI: Among hundreds of good eating places are: Casa Santino, Flamenco, Ambassadors andHoratio’s, moderate to expensive; Don Julio’s and Centro Vasco, Mexican, Spanish and American, moderate; Red Coach Grill and Gallagher’s Steak House steaks and lobster, moderate to expensive; The Rusty Pelican and Miamarina are interesting with their waterfront views.
B/MIAMI: The better eating places In Miami Beach tend to be expensive. It is wise to reserve ahead unless you dine very early or after 8:30 PM. Bernard’s Carriage House in Miami Beach, Cafe Chauveron in Bay Harbor Island and Chez Vendome in the David William Hotel in Coral Gables are all elegantly continental and among the more expensive restaurants. There are dozens of good Chinese restaurants throughout Greater Miami and hundreds of good Cuban restaurants.
ART AND MUSIC: Bass Museum of Art, Lowe Art Gallery, Ballet, symphony and opera performances are held in the Dade County Auditorium in Miami and the Miami Beach Auditorium. Theaters include the Player’s Theater, housed in the Museum of Science, and the Coconut Grove Playhouse, both In Miami. The Ring Theater is at the University of Miami, in Coral Gables.
SHOPPING: Good buys are locally-made women’s clothing and swimsuits. Popular take home souvenirs are coconut candles, citrus fruits, tropical jellies and seashell ornaments. Burdine’s and Jordan Marsh are excellent department stores In Miami. Lincoln Road Mall on Miami Beach has nice shops, but the most elegant shoping area on the beach Is in Bal Harbour, across from the Americana Hotel. Mike’s Cigar’s of Miami Beach Is stacked floor to celling with fine cigars from all over the world.
WHERE TO GO NEARBY: The Oceanfront towns of Hollywood, Fort Lauderdale and those from Delray Beach to Palm Beach. The Lion Country Safari (17 mles), the Parrot Jungle (11 miles), Coral Castle (25 miles) and Everglades National Park (40 miles). For a special treat, Cape Kennedy and Disney World, are a must while in Miami.
