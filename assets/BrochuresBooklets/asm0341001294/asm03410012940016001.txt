The roast beef is not frozen. Instead it is prepared daily, and cooking is started — the roast is seared and cooked for about 15 minutes. Roasting is completed in an infra-red oven in the Clipper’s galley. Roast beef is served from a rolling cart tray which is pushed to the side of your seat, so that you not only may request your portion rare, or medium, or well done, but can actually watch the steward carve and serve it.
A great part of the fun or air travel is eating aloft.
When the cabin attendants have finished clearing away, we’ll get Miss Stanley to tell us how stewards and stewardesses learn how to serve us so well at meal time and throughout the trip.
NGS TO DO
1.	See motion pictures or film strips telling how meals are prepared and served in flight.
2.	Ask your friends who are going to visit other countries to collect menus and cook books for you. By reading them you can learn much about the language and food specialties of the countries.
3.	Plan a "dramatization or airline meal party" in your classroom, arranging your chairs and desks so that they are placed similarly to airline seating. Choose a steward and stewardess to serve milk, sandwiches and cookies or a menu of your choice.
