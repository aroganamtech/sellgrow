Equator and International Date Line
Shortly before you reach Canton Island westbound, you will cross the equator, an imaginary line encircling the earth midway between the poles. Midway between Canton Island and the Fijis on the South Pacific routes and two-thirds of the way between Honolulu and Wake Island on the Central Pacific Routes, the Clippers will pass over another imaginary line—the International Date Line. This line follows approximately the 180th Meridian on either side of which the date differs by one day. When you cross the International Date Line westbound you lose one day from the week—eastbound, you gain a day; thus flying from Canton Island to the Fijis or Honolulu to Wake Island you lose Saturday, Sunday, or any other day in the week concerned. This lost day is made up on the eastbound flight when you will have two Saturdays, two Sundays, etc.

CHARACTERISTICS ... Canton presents a colorful display of bird life and fish. This is the home of big bos’n birds, hijacking frigates, booby birds and terns. In the lagoon are wahoo, tuna and ulua. Temperatures average from 70 to 90 degrees. Average annual humidity: 67%.
POPULATION.. .The resident population, approximately 320, is largely British, CAA and Pan American personnel.
The "native” population was largely imported from the Gilbert and Ellice Islands.
SIZE... Although the largest of the Phoenix Island Group, Canton is merely a coral speck in the Pacific covering only 8.5 square miles. It’s a low, narrow rim of land, sparsely covered with bunch grass, scaevola shrubs and a few coconut palms, surrounding a pear-shaped shallow lagoon. At no point is Canton more than 20 feet above sea level.
GOVERNMENT. . . Two flags fly over Canton Island, an Anglo-American Condominium. In April 1939, Canton and nearby Enderbury Island were placed under joint British and American control for 50 years and thereafter until terminated or modified by mutual consent.

LOCATION.. .New Zealand consists of two large islands (North Island and South Island) and numerous small islands of which Stewart Island, just below South Island, and Chatham Island, 500 miles to the east, are the largest. Auckland is about 1,300 air miles east of Sydney, Australia and about the same distance south of the Fiji Islands. Airport: Whenuapai Field; 20 miles to downtown Auckland—45 minutes by ground transportation.
CHARACTERISTICS ... New Zealand is unique. It is a wonderland of scenery, a sportsman’s dream spot, a delightful place in which to take a completely different vacation. The Southern Alps and the fiords, the fertile and lush bushland, the lakes and waterfalls compare favorably with anything in Europe or America. Their fishing is generally considered the best in the world, their beaches excellent and their winter sports splendid. For anyone looking for the unusual, New Zealand provides the answer; it has the lowest death rate in the world and a climate that is truly perfect.

LOCATION... Australia lies in the South Pacific about as far south of the equator as Mexico is north. LOCATION...Lat. 19°l6'NLong. 166°39'E-Elev. 10'. Wake Island lies alone in the North Pacific, 2,130 miles due west of Honolulu—1,500 miles east of Guam. Airport: Wake.
CHARACTERISTICS.. .This atoll consists of three islets, Wilkes, Peale and Wake which are separated by narrow, shallow channels and enclose a triangular lagoon 4% miles long. Sport fish are found in the waters outside the reef, while the lagoon shelters a colorful collection of small tropical fish. The climate here averages 80°—slightly warmer than Honolulu. Average annual humidity: 78%. Wake is completely dependent upon mechanical distilleries and some rain catchment for its fresh water supply.
POPULATION.. .Wake’s population averages 700,largely representatives of Pan American, Standard Oil Co. and the U.S. Government. Wake has no "native” population.
SIZE.. .Wake Island covers 2 square miles and at no point is it more than 21 feet above sea level.
GOVERNMENT.. .Commander Edward Taussig, U.S.N., on U. S. S. Bennington took formal possession for the U.S. A. January 17, 1899. Jurisdiction of Wake Island was turned over to the U.S. Navy Department in 1934. In 1948 administrative responsibility was placed with the U.S. Civil Aeronautics Administration.
