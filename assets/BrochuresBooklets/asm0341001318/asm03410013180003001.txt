CHAPTER I - “THE FLYING BOAT ERA’
Following his spectacular Atlantic crossing in 1927 Charles Lindbergh made a promotional tour of the 48 States and 17 countries of Latin America, sparking a surge of public interest in commercial aviation. It has been said that “Lindbergh awakened the nation, almost single-handedly, to the enormous future possibilities of air transportation.”
In the life of another young man and a few of his friends 1927 was the year they were trying to pull together an airline to fly between Key West, Florida, and Havana, Cuba! Mr. Juan Terry Trippe, along with a group of associates, came to Florida in 1927 and joined forces with two other groups under the corporate name, “The Aviation Corporation of The Americas”. Juan Trippe was named to head the operating subsidiary called “Pan American Airways, Inc.”
Pan American’s new mail contract called for the company to start flying the mail between

Key West and Havana not later than October 19, 1927. However, the Fokker tri-motors Mr. Trippe had ordered were not yet delivered. As the fateful date approached it became clear to Mr. Whitbeck, Pan American’s manager at Key West, that alternative arrangements had to be made. Accordingly he hailed a passing aviator, Cy Caldwell, who was flying through Florida at the time delivering a Fairchild float plane to West Indian Aerial Express, a struggling Dominican-based airline which Pan American bought up the following year. Mr. Caldwell agreed to lease his plane to Pan American and to fly the airline’s first round trip to Havana carrying the mails.
A few days later two Fokker tri-motors were delivered to Pan American at their Meacham Field base at Key West. On October 28, 1927, the airline began operating a daily air mail schedule to Havana using their own equipment, the Fokker F-7s.
