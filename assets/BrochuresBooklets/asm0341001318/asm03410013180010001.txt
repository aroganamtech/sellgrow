Pan American started trans-Atlantic service to Europe in 1939 with this plane, routing by way of the Azores and Lisbon to Marsailles. In July of 1940, using a B-314, the Company reopened the South Pacific service to New Zealand. This route had been temporarily abandoned in 1938 when Capt. Edwin Musick and his crew were lost in the crash of their S-42 survey ship near Pago Pago.
Pan American’s operations had been moved across the Bay from Alameda to Treasure Island when the Golden Gate Exposition opened in 1939. The comings and goings of the flying clippers would be an added attraction on the exposition grounds!

Pan American’s long-range flying boats became involved in the carriage of top priority personnel and materials to all parts of the world!
