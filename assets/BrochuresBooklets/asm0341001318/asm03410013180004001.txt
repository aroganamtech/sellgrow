The F-7 tri-motor could carry eight passengers and a few hundred pounds of mail at a speed of 90 mph! Only three of this model were used by Pan American. A later model, the Fokker F-10, was introduced in January of 1929, after the airline had moved their base of operations to the 54th Street Municipal Airport at Miami.
In January, 1929, service was begun to Nassau and San Juan. In that month, too, Pan American moved to a field of some 100-odd acres which the company had bought west of town on 36th Street, Miami. That field is no\y part of the much larger Miami International Airport!
The Fokker F-10 carried the larger 425 HP Wasp engines, and its maximum takeoff gross wt. was 13,100 lbs. The new model carried up to 12 passengers.
Since there were few land airfields available for Pan American’s operations in Latin America it soon developed that the seaplane, - or better yet the amphibian,-would be the most efficient vehicle for international air commerce on the Carribean routes. Natural landing sites were available on the surfaces of lakes, rivers and harbors near most of the major cities of the world.
At the behest of Pan American, early in 1928, the Sikorsky Co. of Long Island, N.Y., produced the S-38, a twin-engined amphibian plane which could fly a distance of 300 miles at a speed of roughly 100 mph! The eight-
passenger S-38 was an excellent ship and is credited with saving the Sikorsky Company from financial ruin; 120 of them were built! Pan American’s first S-38 was delivered October 31st, 1928. A total of 38 of these planes were eventually used by the airline.
Sikorsky S-38, 1928
In February, 1929, Charles Lindbergh, who had joined Pan American as technical advisor, piloted the inaugural airmail flight from Miami to Cristobal, in the Canal Zone, with Vice Pres. John Hambleton acting as co-pilot. This was Lindbergh’s first official flight for Pan Ameri-, can.
After two overnight stops, Lindbergh landed at France Field, Cristobal, C.Z., in the afternoon of February 6th, 1929. Total flying time for the 2,000 mile flight was 21:25!
In February, 1929, Pan American and the W.R. Grace Shipping Co. jointly formed “Pan-agra”, Pan American-Grace Airline. This union established Pan American along the west coast of S. America.

Service to San Juan was started in January, 1929; to the Canal Zone in February, as already related; to Santiago, Chile, in July; and over the Andes to Buenos Aires in October,-all in 1929.
Having thus opened up the Caribbean and the west coast of South America to scheduled air service, Mr. Trippe then beheld on the east coast of S. America a formidable competitor -New York Rio&Buenos Aires Line(N.Y.R.B. A.).
