The line, founded by Col. Ralph O’Neil, had sound American financial backing and the latest flying equipment, the Consolidated “Commodores.”
N.Y.R.B.A. Commodore
N.Y.R.B.A. was losing money, however, having just established operations; and when it was made clear that the airline would not get a U.S. mail contract, the company threw in the towel and sold out to Pan American in the Fall of 1930.
Consolidated Commodore, 1930
Commodore Cabin
So by the end of 1930 Pan American was providing air service to nearly all of the major cities of S. America! With the purchase of N.Y.R.B.A. Pan American acquired 14 Consolidated Commodores. This fine ship carried up to 22 passengers at a speed of just over 100 mph. Buenos Aires» by air, was now only six days away from New York! By surface vessel the trip took three weeks!
Capt. Bill Masland, now retired, recalled “When I joined the Company the S-38s and the Commodores made the long weary run to Buenos Aires,-30 stops along the way,-through the islands and down the east coast of S. America.” There were six overnight stops enroute!
A floating barge passenger terminal and docking facility was acquired in the N.Y.R.B.A. purchase and used by Pan American at Dinner Key for about three years, until the permanent facilities were constructed there and put into service in 1934.
With the purchase of the Mexican airline, C.M.A., in January, 1929, Pan American linked Brownsville, Texas, to the Caribbean system. Ford tri-motors were used on the Brownsville-Mexico City run. The inaugural flight to Mexico City was flown by Charles Lindbergh in March, 1929.
Ford tri-motor, 1929
The Ford had a 78 ft. wing span and 14,000 lbs. gross weight; Wasp engines of 420 hp, and a 12 passenger capacity. The radio operator’s station was at the forward right-hand seat in the cabin.
