Fifth Anniversary of Republic Day. Malawi celebrates its achievement of Republic status with traditional dancing, sports, ceremonies. At all main centers. July 6.
MALI
National Army Day. Parades, dancing, parachute jumping. January 20.
Labor Day. Parades, dances. May 1. National Festival of the Republic. Parades, dances. September 22.
MAURITIUS
Game Fishing. South Indian Ocean International Game Fish Clubs Competition, January 2-9; Mauritius International Contest, end of January.
Cavadee. Colorful event marks end of fast of the Tamils. February-mid-March.
Horse Racing. The Maiden Plate (Mauritius Derby) takes place end of August. Season runs May-October.
Festival Kwantee. Commemorates birth of God Kwantee. Depending on Lunar Calendar, celebrated in July or August. Divali—Festival of Lights. October or November.
Fire-walking Ceremonies. October-March.
MOROCCO
Cherry Festival. Sefrou. April.
National Folklore Festival. Marrakesh. 10 days beginning 1st Friday in May. Rose Festival. Kelaa des N'Gouna. End of May.
Waxwork Festival. In Sale, on the Mou-loud. July.
Date Festival. Erfoud. October. Equestrian Festival. Tissa, near Fez, 1st week October.
MOZAMBIQUE
Bullfights. Lourenco Marques. January, April, July.
Lourenco Marques Festival. International events include motor races, championship golf, rowing race, sailing race, horse-riding competition, championship lawn tennis, championship fencing, Ski and folklore exhibitions, championship game fishing. Mozambique Trade Fair and Exhibition. Bullfights. Public concerts. Lourenco Marques. July.
Beira Festival. International golf, bowling, skeet shooting competitions; international city circuit; popular fair; Portuguese folklore exhibition. Beira. August. Chimoio Trade Fair and Exhibition.
Vila Pery. August or September.
Niassa Trade Fair and Exhibition. Nam-pula. October.
International Game Fishing. Bazaruto Islands. November.
International Motor Race. Lourenco Marques. December.
NIGER
Native Market Days. Bigmarkets in full swing as farmers sell their peanuts and other produce. Zinder and Maradi. November through February.
Game Reserves. Lions, elephants and other animals may be visited at a game reserve just 60 miles from the capital. Herds of giraffes may also be seen grazing in Tillabery. Best time—month of December.
Republic Day. Big parade mixes modem and traditional Africa. Participants vary from soldiers with modern weapons to horsemen and camelmen with bows and arrows and spears, dressed in their traditional war attire. December 18.
NIGERIA
Idul Fitr Celebrations. Mark end of Ramadan fast. Prayers, processions, feasting, dancing in Moslem communities at Kano, Katsina, Bida, Zaria, Sokoto, Mai-duguri, other northern cities; Emirs attend prayers escorted by nobles, mounted warriors. Acrobats, jugglers, sideshows, snake charmers appear. At Ka-tisina and Zaria, spectacular jahi charges may take place. January 2, 3.
Argungu Fishing Festival. Colorful "fish drive"—fishing with nets and bare hands—on Sokoto River. Drumming, dancing, merrymaking. Argungu, Sokoto Province. February.
Pateggi Regatta. Colorful festival on River Niger. Canoe races, decorated boats, swimming races, drumming and feasting. February.
Egungun Festivals. Main features: worship, singing, dancing, masquerades. Celebrations last 7 to 14 days, take place at Offa, Oyo, Ijebu, Igbo and many other towns in Western Nigeria. March-May. Agemo Festival. Ageless festival in which Agemo priest blesses celebrants to "prolong their lives." Much dancing and drumming go on. This is only occasion when the Orimulusi (King) of Ijebu Igbo is compelled to dance publicly by tradition. At Ijebu Igbo. In May. Malaudun Nabiyyi/Prophet's Birthday. Notable celebrations—dancing, feasting—at Zaria, Kano, Sokoto, Ibadan, Lagos, and all Islamic communities in Nigeria. June.
Awankere Festival. A popular Okere Masquerade dance with performances once a week for seven weeks. In Warri. July-August.
Ogun Festival. Sacrifices to god of iron. Drumming, dancing, singing, merriment. Many towns in Western Nigeria celebrate between July-October.
Oshun Festival. Sacrificing, drumming and dancing. Oshogbo. August.
Igue Festival. A seven day colorful festival, dating back to the 15th century. Most important yearly event in Benin City. Oba (King) in full regalia does traditional dances, his queens in ancient Bini costumes. Mock war, chiefs' dancing processions. Benin City. December.
