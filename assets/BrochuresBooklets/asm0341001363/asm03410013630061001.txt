JAPAN
New Year's Day. Japan's "festival of festivals". Colorful street decorations. People pay homage at shrines, visit friends and relatives. January 1.
Dezome Shiki/Firemen's Demonstration. Famous parade of fire brigades. Acrobatic firemen perform atop tall bamboo ladders. Tokyo. January 6.
Snow Festival. Elaborate snow sculptures. Sapporo in Hokkaido. Late January-early February.
Setsubun/Bean-Throwing Festival. Traditional throwing of beans to drive away imaginary devils from temple grounds in all Japan. February 3.
Hinamatsuri/ Dolls' Festival. Sets of "Hina" dolls and miniature household articles displayed. In all Japan. March 3. Cherry Blossom Dances. "Azuma Odori" in Tokyo and "Miyako Odori" in Kyoto. In April.
Buddha's Birthday. "Amacha" tea poured on images of Buddha at temples. April 8.
Dontaku. Many processions with participants disguised as legendary gods. Fukuoka, Kyushu. May 3,4.
Cormorant Fishing Spectacle. Trained birds do the fishing by torchlight. Na-gara River, Gifu. May 11-October 15. Toshugu Shrine Grand Festival. Over 1,000 armor-clad people parade, escorting shrine palanquins. Nikko. May 18. Rice Planting Festival. Twelve beautiful girls perform ceremony to accompaniment of music, folk songs. Sumiyoshi Shrine, Osaka. June 14.
Chagu-Chagu Umakko/Horse Festival. Brightly decorated horses led to shrine. Morioka, Iwate Prefecture. June 15. Sanno Festival of Hie Shrine. Shrine palanquins parade in one of Tokyo's largest, gayest festivals. June 15.
Bon Festival, Feast of Lanterns. Lanterns lighted to welcome souls of the dead visiting earth. Nationwide. July 13-15.
Gion Festival of Yasaka Shrine. Famous festival, dating back to 9th century. "Yama" and "hoko" floats. Kyoto.
July 16-17.
Nomaoi/Wild Horse Chasing. A thousand horsemen in ancient armor vie for three shrine flags on Hibarigahara Plain. Men try to catch wild horses. Hara-machi, Fukushima Prefecture.
July 23-25.
Kanto Festival. Young men in festive costumes try skill at balancing the kanto, a bamboo frame holding many paper lanterns. Akita, Akita Prefecture. August 5-7.
Peace Festival. Held at Peace Memorial Park, Hiroshima, in memory of A-bomb victims. August 6.
Awa Odori. Entire city alive with singing and dancing. Day and night, groups of musicians in fancy attire parade the streets of Tokushima. August 15 to 18. Yabusame at Tsurugaoka Hachiman Shrine. Fascinating game of horseback archery, a reminder of feudal samurai warrior competitions. Kamakura, Kana-gawa Prefecture. September 16. Chrysanthemum Doll Shows depict human beings, animals, in garments of flowers. Many parts of Japan during October, November.
Okunchi Festival of Suwa Shrine. Colorful dances of Chinese origin; floats and palanquins add to the gaiety. Nagasaki. October 7-9.
Jidai Matsuri/ Festival of Eras. Celebrates Kyoto's founding in 794; parade groups portray subsequent history.
Heian Shrine. October 22.
Daimyo Procession. Journeys of feudal lords and retainers faithfully re-created. Hakone, Kanagawa Prefecture. November 3.
Toshi-no-Ichi/Year-end Market. Stalls sell New Year decorations. Kannon Temple, Anakusa, Tokyo. Mid-December.
On Matsuri. Gala processions of masqueraders as courtiers, retainers, wrestlers of long ago days. Classical dances, plays. Kasuga Shrine, Nara.
December 16-18.
Namahage. Men disguised as devils visit door-to-door on Oga Peninsula, Akita Prefecture. December 31.
KOREA
New Year's Celebrations. First three days celebrated. Most Koreans observe Lunar New Year. January.
Independence Movement Day. Koreans observe anniversary of 1919 movement. March 1.
Buddha's Birthday. Solemn rituals at temples, lantern parades at night.
April 8.
Dano Festival Day. One of Korea's major holidays. Wrestling, women's swinging contests. May 5.
Farmers' Day. Farmers in colorful costume rejoice with age-old farmers' dances and music. June 10.
Liberation Day and Moon Festival. Anniversary of Korea's liberation in 1945. By the lunar calendar, day is also Chu-sok, the Moon Festival. Viewing of full moon in evening. August 15.
National Foundation Day. Marks traditional founding of Korea by Dangun 4300 years ago. October 3.
LAOS
Laos New Year. National celebration. Water Festival throughout Kingdom, most enjoyable in Royal Capital of Luang Prabang, where entire population—including His Majesty the King— is doused with water. Spring. Visakhabousa. Most amusing of popular celebrations. May.
Celebration, End of Buddhist Lent. Boat races, illuminations along the Mekong and all over capital city. October.
Festival and Exposition. Traditional celebrations, held annually. At That Luang Shrine. November.
