ANTIGUA
Son et Lumiere Showings. Sound and Light production. Friday evenings, De-cember-April.
New Year's Day. Steelbands, masquerades, clowns, street dancing. January 1. Pan Am Annual Spear Fishing Competition. Whit Monday, May 31.
June Week. Games, afternoon teas, flag selling—to raise funds for the Red Cross. First week of June.
Sailing Week. Lord Nelson Regatta—a week of sailing and festivity for yachtsmen of the Caribbean. First week of June.
Carnival Festival. Calypso, steelband, Queen contests; parades, floats, costumed troupes, street dancing, merrymaking. Last week of July through August 3.
Eastern Caribbean Netball Tournment. August.
State Day Celebrations. Arts and cultural exhibitions. November 1.
ARUBA
Carnival. Carnival Queen election, February 17; Grand Carnival Parade, February 21; Grand Old Mask Parade, burning of "King Momo." Oranjestad. February 23.
Queen Juliana's Birthday Celebrations.
Fireworks, parades, dances, sports events, other special events. April 30. Labor Day. Costumed Maypole dancers; "tumba" music, dances. May 1.
St. John's Day. Colorful folk dance, "Derramento di Gai" performed in countryside. June 24.
Aruba Sports Union Olympiad. Sports tournaments. Last week of June.
U.S. Independence Day. Celebrated by Aruba's American colony. July 4.
New Year's Eve. "Dandes" sing and play humorous songs at homes. Parties, fireworks. December 31.
BAHAMAS
Hobby Horse Hall Horseracing. By native teenagers—on Tuesdays and Fridays. January-April.
New Year's Day Junkanoo Parade.
Mardi Gras-like parade along Nassau's Bay Street. Begins 4 a.m. January 1. Sound and Light Festival. Dramatic spectacle of the history of the Bahamas presented each evening at Fort Charlotte. Year-round.
19th Annual Winter Fishing Tournament. 23 game fish eligible.
January 11-April 25.
Miami-Lucaya Open Yacht Race.
Ocean-going yachts race a 95-mile course from Miami across the Gulf Stream to Lucaya. February 18.
Bahamas Humane Society Ball. A highlight of the social season. Nassau.
March.
International Garden Club Annual Flower Show. Nassau. March.
33rd Annual Miami-Nassau Ocean Yacht Race. One of Southern Ocean Racing Conference circuit's outstanding races. Across Gulf Stream, Bahama Banks. March 1.
Bahamas Red Cross Fair. Old-fashioned carnival on the grounds of Government House, Nassau. Sponsored by governor, booths served by socially prominent. March 5.
Nassau Cup Race. From Nassau Harbour Light round Booby Rock Light and return. 30 miles. Open to boats in Miami to Nassau Yacht Race. March 5. 18th Annual Out Island Regatta. Bahamas natives vie for prizes in sailboats that make up one of the world's last wind-driven commercial fleets. George Town, Exuma. April.
Bimini Big Game Fishing Club Ladies White Marlin Tournament. For both members and non-members. April 5-9. Bimini Big Game Fishing Club Invitational Tuna Tournament. International sportsmen go after speedy tuna.
May 24-28.
5th Annual Bahamas "500" Ocean Powerboat race. A test of endurance, the world's richest and longest power boat race starts, ends at Freeport, Grand Bahama—courses through Islands with stops for fuel only. June.
The Queen's Birthday. A public holiday, officially proclaimed the birthday of Her Majesty, Queen Elizabeth II. Ceremonial Parade at Fort Charlotte, Nassau. July.
