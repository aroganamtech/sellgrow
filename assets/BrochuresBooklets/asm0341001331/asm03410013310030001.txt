In 1969, the company ordered nine DC-10s at a cost of $160 million, including spares, and in 1971 ordered two intercontinental versions of the DC-10.
Four additional DC-10s were ordered in January 1973.
The first DC-10 service between New York and Miami,
Palm Beach and Tampa was introduced by National on December 15, 1971, and service expanded as additional new jets joined the fleet. In the winter of 1973-74, DC-10-30 service was inaugurated on the Miami-London route.
National completed its fleet standardization program in May 1976 with the sale of two B-747s. The airline's equipment was then streamlined to only two aircraft types -- DC-lOs and B-727s.
Ground was broken in 1968 for a $45 million expansion program by the Dade County Port Authority for National Airlines at Miami International Airport. The project was completed in 1974.
On July 25, 1969, the Atlanta-San Francisco nonstop route was awarded to National, and service was inaugurated on October 1, 1969.
A $17 million IBM electronic computer reservations system called Res-A-Vision was completed and put into operation in 1970.
National became the third U.S. transatlantic passenger carrier on June 15, 1970, with the inauguration of daily nonstop roundtrip service between Miami and London. This transatlantic route proved appealing to passengers who wished to avoid the congested gateways and inclement weather of northern airports. Service with intercontinental DC-10-30s was inaugurated on the route in the fall and winter of 1973/74.
Dudley Swim, Chairman of the Board, died in Carmel, California on January 31, 1972. Mr. Maytag was again appointed Chairman of the Board on February 11, 1972, in addition to his position as President and Chief Executive Officer.
