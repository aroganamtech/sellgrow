World War II with its tremendous demand to move military cargo overseas spurred the growth of air cargo, and the post-war period produced surplus aircraft that became air freighters. By 1959 Pan Am had the greatest cargo capacity of any airliner. In 1963, it introduced the first all -cargo jet plane, and its total cargo volume reached 195 million ton miles and was growing at the rate of more than 20 percent a year. By 1971, Pan Am was carrying 725 million ton miles of cargo a year, another record.
Juan T. Trippe, who led Pan Am and the industry from the infancy of aviation to the maturity of giant jets, retired in 1968 and was named Honorary Chairman. He was succeeded by Harold E. Gray, one of Pan Am's first pilots.
Mr. Gray retired prematurely in 1970 because of illness and Najeeb E. Halaby, former test pilot, lawyer and former Federal Aviation Administrator, became President and Chairman.
In 1972 Mr. Halaby resigned and the Board elected as Chairman and Chief Executive Officer William T. Seawell. Mr. Seawell, a retired Air Force Brigadier General, former executive of American Airlines and the Air Transport Association, and former President of the Rolls-Royce North American subsidiary, joined Pan Am in December 1971 as President and Chief Operating Officer. In January 1976, Forwood C. Wiser was elected President and Chief Operating Officer and Mr. Seawell retained the title of Chairman of the Board and Chief Executive.Officer. In April 1978, Mr. Wiser resigned and Dan A. Col ussy was elected President on May 9, 1978. Mr. Seawell‘s title remained the same.
