MENDOZA, capital of the province, is 650 miles from Buenos Aires. Its popidation is 110.180. The city has admirable plazas and promenades. Itself some 2460 feet above sea-level, it is situated in the foothills of the great Cordillera of the Andes, within sight of the snow covered peaks. The “garden of the Andes” is the name that the residents give to the town. The products of the gardens are: wine, grapes and fruit, have an increasing commercial importance.
SANTA FE, capital of the province, is 524 miles from Buenos Aires by the Paraná river. Boats leave Buenos Aires 3 times a week, arriving next day. There is also a daily train by the Central Argentine Railway; the distance by this route being about 300 miles. Population is 150.000. It is the centre of an exceptionally fertile region, and has two large docks for ocean going steamers.
ROSARIO, chief city of the Province of Santa Fe, 190 miles by rail from Buenos Aires, is on the Paraná river. The streets are wider than those of Buenos Aires and there are fine boulevards, and handsome open spaces. The urban population is 574.959. Rosario is reached in four hours by rail from Buenos Aires by the Central Argentine Railway.
TUCUMAN, a modern city of 167.822 inhabitants, is the capital of the province of the same name. It is the birthplace of Argentine independence. Here, in 1816 was declared the Act of Independence, when Argentina threw off the yoke of Spain. It is an old city, having been founded in 1564. The original house, where independence was declared, has been preserved and is in excellent condition. Tucumán is 735 miles from Buenos Aires. It can be reached in about 23 hours by Central Argentine Railway expresses. Tucumán is the centre of sugar trade in Argentina.
