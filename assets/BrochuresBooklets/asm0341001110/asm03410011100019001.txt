8TH DAY. FRIDAY:
SAN SALVADOR.
Morning sightseeing tour of the city’s highlights. Afternoon free.
San Salvador was founded in 1524 and its growth, while not spectacular, has been steady enough to make it the second largest city in Central America with a population reaching toward the halfmillion mark. Tour the principal monuments with your Pan Am’s World Host, including the church of La Merced. In the afternoon, shop or relax at the hotel swimming pool.
9TH DAY. SATURDAY:
SAN SALVADOR.
Full-day excursion to Lake Ilopango and the cultural center of Ilobasco, including lunch.
Lake Ilopango is just nine miles from the city. According to local legend the Indians used to sacrifice four virgins each year to solicit the good will of the spirits of the volcanoes and lakes. The largest lake in El Salvador, Ilopango fills a volcanic crater five by eight miles in area. In 1880 a volcano suddenly appeared in the center of the lake and rose to a height of 150 feet. Today the area is a resort popular for fishing, swimming or golf.
10TH DAY. SUNDAY:
SAN SALVADOR/GUATEMALA. Morning departure by motorcoach. Lunch en route and late afternoon arrival.
This trip is not just traveling, it’s a tour; first we visit Los Chorros, a paradise of natural beauty, continuing the drive to Santa Ana, the second largest city in El Salvador. After visiting the unique Cathedral we continue to the Tazumal ruins. Before we cross the frontier to Guatemala you will stop for lunch at a pleasant local restaurant. Cross the border into Guatemala and continue to your hotel.

12TH AND 13TH DAYS. GUATEMALA.
Two free days to relax or take optional tours.
You’ll want to see Antigua, just a 45-minute drive from Guatemala City. This beautiful restored colonial town was founded in 1541 by the Spanish Con-quistadores. It remained the splendid capital of the Kingdom of Guatemala and Central America until an earthquake ruined it entirely in 1773. The remains are a national monument. You can still see the ruins of magnificent colonial palaces and churches.
14TH DAY. THURSDAY: GUATEMALA.
Full day tour to Chichicastenango and Lake Atitlan.
Drive to the unspoiled Indian-colonial town in the Maya-Quiche highlands; and you’ll meet the hill Indians, on their way to market with their wares, all along the road. In the marketplace, Indian women in huipiles (embroidered blouses) and men in knee-britches with bright belts throng together, bargaining quietly for each other’s wares. You’ll want to bargain, yourself — all this is the place to pick up typically Guatemalan artifacts and woven goods. The wood carvings of Guatemala are particularly attractive, with a style all their own. Ponchos and silver beads at Chichicastenango are a good buy.
