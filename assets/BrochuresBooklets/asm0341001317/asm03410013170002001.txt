an aircraft is taking off for a regularly scheduled flight somewhere in the world.
Before it lands, it may well have spanned a continent or an ocean and crossed the borders of many countries.
Beneath it, frontiers vanish and the world shrinks.
For the people and the goods in it, distance exists only as a matter of time, and miles become fractions of a minute.
The web of service woven around the world each day by these thousands of flights constitutes one of mankind’s most effective tools for peace, prosperity and human understanding.
It represents as well man’s successful effort to make scientific discovery serve peaceful ends.
This miracle of modern flight has been possible in ten years since the end of the Second World War because men and women in many nations have joined in the common effort to make civil aviation a servant of all mankind.
