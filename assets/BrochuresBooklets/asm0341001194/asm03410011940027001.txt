YUGOSLAVIA
FOOD AND RESTAURANTS
Yugoslav food is based upon generous portions of meat with lots of onions, potatoes, and good coarse breads. Broiled meats on a skewer are tasty; cevapcici (meatballs barbecued long) and raznjici (hamburgers) are great with a piece of bread. Savory stews are rich, the egg-and-veal ragout teleca corba, or djuvec, a delicious mixture of eggplant, carrot, potato, rice, and meat with a grated cheese topping. Punjene tikvice (stuffed zucchini) and stuffed cabbage are tasty with tomato sauces. Pohovani kackavalj is a delicious slice of fried matured cheese. Also try bosanski lonac, a spicy stew of cabbage, zeljanica, a spinach-egg-and-cheese pastry, as well as gibanica, a rich savory cottage cheese and egg pie, and cornpone with kajmak. The seafoods along the Dalmatian coast are excellent, and the oysters around the Limski Canal and Ston are exceptional. Yugoslav cheeses and sausages are strong and tasty. While in Belgrade visit the many restaurants with roving folk and gypsy groups in Skadarlija, and sample the Serbian cuisine at Ima Dana (Skadarska 38— tel: 334 422), which specializes in game and fish, and Tri Sesira (Skadarska 29—tel: 347 501) with its quaint authenticity or Skadarlija, (Cetinjaska No. 17—tel: 334983). Dva Ribara (NarodnogFronta21—tel: 328 807) serves excellent fish dishes; in the warm season, Stadion (Humska 1—tel: 648 179) Graficar, (Vase Pelagica No 31—tel: 651 467) Milosev Konak, (Toplider No 1, tel: 680 146), Seher, (Andre Nikolifca No 3, tel: 651 726) all have open-air grills and music and dancing; Park (Pariska No 20—tel: 621 636), and London (Marshal Tito No28—tel: 642 969) all have their Serbian specialties. Dom Lovaca (Njuegoseva36—tel: 436 128) serves game; Dosanov Grad (Terazije4—tel: 321 986), Beograd-janka (Masarikova ul.—tel: 684 651), andDunavskiCvet (TadeushaKos-cuscog 65—tel: 625 985) serve fine Yugoslav and international cuisine in contemporary settings, and Romani Tar (Teraziji Square—tel: 333280) is good for gypsy specialties and dancing. In summer you can dine aboard a Danube cruise boat; ask at your hotel. For less expensive meals, self-service centers can be found all over the city. Some of these are Kasina, Luksur, Studentski Park, and Zagreb, Kalemegdan Castle has a little coffee shop overlooking the broad confluence of the Sava and Danube Rivers.
DRINKING TIPS
Yugoslavia produces excellent wines at relatively tow cost. The bottle ranges from about 30-60D. White wines to try are Ljutomer, Sijlvanec, Traminec Rebula, and Tokaj from Slovenia, Kutjevo, Belie and Maras-tina from Croatia, Zilavka and Blatina from Mostar; good reds are Barbera and Merlot from Slovenia and the strong and pungent reds Dingac, Posiop, and Grk from Dalmatia. Try Yugoslavia’s famous plum brandy SIjivovica (very potent). Maraschino, a liqueur, is tasty, and you will like Yugoslav beer. Drinking hours are not specifically regulated.
