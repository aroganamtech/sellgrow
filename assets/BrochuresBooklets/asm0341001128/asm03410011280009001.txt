20 YEARS OF LEADERSHIP
Pan American’s long record of achievement began in 1927. The first flight was made between Key West, Florida and Havana. Pan American “cut its teeth” on over-water flying and held the record in 1930 by regularly flying the longest over-water route in existence. This was between Kingston, Jamaica and the Panama Canal Zone by way of Barranquilla, Colombia... a non-stop flight of over 600 miles across the Caribbean Sea.
Today, Flying Clipper routes are world-wide. After extending its airlanes through South America, Pan American was first to establish regular trans-Pacific service in 1935. During 1939, Pan American was first to introduce regular trans-Atlantic service which included sleeper accommodations, connecting Europe with the United States. Subsequent inauguration of many additional routes in Europe, the Near East, Asia, Africa and Australasia was climaxed when Pan American began the first singlecarrier, ’round-the-world air service east from New York and west from San Francisco.
