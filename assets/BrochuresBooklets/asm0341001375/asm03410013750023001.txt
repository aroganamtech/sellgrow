SLIPPER SOCKS AND EYE MASKS: These items are distributed, as a matter of course, in first class sections, but a lot of travelers usually carry their own. The socks have two advantages: first, they will help keep your feet warm; second, they are comfortable substitutes for shoes in moving about the cabin. The eye masks are something else. If you’re used to wearing a pair at home for keeping out the light, you’re in good shape. If you’ve never worn a pair, you may find that the mask feels strange enough to hinder your attempts to rest or to sleep. Try the mask, but if it’s not going to work, get rid of it. If it helps, take it with you and use it for the rest of your trip.
STEREO: Listening to music on an airplane, or using stereo to pick up the sound track of a movie, is great fun, but it may have limits in aiding rest. If you use music as a sleep inducer at home, the stereo on the airplane may do it for you. But, if you’re not a music-user, there’s no reason to expect Pan Am’s choices to perform magic, and either way, the earphones may have something of the same feeling for you as the eye mask. They’re not part of your regular sleep pattern. You might also want to try Pan Am’s special “sleep music,” available on most stereo-equipped airplanes. Ear muffs, plugs, etc. may help if the music doesn’t, particularly if you are used to them, but we try to keep the background noise level in the cabin at a steady, reassuring level.
