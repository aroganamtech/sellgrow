As part of its total involvement in transportation, Pan Am over the two generations of its 100 million airborne patrons, has continuously studied the problems of across-the-time-zones travel. After the experience of 12,000 round-the-world flights, one of the airline’s more important commitments has been the sponsorship of the first interdisciplinary conference on the subject, by the International Society for the Study of Time. This meeting brought together some of the world’s leading specialists in the study of biological and psychological rhythms, as well as social psychologists, anthropologists and even astronomers concerned with this problem. Scientists and medical men from the great American and European universities and research institutions tried to define exactly the problems of flight desynchronization; that is, the ways in which an individual’s personal rhythm adjusts to the rhythm of a different environment, how man adjusts to rapid transmeridian transit.
The symposium, organized and conducted by Dr. J. T. Fraser of the University of Maryland, one of the world’s leading experts on the study of time, produced no “instant answers,” nor came up with any formulas for magic potions. Instead it pointed some directions that future research must take if we are to determine the best ways of making our “circadian rhythms”—the 24-hour cycle of man’s clocks—mesh with the circadian rhythm of the environment encountered after an inter- or transcontinental flight.
Pan Am is committed to sharing this follow-on research. Meanwhile, this booklet offers in simple terms some of the ideas in the papers written for that symposium—because Pan Am cares for your safety, health, and comfort.
