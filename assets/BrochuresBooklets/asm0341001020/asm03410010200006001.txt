Mandalay... About 350 miles north of Rangoon by railroad (529 miles by boat) on the Irrawaddy River is Mandalay, the last Burmese capital before the British Annexation of 1885. Mandalay Palace and fort, Mandalay Hill and pagoda, the Arakanese Pagoda and the silk-weaving industry of Mandalay are features of interest. Also the Mingun Bell, weighing 100 tons and ranked second only to the Moscow Bell. Air flight to Mandalay is just over 2 hours.
Pagan. . . About 400 miles north of Rangoon on the Irrawaddy River are the ruins of the eleventh-century city of Pagan, an area of about 16 square miles covered with well-preserved and awe-inspiring pagodas and temples. Known as the city of pagodas (4,486,733), this ancient capital of Burma contains most of the relics of the first period of Burmese greatness (1044-1087 a.o. ). It’s considered one of the richest archaeological sites in the Indo-China peninsula.

Other points of sightseeing interest are: the lacquerware factories near Prome on the Irrawaddy River, 110 miles north of Rangoon; Mawchi Mines (wolfram, tungsten and tin); Mogok, gem center of Burma, producing the finest quality of rubies and sapphires in the world; and the teak sawmills in the picturesque rural and seaport town of Moulmein, where elephants are employed to fell timber.
