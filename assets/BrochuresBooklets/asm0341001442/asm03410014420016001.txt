No doubt you’ve wondered what a water landing feels like. The aircraft’s belly will hit the water first and you’ll feel a slight jar...then the nose and wing sections will hit with a greater jolt of about the intensity a navy pilot feels on routine carrier landings.
As soon as the plane stops its forward motion, open your seat belt and follow the next instructions of your crew members. Don’t hurry. There’s plenty of time—and there are eleven exits in your cabin.
After you are out of the aircraft pull the two small knobs at the base of your life jacket. They will sud-
denly inflate. See, now, why the Navy calls them Mae Wests?
The captain, co-pilot, flight engineer, purser and steward will be in charge of the five rafts.
Your sea-going raft has adjustable sides and a sun shade to protect you from sunburn and spray.
During the late war some army and navy fliers ditched their aircraft as many as five times, without injury. It almost got to be a habit.. . really not so dangerous as you might think, but Pan American wanted you to know.

The World’s most experienced Airline—
Since 1927, Flying Clippers have carried approximately 9/4 million commercial passengers. Excluding its hundreds of thousands of route miles flown during the war for the U. S. Army and Navy, PAA has flown 700 million miles—a record unequaled by any
