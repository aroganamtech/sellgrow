LOCATION . . . Approximately 670 miles southeast of New York, with the Gulf Stream running between Bermuda and the United States.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . Bermuda, “Where it is Always Spring,” is a place for tourists to enjoy bicycling, tennis, golf, swimming, sailing, fishing and relaxing in an atmosphere of semi-tropical beauty. While one can dance after dinner at some of the hotels, there is no “night club life.”
POPULATION . . . 37,254 according to the 1950 census, of which approximately 13,500 are white and 23,500 are colored.
SIZE . . . The total area of the more than 150 islands which make up “Bermuda” is about 22 square miles.
CAPITAL ... Hamilton.
GOVERNMENT . . . The government of Bermuda is a system analogous to that of Great Britain. Since 1685, the Governor has been appointed by the British Crown to represent the King. It is a misnomer, however, to speak of Bermuda as a Crown colony. Politically, these islands hold a unique place among the possessions of the Crown. Bermuda has its own elected House of Assembly and a Legislative Council, and is officially designated as the “British Colony of Bermuda.”
HOW TO GET THERE ... by Pan American By ship from New York: 40 hours.

ACCOMMODATIONS . . . One of the charms of Bermuda is that you can find almost any type of accommodation you wish, from luxurious hotels with swimming pools and tennis courts to charming quiet guest houses with limited numbers of guests. Rooms in private homes are also available.
Among the luxury hotels are:
Belmont Manor in Warwick Parish opposite the city of Hamilton, overlooking the Great Sound. Tennis court, swimming pool, golf course. Rates: $11 to $20 American plan.
Castle Harbour in Tucker’s Town. The last word in luxury, it has just been re-opened. Swimming pool, tennis court, skeet shooting, fishing. Dancing nightly. Accommodates 500. Rates: $14 to $24 American plan.
Elbow Beach Surf Club the only hotel located on its own beach. Gay social spot with dancing nightly, social directors, group activity. Each room with bath. Accommodates 320. Rates: $12 to $18 modified American plan (breakfast and dinner only).
Princess Hotel and Cottages in Pembroke on a promontory between Hamilton Harbour and Pitt’s Bay. One of the oldest and most conservative hotels. Recently remodelled—all rooms have baths. Swimming pool, tennis courts, dancing. It has its own beach club on the south shore. Accommodates 450. Rates: $12 to $18.
St. George Hotel in St. George’s is quiet and remote from Hamilton. Swimming pool, golf and beach facilities for guests. Accommodates 200. Rates: $8 to $16 American plan.
Among the smaller hotels:
Harmony Hall in Paget accommodates 65 guests. Rates: $10 to $20 American plan.
Inverurie at Darrell's Wharf accommodates 90 guests. Rates: $10 to $16 American plan. Some cottage suites.
Eagle's Nest in Pembroke accommodates 54 guests. Rates: $12 to $16 American plan. All rooms with bath.

Bermuda abounds in guest houses which offer the same service as hotels but provide a quieter, more leisurely vacation. Most of them limit their guests to 50. Rates are about the same as hotels. Most are American plan.
Cambridge Beaches in Somerset—small cottages and central dining hall. All rooms with baths. Rates: $14-$22 American plan.
The Horizons in Paget near Coral Beach, main guest house and cottages. All rooms have baths. Rates: $14-$18 American plan.
Pomander Gate in Paget, old Bermuda mansion with quiet formal charm. Swim from dock in Hamilton Harbour, or cycle to beaches on south shore. Rates: $12-$ 17.50, American plan.
These are typical of the guest houses available. There are scores of others.
BANKS. ..Bank of Bermuda; Bank of N.T. Butterfield & Son.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . New Year’s Day, Good Friday, Empire Day (May 24), the King’s Birthday (celebrated on June 7), Somers Day (July 28), Cup Match Day (day before Somers Day), Remembrance Day (Armistice Day), Christmas, Boxing Day (December 26).
CIGARETTES AND TOBACCO . . . American cigarettes are available but are more expensive. Tourists are allowed to bring in one carton. English tobacco, pipes and cigarettes are available in good supply.
CLIMATE . . . With the exception of a few chill weeks in winter, Bermuda has a spring-like climate. Easter is a very popular season, when the lilies are in bloom; but it is a balmy spot the year round. There are few all-rainy days, but there are occasional quick, unexpected showers.
CLUBS . . . American Legion, American Veterans of Foreign Wars, Bermuda Bridge Club (visitors may join for a small fee), English-speaking Union, Garden Club, Lions Club, Rotary Club.
