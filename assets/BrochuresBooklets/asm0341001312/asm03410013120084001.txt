Paradise Beach
LOCATION . . . The Bahamas comprise a chain of hundreds of small islands, cays and reefs lying off the Florida coast and north of Cuba.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . Nassau is typically British, very formal and utterly lovely. Spend your days lying on magnificent Paradise Beach which is famous the world over, and your nights dancing under the Bahaman moon on the terrace of one of the island’s luxurious hotels. A wonderful vacation spot where tourists are no novelty and where everyone goes all out, British fashion, to make them comfortable.
POPULATION... Estimated at 80,000.
SIZE . . . The total land area of the group is 4,404 square miles, about half the area of Massachusetts.
CAPITAL... Nassau, on the island of New Providence; population about 35,000.
GOVERNMENT... A British colony with a Governor representing the King.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper, Nassau is only 55 minutes flying time from Miami, four hours longer from New York. By ship, overnight from Miami.
NASSAU
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . Nassau has some of the most luxurious and beautiful hotels in the Islands. The most famous of them all, the British Colonial Hotel on Bay Street, rates in the winter about $24 to $36, single; $36 to $50, double, American plan; Royal Victoria Hotel, $11 to $13 per day, single, European plan; Prince George, $8 to $10, European plan; the Windsor, rates $8 to $13, European plan. Others are the Carlton House; the Balmoral Club and the Fort Montagu Beach Hotel, which has tennis courts, private beach, croquet and special entertainment. All rates are lower in the summer months. In Nassau there are also many guest houses, including Buena Vista and Cumberland House. There are several residential clubs, too.
ARTS. . . There are no art museums in Nassau. Every so often there are art exhibits of the works of natives and visitors.
BALLET .. • None.
BANKS .. . Royal Bank of Canada, Barclay’s Bank.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS ... New Year’s Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Whit Monday (day following Whit Sunday), Empire Day (May 24th), King’s Birthday (June 10th), August Bank Holiday (first Monday), Columbus Day (October 12th), Christmas Day, Boxing Day (December 26th).
CLIMATE . . . While it is almost always warm and sunny, some evenings are cool. Nassau is pleasant to visit the year around, but the social season is from Christmas to Easter.
CLUBS . • • Bahamian Club, Bahamas Country Club, Nassau Yacht Club, Royal Nassau Sailing Club, Emerald Beach Club, Porcupine Club. For golf and tennis clubs, see sports.
(COMMON COURTESIES AND LOCAL CUSTOMS. .. Don’t wear shorts or backless dresses any place but the beach. Nassau is very conservative.
COMMUNICATIONS .. . Radio Telephone Service, Radio Teletype System. Airmail (Rate to U. S. A.—6d. or 7c per half ounce.)
CURRENCY. . . The British pound, worth about $2.80 in United States currency.
CUSTOMS REGULATIONS AND DOCUMENTS REQUIRED FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS . . . No passports or visa required. You may take in 200 cigarettes, 50 cigars or one pound of tobacco, and one quart of liquor. You may
take in as much United States money as you want.
DRUG STORES . . . Very modern.
FAUNA. . . Raccoon on New Providence; flamingos, parrots, wild ducks and pigeons, wild hogs, wild horses and cattle and iguana on some of the out islands, particularly on Andros and Inagua islands.
FLORA . . . Radiant hibiscus, oleander, bougainvillea, poin-ciana and other exotic flowers.
FOOD . . . With the exception of vegetables and fish, most food is imported from other countries. Meat from the United States, Canada and Australia. Native foods include Conch chowder, turtle soup, turtle pie, and wonderful coconut ice cream.
GAMBLING . . . Horse racing, and there is a casino at the Bahamian Club.
LANGUAGE ... English, of course.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING FACILITIES . . . Excellent.
LIQUOR . .. Everything and much cheaper than at home.
MEDICAL FACILITIES . . . There’s a hospital and excellent doctors.
MOTION PICTURES...The Savoy where they show pictures sometimes that haven’t even been shown in the United States.
MUSIC . . . Concerts on the green once a week.
NIGHTCLUBS AND CABARETS . . . Jungle Club at the Fort Montagu Beach Hotel, the Spiderweb, Silver Slipper and Zanzibar.
PAN AMERICAN’S OFFICE . . . Mathew Avenue.
RELIGION . . . Twenty-eight denominations.
RESTAURANTS. . . There are few restaurants as such in Nassau; the hotel dining rooms have wonderful food, and even though you are not staying there you should arrange to have a meal at Buena Vista and at Cumberland House (guest houses). Their food is excellent. There are two restaurants, the City Garden Club and Grand Central which is air-conditioned, also the Candy Kitchen and the Elbo Room with American soda fountains.
SHOPS AND STORES. . . Nassau has wonderful shops where you can buy almost anything. Among them are: W. J. Saunders, Stewart, Ltd., Ambrosine, The City Gift Shop, Barry’s, The Park Store, the Men’s Shop, Treasure Traders, The Island Bookshop. All places of business are closed Friday afternoons.
