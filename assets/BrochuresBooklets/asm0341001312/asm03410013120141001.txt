LOCATION . . . Bolivia, near the center of South America is the only country except Paraguay without a seacoast. While much of the country consists of low plains which are humid and hot, the western section where the principal cities are located, is a large plateau with an altitude of 12,000—more than twice the altitude of Denver.
CHARACTERISTICS ... La Paz is the highest capital in the world. It is over 12,000 feet above sea level. The city itself is in the bottom of a deep narrow gully entirely surrounded by mountains. It is a city of steep, steep streets with houses built on inclined surfaces. The airport is on a plateau high above the city.
POPULATION . . . Estimated 3,922,000 in 1948, the population is about the same as that of Chicago and Tacoma combined.
SIZE... Estimated 500,000 square miles, about the size of Texas and California combined.
CAPITAL... La Paz with about 350,000 inhabitants, slightly larger than Rochester, N. Y.
GOVERNMENT ... A republic with a President, a Senate and a House of Deputies.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American and Panagra to La Paz, 15 hours (elapsed time) from Miami; 21 hours from New York. By Panagra 3y2 hours from Lima, Peru. Cochabamba is only about an hour’s flying time from La Paz. By ship from New York, 14 days to Mollendo, Peru; then about 46 hours by rail and lake steamer to La Paz.
LA PAZ
ACCOMMODATIONS. . . There are not many hotels in La Paz. Best one is Sucre Palace where rates start at $2.25 single European plan and go up. It is advisable to get confirmed reservations in advance. More modest are the Austria, the Paris and the Nacional in the suburb of Obrajes. There are also several pensions such as the Pension Cuellar.
ARTS.. .The Tiahuanaco Museum houses a significant collection of pre-Inca relics and is well worth a visit. Check for hours.
BALLET . . . The National School of Ballet of Mrs. Valentina Romanoff. Also visiting companies.
BANKS . . . The Panagra office should be consulted before changing money. Banco Central de Bolivia is official issuing bank; Banco Nacional de Bolivia; Banco Popular de Peru.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS.. . New Year’s Day; Carnival, usually in February; Easter; Labor Day, May 1; Corpus Christi in June; National Festival, August 5, 6, 7; National Anniversary of the Discovery of America, October 12; All Saints Day, November 1, 2; Christmas Day; various local holidays.
CIGARETTES, CIGARS, PIPES, TOBACCO.. There are American cigarettes available in a few places. The tourist may bring 100 cigarettes or 20 cigars or 2 pipes and 2 tins tobacco.
CLIMATE . . . Foreigners find the high altitude very difficult for the first few days. They are advised to walk as little as possible and to hire cabs for any destination. The weather is cool all the year around and cold in Winter. Even in Summer you need blankets at night.
CLUBS . . . Anglo-American Club; Casa Argentina, Círculo Francés and Círculo Perruano. Club de La Paz, Club Tennis La Paz, Club Tennis Sucre, Club de Caza y Pezca, Yacht Club, Rotary International, Lion’s Club, Automóvil Club, Club 16 de Julio, Club Arabe, Club Social Chileno.
COMMUNICATIONS ... All American cables, Western Union maintains an office on Calle Socabaya. There is also West Coast Cables office. By CIRBOL you can have long distance communications. ■
CURRENCY... The Boliviano is the currency unit. It is worth about 100 Bs to the U. S. dollar, but varies.
CUSTOM REGULATIONS FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS . . . Passport necessary, but no visa for tourist visits up to 90 days. Personal effects such as cameras and typewriters are allowed, but get permits when entering. For all other articles, passengers must secure special permission. One bottle of liquor allowed duty free.
DOCUMENTS REQUIRED . . . Passport. Health and inoculation certificates.
DRUG STORES . . . Almost all the drug stores have American products. All prescriptions can be filled.
FAUNA. . . Llama, vicuna and alpaca on the plateau; fresh water fish near Cochabamba; wolf, American tigers, bears and game birds in the forests; monkeys, parrots, crocodiles, caimanes in the tropical regions.
FLORA. . . The vegetation of the valleys of Bolivia is very beautiful. There are also forests in the Orient of Bolivia.
FOOD . . . Truly native food is highly seasoned with a really hot pepper. One of the favorite dishes is chuno, frozen potatoes mixed with meat, fish or eggs. Empanadas, native meat pies, are highly seasoned so be careful. Fish is served well. And try picantes, bits of chicken cooked in pungent sauce. You can find more cosmopolitan food in the hotels and some of the restaurants.
LANGUAGE. . . Spanish; but in the better hotels, restaurants and shops, English is spoken and understood.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING. . . “La Nevada’’ and “Pamba” are among the best, where all clothes can be very well cleaned or dyed. Very fast service.
LIQUOR...There are native red and white wines which are not so good as those produced in Chile. Most liquors are high because of the import duty.
MEDICAL FACILITIES ... The American Clinic is one of the best, with a North American director.
MOTION PICTURES . . . Municipal Theatre, Monje Cam-pero, Tesla, La Paz, Miraflores, Avenida, and others.
