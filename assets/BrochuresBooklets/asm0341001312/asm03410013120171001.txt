LOCATION . . . The British possession of Hong Kong comprises Hong Kong Island, Kowloon Peninsula and New Territories in southeast China, about 90 miles south of Canton. Hong Kong is 700 air miles northwest of Manila.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . This British Crown Colony is one of the busiest ports in the Orient. It olfers the traveler a chance to see an interesting and colorful Chinese city. Macao Colony of Portugal, easily accessible from Hong Kong, provides an interesting experience, too.
POPULATION... Estimated 2,500,000 somewhat larger than the population of Philadelphia.
SIZE . . . The island of Hong Kong is 11 miles long with an area of 32 square miles. The total area of the colony is 391 square miles.
CAPITAL . . . Victoria, often referred to as Hong Kong, with a population of about 365,000, slightly smaller than Portland, Oregon.
GOVERNMENT... A British Crown Colony, the king represented by a governor.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper from the United States West Coast via Honolulu about 57 hours (elapsed time), 4 hours from Manila, 5 hours from Bangkok. Hong Kong is on Pan American Round-the-World Route. By ship about 24 days from San Francisco.

ACCOMMODATIONS . . . There are several fine hotels in Hong Kong, including: the Hong Kong Hotel. It’s still the place to meet your friends in the lobby. The Peninsula, Repulse Bay, Gloucester, Metropole, Miramar, Sunning House, International, Winner House, Paramount, Waldorf and Cathay. Rates range from $3 to $6 single, $4 to $8 double. Suites from $8 to $12, all European plan. Make your reservations in advance.
ARTS.. .No museums. Frequent public showings by Chinese artists and private collectors. Local arts and crafts on display in Chinese shops.
BALLET . . . None.
BANKS. . . The Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corporations, Dao Heng Bank, Cheuk Kee Bank, Bank of China, National City Bank of New York, Chartered Bank of India, Australia and China.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS ... The Chinese New Year’s is a colorful event. Others include: Good Friday and the day following; Easter Monday; Whit Monday; King’s Birthday (celebrated in June); the first week-day in July; the first Monday in August; Liberation Day; Chinese Mid-Autumn Festival Day; The Monday nearest the Tenth of October (Chinese Festival Day); the day following Remembrance Sunday, Christmas day.
CIGARETTES . . . British and American cigarettes are readily available at prices no higher than in the United States.
CLIMATE.. .The best season is from September 15 through March 1. Worst months are from the end of June through August.
CLUBS. . . The Hong Kong Club, The Jockey Club, the American Club, Rotary International, Junior Chamber of Commerce, Chinese Bankers Club, the Cricket Club and the Ladies Recreation Club. Cards may be arranged for most of these.
COMMON COURTESIES AND LOCAL CUSTOMS . . . About the same as at home.
COMMUNICATIONS . . . Cables to all parts of the world. Local telephone system extensive. Long distance available to: United Kingdom, United States, Philippines, Taipeh (Formosa),
Singapore, Canton, Macao, Australia, Canada, Shanghai, Mexico, Cuba and Japan.
CURRENCY. . . The monetary unit is the Hong Kong Dollar. The official rate of exchange is HK $5.80 to United States $1.00; however, all United States dollars are exchanged at the recognized open market rate which fluctuates drastically at times.
CUSTOM REGULATIONS FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS . . . Passports and visas required. Each passenger is allowed 200 cigarettes, 4 ounces of tobacco and one quart bottle of liquor duty free. No other restrictions.
DRUG STORES . . . Chemists shops are numerous and have most recognized United States pharmaceuticals.
FOOD . . . Chinese food is famous the world over. Some of the specialties include such delicacies as shark’s fin, birds’ nest soup and buried eggs, pressed duck and boneless chicken, pork served with sweet and sour sauce, shrimps, crabs, lobsters and, of course, rice and soya beans. The old fashioned Chinese usually have just two meals a day. However, the younger generation have the same eating customs as we do. You can also get Continental cuisine in Hong Kong.
HORSE RACING. . . There is a wonderful race track at Happy Valley with pari-mutuel betting. Spring and fall racing seasons.
LANGUAGE . . . Local Chinese speak Cantonese dialect. A person can get by everywhere with only English.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING FACILITIES . . . Laundry is good. Dry cleaning is satisfactory, but should be watched carefully.
LIQUOR . . . The Chinese have some wonderful native liqueurs such as Mui Quai Low, Ng Gar Pai, Sam Ching and Lor Mai Tsao. There are Scotch, gin, wine and liquors from the United Kingdom, Bourbon from the United States, wines and liqueurs from France and Portugal. Prices are reasonable.
MEDICAL FACILITIES . . . Excellent. The University of Hong Kong has a fine medical school.
