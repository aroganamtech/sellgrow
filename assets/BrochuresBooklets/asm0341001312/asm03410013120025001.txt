LOCATION . . . England and Wales occupy the southern part of the British Isles. Northern Ireland comprises the six northeastern counties of the island to the west of Great Britain.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . England, with all its differences is the closest thing to home you’ll find abroad. This is mainly because of the language. Don’t let the rumors of austerity keep you from going. The average tourist sees nothing of this. The English, though reserved, are friendly, unfailingly courteous and their country is filled with history. It is one of the few remaining countries in which royalty is respected and that in itself breeds a tradition and dignity not found elsewhere. London has magnificence and the English countryside is utterly charming. There are places of historic and cultural interest from one end of the “tight little isle” to the other. You’ll have fun.
POPULATION . . . The latest estimated population of England and Wales is 43,297,000, a couple of million more than that of France. Northern Ireland has a population of 1,380,000.
SIZE . . . The area of England is 50,874 square miles, about equal in size to Alabama. Wales with 7,466 square miles is the size of New Jersey. Northern Ireland with 5,238 square miles is a little larger than Connecticut.
CAPITAL . . . London, the largest city in the world with an estimated population of 8,349,870, the population of New York plus Cincinnati. Belfast (population 448,000) is the capital of Northern Ireland.
GOVERNMENT . . . The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland with the Crown Colonies, of course, form a constitutional monarchy with executive power held by the Prime Minister who must be supported by a majority of the House of Commons. Parliament consists of two Chambers: the House of Commons whose members are elected directly by the people, and the House of Lords whose members sit for life, either by hereditary right, by appointment by the King or by virtue of office.

LONDON
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . Regardless of rationing and shortages, no amount of austerity has been able to cut down the friendly service and pleasant atmosphere of London’s fine hotels. Rates vary considerably with the average around $3.75 a night without breakfast. Here are some of the best hotels in different price brackets. Prices quoted are for single rooms with bath. Claridge’s, still the swankiest hostelry in town, is $10 up. The rooms are large and gracious and you’ll be treated royally. The Savoy, of course, is known the world over as is the Ritz. The fashionable Berkeley is equally fine. Rooms are $8.50 a night. The Dorchester is very good. Rates here begin at $7.70. The Grosvenor House is very popular and costs about $7.50 per day. Brown’s Hotel hasn’t changed in 100 years, chintz chairs, atmosphere and quite moderate at $7.70. The Strand Palace, the Regent Palace and the Cumberland are pleasant and inexpensive. Rates beginning at $3.00 to $4.00. Other good hotels include the Hyde Park Hotel, beginning at $4.50, the May Fair Hotel, beginning at $7.00 and the Park Lane Hotel, beginning at $4.50.
ARTS . . . London offers a wealth of museum and gallery-visiting experiences. There is the British Museum with its vast collection of the art of all ages, including some famous must-be-seens. Elgin Marbles from Greece, manuscripts of the Magna Carta and some of Shakespeare’s First Folio and important anthropological exhibits. The National Gallery in Trafalgar Square is a must for all art lovers. It has one of the world’s most brilliant collections of French, Italian, Spanish, Flemish and English painting. The Tate Gallery, Millbank, offers an excellent collection of modern art from Turner on. The National Portrait
Gallery in St. Martin’s Place presents portraits of Britain’s great by artists of their times. Besides the large galleries and museums, London has a number of charming, intimate museums that were the houses of great men of letters and public life in England’s colorful history. Visiting Carlyle House in Chelsea, Keats’s House in Keats Grove or Dickens’s home on Doughty Street is like going back to a place in time.
Galleries and museums in London are open from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily and from 2 to 6:30 p.m. on Sundays. Admission is free most places.
BALLET . . . Though comparatively young in the dance world, London’s Sadler’s Wells Ballet Company has gained sensational success and reputation both at home and abroad. Their repertoire includes both classic and modern ballets. During the winter they give performances at the Covent Garden Opera House. Many foreign ballet and dance groups may also be seen in London.

CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . British national holidays include the Bank Holidays on Good Friday, Easter Monday, Whitsuntide and the first Monday in August; Christmas Day; Boxing Day on the 26th of December. The King’s Birthday celebrated in June is a festive occasion.
