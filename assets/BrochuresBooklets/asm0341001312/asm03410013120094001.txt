LOCATION . . . The island of Jamaica lies in the Caribbean Sea about 90 miles south of Cuba. Kingston is 589 air miles from Miami.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . Long famous as a resort, Jamaica has lots to offer the tourist. Kingston is colorful and comfortable with fine shops, wonderful drives and good hotels. The North coast which includes such well-known resorts as Montego Bay, where the International set is to be found during the season, has hotels which are super-luxurious, white coral beaches which are lapped by cerulean blue waters which are always warm, golf courses, fishing, yacht clubs and all the other things that give glamour and luxury to a vacation. If you want to escape all the gaiety, you may do this, too, by going to one of the lesser known but no less delightful resorts along the North coast. Jamaica is British and therefore fairly formal, but it truly has something for everyone.
POPULATION ... Estimated at 1,340,000 of which about 15,000 are white.
SIZE . . . Largest of the British West Indies, Jamaica is 145 miles long, has an area of 4,450 square miles, smaller than Connecticut.
CAPITAL ... Kingston with a population of approximately 120,000.
GOVERNMENT... A British colony with a governor appointed by the Crown.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper, from Miami via Camaguey, Cuba to Montego Bay, 3 hours and 15 minutes (elapsed time); total elapsed time to Kingston, 3 hours, 10 minutes. Non-stop by air from New York to Kingston, 6 hours. No regular service by ship.
KINGSTON
ACCOMMODATIONS... The Myrtle Bank is the swankiest hotel in Kingston, the capital of Jamaica. It has its own swimming pool. Rates about $11 to $15 per day. The South Camp and the Melrose are less expensive. In the Parish of St. Andrew a suburb of Kingston (a part of the Blue Mountains) there is the Manor House which has an adjoining golf course. Rates from about $6 to $15 per day. The Mona and Courtleigh Manor on Trafalgar Road. Rates about $12 to $15.
ARTS...The Institute of Jamaica, which contains the Science Museum and Library, the History, Art and Exhibition Galleries and Lecture Hall.
BALLET. . . None.
BANKS.. . Barclay’s Bank, Harbour Street; Royal Bank of Canada, King Street; Canadian Bank of Commerce, Harbour Street and Bank of Nova Scotia, King Street.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS. . . New Year’s Day, Ash Wednesday, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Empire Day (May 24), The King’s Birthday (June 7), Emancipation Day (August 1st), Constitution Day, Christmas, Boxing Day (December 26th).
CIGARETTES, CIGARS. . . The manufacture of cigars and cigarettes is one of the leading industries in Jamaica. But you can buy American cigarettes at all hotels.
CLIMATE. . . The “high season” mid-December to mid-April is absolutely perfect, free from rain. Sunshine every day and cool nights. The summer season—June to October has recently been “discovered” by United States tourists, with the result that this season almost equals the winter season in popularity. The constant trade winds keep the island far cooler than any resort on the United States seaboard and, of course, cooler than the inland resorts in the United States. Cheap hotel rates are available and summer excursion fares.
CLUBS. . . The Royal Jamaica Yacht Club, many golf clubs. See sports. Others are the Jamaica Club, St. Andrew’s, Liguanea
Club, and Jamaica Club. There is a Y.M.C.A. and a Y.W.C.A.
COMMON COURTESIES AND LOCAL CUSTOMS. . . Jamaica being British, is on the conservative and formal side.
COMMUNICATIONS. . . Telephone, cables, radio and airmail.
CURRENCY. . . The British pound, shilling and pence. The pound is worth about $2.80.
CUSTOMS REGULATIONS AND DOCUMENTS REQUIRED FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS... No passports or visas required for a stay up to six months, providing you have a return ticket. Visitors are given a special tourist card on arrival which must be handed back to Immigration Authorities on departure. All funds must be declared on arrival. No household pets are allowed.
DRUG STORES... Good.
FAUNA. . . Red tailed buzzard, hawks, parakeets, Blue Mountain duck, in all there are about 52 species of birds in Jamaica.
FLORA. . . Jamaica produces a great variety of plant life, wonderful fruits obtainable all seasons of the year, beautiful trees, shrubs, flowering plants, ferns and exquisite orchids, the silk cotton tree, or ceiba, the poinciana.
FOOD. . . Mutton is one of the popular meats on the island. There is a great variety of locally produced beef and pork and vegetables. The residents of the island eat large quantities of fish. Milk is produced in large quantities.
GAMBLING. . . There is horse racing under the auspices of the Jamaica Jockey Club, at Knutsford Park Race Course, Kingston. Thirty-six meets a year, including 3 sweepstakes. All betting is pari-mutuel.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING. . . Laundry is good. Hotels have 24-hour service. Numerous excellent dry cleaning establishments with 24-hour service.
