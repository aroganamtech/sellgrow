LOCATION . . . The most southerly of the Windward Islands group, Grenada is only 109 air miles from Trinidad.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . This little British island is called the spice island because of the cocoa, nutmeg and clove that grow here. From end to end the island is twenty-one miles long, but in this small area there is a great variety of scenery. St. George’s lies on a point of land, and the streets rise rather sharply from the bay. Its red-roofed houses are pale pink and green, the effect charming. There are mountains, valleys, rockbound coasts where the surf thunders, a calm harbor where the water is deep blue and quiet. And 2000 feet above sea level is Grand Etang, a volcanic lake. This is a tropical isle, quiet, peaceful with a rainy and a dry season. The hot days are tempered by trade winds; the swimming is superb, the whole place utterly beautiful and peaceful and just made for the traveler who wants to rest in sunshine. Some of the most beautiful white sand beaches in the world are here.
POPULATION... Estimated 90,586, mostly negroes of African descent.
SIZE . . . Oval in shape, the island is 21 miles long, 12 miles wide at its maximum and has an area of 120 square miles.
CAPITAL ... St. George’s (population about 10,000) also serves as capital for the Grenadines, Dominica, St. Vincent and St. Lucia, the other Windward Islands.
GOVERNMENT ... A British colony which governs Grenada and the Grenadines including the island of Carriacou.
HOW TO GET THERE . . . Grenada is only 55 minutes flying time from St. Lucia; hours from Martinique, 5l/i hours from Antigua, and 50 minutes from Trinidad (all by British West Indian Airways connection with Pan American flights). From New York to Trinidad, 9 hours flying time by Pan American’s de luxe non-stop “El Presidente” service. By ship about 11 days from Boston.
ST. GEORGE’S
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . The new Santa Maria Hotel is the best on this charming little island which is the most southerly of the Windward Islands. The hotel overlooks the bay and harbor of St. George’s which is the main and capital city. There is swimming, fishing, golf and tennis, and rates run from about $4.00 to $7.00 per day with meals. The Antilles and Grand hotels are in the town; rates are about $4 per day with meals. The Saint James runs from about $3 per day. There are many pleasant and inexpensive guest houses.
BANKS . . . Barclay’s Bank and Royal Bank of Canada.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . New Year’s Day, Good Friday, Easter, Whitsuntide, King’s Birthday, Christmas Day, Boxing Day (December 26th). There is a very gay pre-Lenten Carnival here.
CIGARETTES ... English cigarettes available at lower prices. American cigarettes scarce and high. English pipes and tobacco are available.
CLIMATE . . . Tropical, but cooled by trade winds. Very little seasonal or night and day temperature variation. Rainy season, June to December. Same climate as Trinidad. Grenada is at its best during the dry season, December to June.
CLUBS . . . Visitors may obtain guest cards to the following clubs: St. George’s (men only); Richmond Hill Club; Aquatic Club; Silver Sands Club and St. Andrew’s.
COMMUNICATIONS . . . Cables to all parts of the world. Cable office is on the Esplanade. Wireless telephone calls to some West Indies Islands. Air-mail to United States.
CURRENCY ... The monetary unit is the British West Indies
dollar, worth about 60 cents in United States currency.
CUSTOMS REGULATIONS AND DOCUMENTS REQUIRED . . . No passports are necessary for genuine tourists. No duty on personal effects to the value of $1,200.
DRUG STORES . . . Adequate, but few American products.
FAUNA ... Many birds of all sorts.
FLORA . .. Wonderful tropical flowers and lush vegetation everywhere. It is the Spice Island of the West, so there are many spices of all sorts.
FOOD .. . Fish is excellent here.
GAMBLING ... Horse racing occasionally during the season.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING FACILITIES ... Laundry is all right, but don’t dry clean.
LIQUOR .. . Rum and Scotch, but no bourbon. Liquor is very inexpensive here.
MEDICAL FACILITIES ... Hospital and private physicians.
MOTION PICTURES . .. The Empire at St. George’s presents American and English films. There is a cinema at Grenville, too.
MUSIC . . . Native music rather similar to Calypso. Steel bands are groups of musicians who play on steel instruments. It is at Carnival time that you hear this mostly. The Carnival is very gay but not so uninhibited as the one in Trinidad. Everyone is in costume, and there is as usual a King and Queen.
NIGHT CLUBS AND CABARETS . . . There’s a Stork Club in Grenada.
RELIGION . . . Anglican, Roman Catholic, Presbyterian, Methodist, Christian Science, Seventh Day Adventists and the Salvation Army.
