LOCATION ... At the very top of South America, the coast of Venezuela is only about ten miles from Trinidad.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . The birthplace of the great liberator, Simon Bolivar, has a lot of authentic charm to offer the visitor. Caracas is a composite of modern and Spanish Colonial architecture. Its hotels are luxurious; its clubs magnificent and the city is one of the wealthiest capitals in the world. There’s lots to see and do and the climate is fine.
POPULATION . . . Estimated 5,000,000, Venezuela’s population is about a half million less than that of Chicago and Los Angeles combined.
SIZE.. . 352,150 square miles, as big as Texas and Minnesota together.
CAPITAL ... Caracas, a city of 500,000, the size of Cincinnati.
GOVERNMENT... The United States of Venezuela is a republic as are all other countries of South America. HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper one-stop flight, 9 hours from New York to Caracas. One-stop flights from Miami, 5Vi hours. Caracas is 5*4 hours from Panama where connections are made with Pan American flights from New Orleans or Houston and Brownsville, Texas via Guatemala and Central America. By ship, about 5 days with stops three days a week.
CARACAS
ACCOMMODATIONS... Hotel Avila, Nelson Rockefeller’s good-will gift, is good with superb mountain views. Good chef. The Caracas Country Club one of the most lavish and beautiful has a few excellent rooms (card needed). The Hotel Ambassador at entrance to the park is small, but quite good.
Others are: El Conde, Nacional, Waldorf, Potomac, Savoy and El Comercio. Rates Bs 30 to 50 per day, American plan.
ARTS . . . The Museum of Fine Arts has excellent collection of paintings. Bolivar Museum is an important national shrine. Contains relics of the war of independence and a lock of George Washington’s hair which was sent to Bolivar by members of the Washington family. Murals by the famous Tovary Tovar can be seen at the Plaza Bolivar and the Palace of Justice.
BALLET . . . Year around performances by visiting troupes.
BANKS . . . Banco de Venezuela, National City Bank of New York, Royal Bank of Canada, Anglo-South American Bank.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . Major holy days of the Roman Catholic church are observed throughout the country. Other holidays include New Year’s Day; Carnival Monday and Shrove Tuesday; June 24, Battle of Carabobo; July 5, Independence Day; July 24, Bolivar’s birthday; October 12, Columbus Day and November 1, All Saints Day.
CIGARETTES AND TOBACCO . . . American cigarettes are 50 cents a package. 100 cigarettes may be brought in.
CLIMATE . . . Caracas is at an altitude of 3,164 feet and the climate is springlike all year. Weather is “perfect” even in the rainy season, June to December. Venezuela has every kind of climate, from tropical to cold, depending on the altitude.
CLUBS.. .American Club has good food. The Country Club of Caracas, one of the finest although the new Valle Arriba Country Club, organized by young business set, is more lively (cards required). Jockey Club at the track has good restaurant and bar. Club Paraiso is old and distinguished (card required). Rotary club is active here. For golf clubs see sports.
COMMON COURTESIES AND LOCAL CUSTOMS ...
Hot chocolate is as popular as coffee. Almost the national drink. The dinner hour is late.
COMMUNICATIONS ... All American cables. Radio telephone to United States and other countries.
CURRENCY. . . The monetary unit is the bolivar. Worth 30 cents United States. It is divided into 100 centimos.
CUSTOM REGULATIONS FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS AND DOCUMENTS REQUIRED . . . Vaccination, health certificates, police certificate. Passport and visa (or tourist card) from Venezuelan consulates.
DRUG STORES . . . Caracas, Maracaibo, other towns of any size have stores which carry United States toilet goods, patent medicines and staples. Prices higher than at home.
FAUNA . . . Herons, egrets and tapirs, wild cats and crocodiles are found in the wilderness area of the country.
FLORA . . . Orchids, hibiscus, oleander, mahogany trees and eucalyptus are found in Caracas.
FOOD .. . Excellent cuisine, French and International in the best restaurants and hotels of Caracas. Good, native dishes, for which the country is famous: aresancocho, a stew of meats, roots, fruits; the arroz con polio, chicken rice dish with tomatoes, capers, red pepper, pimentos and raisins; banana en dulce, the banana sprinkled with cracker meal, cinnamon, powdered sugar; and baked Pastel de Maracay, a great delicacy, sort of glorified turtle pie, with onions, potatoes and olives. Confections unusually fine. Merida fruits celebrated. Excellent tropic and temperate zone fruits. Watch for Red Snapper filets, all sea-food.
GAMBLING . . . First-class racing Saturdays, Sundays and holidays at the Hipodromo, in the Paraiso section. Cockfighting, and, of course, the lotteries.
LANGUAGE . . . Spanish, but you won’t have any language problems in the better hotels and shops.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING FACILITIES.. .Good, fast, excellent service in the good hotels.
LIQUOR . . . Some good native rums. Ponche crema, a not too potent cream eggnog; also may be bought by the bottle.
