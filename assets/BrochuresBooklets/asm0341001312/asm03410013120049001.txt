LOCATION . . . Germany occupies a large section of Central Europe. Frankfurt, one of the principal cities of Western Germany, is about 400 air miles from London, less than 300 air miles from Paris.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . Nothing has changed the basic beauty of Germany. And now there are all places again open to tourists. Anyone interested in world affairs will find a visit to Germany a stimulating experience, for here the United States and its great allies, France and Great Britain, are operating in complete unity. Many of the age-old cities of the Rhineland are magnificent. The Rhine itself is a superb sight and, of course, the thermal baths of Germany are famous. The German people have a true desire to make the tourist happy and comfortable.
POPULATION . . . Western Germany has a population of 48,000,000, while in the entire country there are approximately 69,000,000, more than one and a half times the population of Italy.
SIZE . . . The area of Western Germany is 93,600 square miles. The entire country occupies 143,200 square miles, an area slightly smaller than that of Montana.
CAPITAL . . . Bonn, a city of 100,000 on the Rhine, is the capital of the Western German Federal Republic. The former German capital, Berlin, has a population of 3,200,000, about a half million fewer than Chicago.
GOVERNMENT . . . Western Germany is a Federal Republic with a legislative body of two houses. The country as a whole is under military occupation by the United States, Great Britain, France and the U.S.S.R.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper, New York via London to Frankfurt, Hamburg, or Cologne and Duesseldorf, about 16 hours (elapsed time). Frequent local service via Pan American between Frankfurt and Berlin, Hamburg, Stuttgart, Bremen, Munich and Vienna. By ship, about 9 days from New York to Hamburg or Bremen.
FRANKFURT
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . Among the leading hotels in Frankfurt which are modern and have good dining rooms are Frankfurter Hof Friedenstrasse 12; Monopol-Metropole, Mannheimer Str. 11-13; Hotel Savoy, Wiesenhuettenstr. 42; Hotel Hamburger Hof opposite the railroad station, and the Hotel National, Baseler Str. 50. Rates are about $2 to $4 per day single.
ARTS . . . There are many museums to be visited in Frankfurt which house magnificent art collections. Among them are: The Goethe Museum, Grosser Hirschgraben 42; the Staedel Institute of Art, Schaumainkai, City Gallery, Duererstrasse; the Frankfurt Society of Art, Eschenheimer Anlage 35; Frankfurt Cabinet of Art, Boersenstrasse; Zimmer Gallery Frank, Boehm-erstrasse 7.
BALLET . . . Great ballet at the Staedtische Opera; ballet groups in the Circus Althoff-Bau in the Zoo.
BANKS . . . Bank Deutscher Laender, Taunus-Anlage 4-5; and branches of all large German banks such as: Deutsche Verkehrs-Kredit Bank, Untermainkai 23/25. In addition to the banks, traveler’s checks may be cashed at the Exchange Counter, Main Railroad Station, Thos. Cook, 27 Friedrich Ebertstrasse and American Express, 9 Taunusanlage.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . New Year’s Day, Good Friday, Easter (Sunday and Monday), Ascension Day, Whitsuntide (Sunday and Monday), Labor Day (May 1), Repentance Day (Wednesday before Sunday of the Dead, in November, a Protestant Holiday), Christmas Day and December 26. Roman Catholic Holidays of Corpus Christi and All Souls Day are also observed.
CIGARETTES, CIGARS, TOBACCO... All kinds of brands including American (expensive) can now be purchased in tobacco stores and most hotels and restaurants.
CLIMATE . . . With the exception of January which is the
coldest month, the winters are mild. There are long springs and agreeable summers, Indian summer until late October.
CLUBS .. . American Press Club, Zeppelinstrasse.
COMMON COURTESIES AND LOCAL CUSTOMS . . . Just about the same as at home.
COMMUNICATIONS... Telegrams, cable, radiogram, telephone and air-mail services to all parts of the world.
CURRENCY. . . The monetary unit is the mark which is worth about 23.8 cents in United States currency.
CUSTOMS REGULATIONS AND DOCUMENTS REQUIRED FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS . . . Passport; visa which may be obtained from German Information and Consular offices, 747 Fifth Avenue, New York. The cost is 50 cents for transit visa, $2 for single return journey and $4 for repeated journeys. You may take in, duty-free, 400 cigarettes or equivalent grams of cigars or 500 grams of tobacco. Also all sports equipment, and you may even take pets.
DRUG STORES . . . There are two types of drug stores in Germany, neither of which have soda fountains nor serve food. The “Apotheke” which sell only pharmaceuticals, and the “Drogerie” which sell standard drugs, cosmetics, etc.
FAUNA. . . About the same as New York State with the exception of chipmunks, skunks, and such birds as tanagers and cardinals.
FLORA . . . About the same as in New York State.
