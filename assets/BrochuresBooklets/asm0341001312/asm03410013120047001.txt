LOCATION . . . Across the North Sea from England, The Netherlands (or Holland) is located between Belgium and Germany. Amsterdam is only 100 air miles north of Brussels.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . Crisp and clean and courteous sums up the land of tulips and canals. You’ll enjoy seeing all the things you’ve read about; the windmills, the wooden shoes, the colorful native costumes. And you will enjoy, too, the modern hotels and good restaurants where food is prodigious and inexpensive.
POPULATION... 10,000,000 which approximates the populations of New York City and Detroit.
SIZE ... 13,433 square miles, the size of Connecticut and Massachusetts.
CAPITAL ... Amsterdam with a population of 830,000, slightly larger than Washington, D. C.
GOVERNMENT ... A constitutional monarchy ruled by a Queen and parliament of two chambers.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper, through-plane flights to Amsterdam from New York and Boston, 16 hours, 45 minutes (elapsed time).
AMSTERDAM
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . There are many fine hotels in Amsterdam, among them: The Victoria, where the food is superb, dancing in the American bar; the Amstel, which is conservative; the Doelen; Europe; American; Schiller, a favorite of Americans; the Park; Pays Bas and Krasnapolsky. Rates run about $2.00 to $3.00 per day and breakfast is always included. It’s wise to bring your own soap, toilet tissue and kleenex.
ARTS...The Rijksmuseum (The State Museum) with its collection of Rembrandt’s paintings including his famous “Night-watch”; Willet Holthuysen Museum, a seventeenth century residence with a collection of porcelain, furniture, and library on the history of art; the Stedelijk Museum (Municipal Museum); Rembrandt House; Royal Palace on the Damsquare, built in 1648 by Jacob van Campen; Allard Pierson Museum, which houses a collection of Egyptian, Greek and Roman antiquities.
BALLET . . . There are two permanent ballet groups considered to be good.
BANKS . . . The Nederlandsche Bank, which is the National Bank; the Amsterdamsche Bank; the Twentsche Bank; the Rotterdamsche Bank Vereniging. Most have affiliations with New York banks. Travelers checks cashed in American Express, Thos. Cook & Son, stores and restaurants and some hotels.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . New Year’s Day, Queen’s Birthday (April 30th), Liberation Day (May 5th), Good Friday, Easter Monday, Ascension Day, Whitsuntide Day, Christmas.
CIGARETTES, TOBACCO . . . Dutch tobacco reasonably good. American cigarettes are scarce and expensive. Bring your own and pay the duty, it’s worth it.
CLIMATE . . . Very moderate, mild winters, cool summers, same as in England. May to September is the ideal time to visit.
CLUBS . . . The American businessman’s club, other private clubs through introduction by a member only.
COMMON COURTESIES AND LOCAL CUSTOMS . . . Not much different from home. Coffee is served in all offices at 11 a.m. The luncheon hour usually lasts from 12:30 to 2 and dinner is early, not later than 7 p.m.
COMMUNICATIONS . . . Telephone, cable, radio, and airmail to the United States.
CURRENCY . . . The monetary unit of the Netherlands is the guilder or florin, worth about 26 cents in our currency.
CUSTOMS REGULATIONS FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS AND DOCUMENTS REQUIRED . . . Passport, no visa required. You may bring in 400 cigarettes duty-free. If you plan to spend more than six days, you may bring additional cigarettes, but these will be subject to an import duty. You must register with the police after 30 days.
DRUG STORES . . . Only real pharmacies. Some are open 24 hours a day.
FAUNA . . . There is not much hunting in Holland . . . small stuff only, like rabbits and foxes. Boars and deer live in reservations. Dutch cattle are world famous. There are fine horses, too, and plenty of pigs and sheep.
FLORA . .. Plenty of flowers . . . the bulbfields near Haarlem . . . season is from about April 15 till May 15. Aalsmeer is the center of the flower “industry” ... roses, lilacs, etc. A visit to one of the flower auctions is recommended.
FOOD . . . Dutch food is world famous. Some typical Dutch dishes are Rolpens (minced beef, usually served with fried apples), herring, smoked eels, oysters, mussels, lobster, salmon, poffertjes (small salted fritters). Throughout Holland there are many East Indian restaurants which you should try. The food is exotic, but simply delicious. Some of the specialties are nasi goreng, bami goreng, samba! goreng, sate and kroepoek. They taste something like Chinese food. And of course the Dutch cheeses are known the world over for being the finest.
LANGUAGE...English understood and spoken everywhere.
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING FACILITIES... Available everywhere. Modern laundries and cleaners are expensive.
LIQUOR . . . All kinds of liquor available. A specialty is the Dutch “jenever” (Dutch gin). The Dutch drink this straight, and chilled just as we do martinis and Manhattans. Try the famous Holland beer as well as Advocaat, an unusual egg drink.
MEDICAL FACILITIES . . . Excellent and available everywhere. Ask your hotel clerk.
MUSIC.. .Amsterdam Concertgebouw Orchestra is the third largest in the world. Regular concerts are held during the winter.
NIGHT CLUBS AND CABARETS . . . There are innumerable night clubs in Amsterdam; in the smart ones the prices are about the same as at home.
