LOCATION . . . Spain along with Portugal occupies the Iberian Peninsula, from the Pyrenees at the French border to Gibraltar. Madrid is 319 air miles from Lisbon.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . The land of the bullfight is a land of color. The skies and waters are brilliant blue, the foliage is almost tropical. The architecture shows the imprint of the many influences that have shaped the country. Art galleries and museums filled with treasures abound. Food is wonderful, the top hotels are good, the tempo restful. . . . There is music and gaiety in Spain, a color that is not duplicated anywhere else in the world. Spaniards are unfailingly courteous to strangers, and while they may not always understand you, they will at least make the effort. It’s a wonderful spot to rest. They won’t let you hurry.
POPULATION ... Estimated 27,761,000, the population density is similar to that of Illinois.
SIZE ... 196,607 square miles, about 3 Vi times the area of Illinois.
CAPITAL... Madrid, population 1,560,000, about the size of San Francisco and Washington, D.C. combined.
GOVERNMENT ... A state, with Generalissimo Franco granted a life tenure as chief-of-state.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American clipper, elapsed time from New York or Boston, about 18Vi hours to Barcelona; about 22Vi hours to Madrid via Lisbon; about 23 hours to Madrid via London. By ship,
about 7 days to Lisbon, 6 to 8 days to Gibraltar.
MADRID AND BARCELONA
ACCOMMODATIONS ... In Madrid, Palace Hotel, Plaza de las Cortes; the new Hotel Velazquez and the Ritz Hotel, Plaza de la Lealtad, classed as de luxe by the Spanish State Tourist Department and the best in town. Single rooms with bath begin at $4.50 per day, American plan. The Hotel Astoria and Hotel Emperador are also very good. At Barcelona is the Ritz Hotel ($5.00) and Hotel Colon ($5.00) in the de luxe category. Other good hotels are Majestic ($4.00), Continental ($4.00), Gran Via ($3.00) and Principe ($4.00). On the famous hill overlooking Barcelona city and the Mediterranean is found the Hotel Florida ($4.00). All prices are American plan starting prices. There is a 12 per cent service charge at all hotels and restaurants.
There are also 24 government-sponsored inns, plain, clean, practical, in areas not served by hotels. Some, as those in Ireland, are remodeled castles. Basic rates, with meals, about $3.50 a day. Local and Continental cooking—local architectural features in them all. These are found at halfway points on main highways.
ARTS . . . The Museo de la Real Armeria in Madrid has the world’s greatest collection of armour. The Prado Museum, classical picture gallery adjoining the Salon del Prado, with its great paintings (Velazquez, El Greco, Goya, Rubens are found here in numbers and quality not found anywhere else) is the number one must in Madrid, and all Spain. Not to see it would be like going to Washington and not seeing the Capitol. In Barcelona the Museum of Modern Art is important.
BALLET . . . Many prominent European and American ballet stars appear in Madrid and Barcelona during the spring. Fine Italian and French opera companies appear in both cities.
BANKS. . . Both Barcelona and Madrid have branches of Banco Hispano-Americano, London & South America Bank, etc.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . New Year’s, Good Friday, November 1, Christmas, and in different localities many feast-days; besides those big fairs, such as Barcelona’s famous “verbenas” of San Juan and San Pedro, 24 and 29 of July, celebrated on the eve of these days, there is Seville’s Holy Week
and the popular “Feria” during the months of April and May; Pamplona’s Patron Saint Day “San Fermin”, July 7; Valencia’s Patron Saint Day “San Jose”, May 19, when the “Fallas” are celebrated; Madrid’s festival of San Isidro, May 15, very typical and gay; San Sebastian’s, August 15, a festival starting on the 13th and lasting the whole week. Try to visit where you can on the big days.
CIGARETTES, CIGARS, TOBACCO . . . Imported American and other foreign brands of cigarettes plentiful but expensive. Spanish cigarettes, black, strong, inexpensive. You may bring in enough cigarettes for your personal use, about 2 cartons.
CLIMATE . . . Madrid has a 2,000-foot elevation and it is fairly cold in the winter and hot in the summer, but due to its dry climate it isn’t bad. It receives the fresh breeze from the nearby Sierra de Guadarrama which, it is said, makes it the most healthful capital of Europe. Best months are May, June, September and October.
Barcelona, situated on the Mediterranean coast, offers an ideal holiday climate. It is hot in the summer, mild in winter. Best seasons are spring, which starts as early as March, and autumn; winter does not start until November. Barcelona’s hottest months are July and August, but the beautiful nearby mountains and coast are an excellent and comforting refuge.
CLUBS . . . Many exclusive, smart clubs in both cities.
COMMON COURTESIES, LOCAL CUSTOMS . . . Spaniards are not too punctual, sometimes a little late for appointments. Luncheon is late; “aperitivo” (cocktail hour) may run as late as 8 or 9:30; with dinner served from 10 p.m. on to midnight, when the town really becomes alive. Shops everywhere close from 1 to 3, open until 7 p.m. The siesta is universal.
COMMUNICATIONS . . . Telephones, slow—there is regular air-mail service to United States.
CURRENCY. . . The monetary unit is the peseta, about 39.86 to the dollar.
