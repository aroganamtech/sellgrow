LOCATION . . . The Dominican Republic and Haiti occupy the island of Hispaniola about 50 miles southeast of Cuba and about the same distance west of Puerto Rico.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . This little country is the spot where Columbus landed and where he is buried. It is a curious combination of modern efficiency with modern buildings, spotless streets, supermarkets, luxurious hotels and centuries-old civilization. The hotels are the newest and most elaborate in the West Indies, the beaches are good, the fishing and hunting excellent. Tourists are welcome here and they are treated courteously and with the efficient service that marks this government. The climate is unvaryingly pleasant and the highways excellent.
POPULATION . . . Estimated 1,969,773, approximately the population of Los Angeles.
SIZE . . . Occupying the eastern two-thirds of the island, the area is 19,332 square miles, about the size of New Hampshire and Vermont.
CAPITAL . . . Ciudad Trujillo, a city of about 139,000 population, a few thousand greater than Sacramento, California.
GOVERNMENT ... A republic.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American clipper to Ciudad Trujillo, 5 hours from Miami. By connecting airline, about 9 hours from New York. By ship, about 6 days from New York.
CIUDAD TRUJILLO
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . The Hotel Jaragua is one of the newest, most luxurious hotels in the West Indies. Built by the government, it is managed by American hotel operators and has everything. The surf breaks at the edge of its lawns, there is dancing in the patio nightly, the rooms are luxurious, with radios, telephones and baths. There are tennis courts, a swimming pool, a golf course. There are separate cottages on the grounds available. Rates, December-April, range from $12 to $17, double. Less in the off season. The Fausto, a smaller hotel has rates from $7 to $16, double. The Victoria, $10, double.
ARTS . . . The National Gallery of Fine Arts shows painting, sculpture and ceramics by Dominican and foreign artists.
BALLET...No national ballet, but there are native dances.
BANKS. . . Royal, Bank of Canada, Dominican Republic Reserve Bank, Bank of Nova Scotia, Agricultural and Credit Bank of the Dominican Republic, the Central Bank.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS. . . January 26, Duarte Day (equivalent to George Washington Day in the United States); February 27, Independence Day; May 1, Labor Day; May 16, the date President Trujillo was inaugurated into office; August 16, the date President Trujillo took the oath of office; October 12, Columbus Day; October 24, Flag Day—birth of President Trujillo.
CIGARETTES ... As tobacco is grown here, there are good cigars and some strong cigarettes. American cigarettes available at 80 cents a pack. No restrictions on bringing in up to 1 carton; on more than 1 carton, duty must be paid.
CLIMATE... It is warm and tropical, but never uncomfortable. Best time to visit the Dominican Republic is November through March.
CLUBS . . . The Santo Domingo Golf Club has a fine course. Casino de Guibia, located on the ocean front; minimum fee for Jaragua guests. Club de la Juventud, popular club of the social set of the city. Dancing once a week.
CURRENCY. . . The monetary unit is the Dominican peso
worth the same as the American dollar.
CUSTOMS REGULATIONS FOR UNITED STATES CITIZENS . . . No passport or visa needed. Identification cards may be bought when booking accommodations. There is a one-dollar tourist card which permits the visitor to stay in the country 30 days.
COMMUNICATIONS...International telephone and cable service. All American Cables, Inc. RCA serves the Republic, too.
DRUG STORES... All very modern with United States products available.
FAUNA . . . Wonderful tropical birds.
FLORA... Beautiful tropical flowers of all varieties. Banana, palm and coffee trees in abundance. Pineapples, avocados, other fruits. Sugar cane, of course, and tobacco.
FOOD . .. The food is naturally Spanish:sancocho, for instance, is a soup containing 18 ingredients such as chicken, pork, yams, Spanish sausage, onions, pumpkins and tomatoes. Very fine, indeed. There is the inevitable arroz con polio, casava baked with cheese and boiled eggs and pastelitos, pastries filled with chicken or meat. The hotels serve international food, too.
GAMBLING. . . The Jaragua Casino is open every night; there is a race track called the “Pearl of the Antilles.”
LAUNDRY AND DRY CLEANING FACILITIES.. Good.
LIQUOR. . . Everything is available at about the same prices as at home.
MEDICAL FACILITIES . . . There are good English-speaking doctors and several modern hospitals.
MOTION PICTURES. . . There are several new motion picture theatres showing American and European films as well as Latin American ones.
MUSIC.. .There is the National Symphony Orchestra which presents a series of concerts each year. Then, too, there is lovely native music and the national dance, the merengue.
NIGHT CLUBS AND CABARETS... The Jaragua has several night clubs, including the roof night club which insists on evening clothes.
