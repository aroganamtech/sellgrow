LOCATION . . . Argentina fills the point of South America except for the thin strip of Chile along the western border. It extends for 2,300 miles from Bolivia to Tierra del Fuego, almost the distance across the whole United States.
CHARACTERISTICS . . . “B.A.” is a modern city as cosmopolitan as any in the world. The theatres, the shops, the hotels, the subways, the public buildings are the last word in progress. You will do the same things here that you would do in New York, London or Paris. You will dress in the same way, too. There is lots that is strange, lots that is new and exciting. But keep in mind that the Argentinians and particularly the Porteños, as residents of Buenos Aires are called, are sophisticated people not in any sense to be considered or treated as “natives.” The Jockey Club is one of the finest in the world.
POPULATION . . . Estimated 17,342,000—about equal to the population of New York State and Iowa.
SIZE... About 1,072,745 square miles, a third the size of the United States.
CAPITAI*. . . Buenos Aires, the largest city of Latin America, with a population of about 3,144,457. Greater Buenos Aires has a population of 4,500,000.
GOVERNMENT . . . Like all South American countries, Argentina is a republic, with a constitution similar to that of the United States.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American’s de luxe through-plane service to Buenos Aires via the East Coast of South America, 29 hours from New York. By Panagra down the west coast 19 hours from Miami. By ship 19 days from New York or New Orleans.
BUENOS AIRES
ACCOMMODATIONS . . . The Plaza, on the Plaza San Martin, has the top reputation among North Americans. It is modern and beautifully run. Much of the social life of the city revolves around it. Rates $5.00 to $6.00 single, $8.00 to $10.00 double with bath, European plan.
The Alvear Palace on the Avenida Alvear also has a great tradition. The rates are about the same as the Plaza. Slightly less expensive are the Continental in the business district, the Lancaster accessible to the shopping district, the City and the Nogaro. All of these hotels cater to an international clientele. Because of the hotel shortage, make reservations well in advance. There are various apartment hotels if you plan to stay for a longer time. You may also find comfortable accommodations in the various pensions which abound. The best way to find these is through advertisements in the local newspapers.
ARTS . . . The National Museum of Decorative Arts is notable for its paintings, tapestry and antiques. The Municipal Museum “Isaac Fernandez Blanco,” Suipacha 1422, has an interesting collection of old silver, costumes and other Argentinian antiques. This museum is a lovely reproduction of a colonial house and is one of the most attractive and interesting in the city. The Exposition Salon, Posadas 1725, holds interesting exhibitions of contemporary art. National Historical Museum, Defensa 1600, is the largest and most important museum in the country. In addition to historical exhibits it contains a permanent exhibition of the Spanish and Impressionistic Schools of painting. Works of native Argentinian painters are added to the collection each year. This is a rrlust. There are, of course, a number of private art galleries, including Wildenstein, which have interesting exhibitions,
BALLET . . . There is a native ballet company which during the season (May to September) performs at the famous Teatro Colon. There is a short ballet season before and after the grand
season, too. In addition to native artists many world famous dancers appear in Buenos Aires during the season.
BANKS . . . National City Bank of New York, Bartolomé Mitre and San Martin, First National Bank of Boston, Diagonal Norte and Florida, The Royal Bank of Canada, San Martin 85, The Bank of the Argentine Nation, The Central Bank, similar to our Federal Reserve.
CALENDAR OF HOLIDAYS . . . There are about twenty holidays during the year, plus special holidays declared by the government. Most important are: May 1, Labor Day; May 25th, Cabildo Abierto of May 25th, 1810; July 9th, Independence Day; January 6th, Dia de Reyes; August 17th, the anniversary of the death of San Martin; October 17th, anniversary of the Revolution of 1945; November 1st, All Saints Day; December 25th, Christmas; January 1, New Year’s Day. There is a carnival on the Monday and Tuesday preceding Lent. There are various half holidays celebrated throughout the year.
CIGARETTES, PIPES, TOBACCOS , , , American cigarettes are available but will cost around 75 cents United States currency per pack. United States citizens can bring in 100 cigarettes and 25 cigars. American pipe tobacco is hard to obtain.
CLIMATE . . . Buenos Aires has a climate similar to that of Charleston, S. C. The seasons are reversed, of course. Winter begins in June and summer in December. The warmest months are January and February. It seldom freezes and never snows in winter, but it is chilly. There is no rainy season. All in all, November to December is the ideal time to visit Argentina.
