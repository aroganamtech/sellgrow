LOCATION . . . Mexico lies between the United States and Central America with coast lines on the Pacific Ocean, the Gulf of Mexico and the Caribbean. Mexico City is 1560 air miles from Los Angeles, 1275 air miles from Miami, 746 air miles from Houston.
CHARACTERISTICS ... Magnificent Mexico, land of the ancient Aztecs, has a wide range of attractions for everyone. Fabulous beaches and world-famous resorts . . . modern cities flavored with the charm of Old Spain .. . volcanoes, snow-capped mountains, blooming deserts . . . thrilling bullfights, colorful fiestas . . . exquisite shops, luxurious hotels, the gayest of night clubs, whatever your pleasure, you’ll find it in abundance in Mexico. And happily for vacation plans, you’ll receive more than eight pesos for one United States dollar!
POPULATION . . . Estimated at 25,261,064—equal to about a sixth of the population of the United States.
SIZE ... 760,290 square miles, roughly a quarter of the area of the United States.
CAPITAL.. . Mexico City, officially Mexico D.F. (Federal District), population 3,158,029, about a half million smaller than Chicago.
GOVERNMENT . . . Mexico is a republic of 28 states, federated in the same manner as the United States. There are also two territories and a Federal District where the capital is located.
HOW TO GET THERE ... By Pan American Clipper, Mexico City is 334 hours, non-stop flying time from Houston; 5y2 hours from Los Angeles; 314 hours from Brownsville, Texas via Tampico. By connecting airlines at Houston, elapsed time to Mexico City is 8 hours from St. Louis, about 9l/i hours from Chicago, 10 hours from Washington, D. C., 11 hours from New York. Merida is 3 hours from Miami or New Orleans. Frequent air services within Mexico by CMA and AERONAVES (Mexican airline companies). By train Mexico City is 3 days and ^ nights from New York, 2 days and 2 nights from St. Louis.
MEXICO CITY
ACCOMMODATIONS • • • There are hotels of every type available to the tourist. You can live luxuriously or modestly, depending upon your budget which must be your guide in choosing "accommodations. Outside Mexico City there is little difference in price between the Class A and the Class B hotels, but the difference in comfort can be great. Investigate carefully when making your reservations. Here are some of the good hotels (all European plan) in Mexico City:
Del Prado: facing the Alameda, largest, most luxurious hotel in Mexico. Murals by Diego Rivera, Covarrubias and Montenegro. Rates average $5 single, $6 double.
Reforma: Paseo de la Reforma and Calle Paris. Very swank, with roof-garden, Cafe de Paris and lots of Hollywood folk. Rates average $5 single, $6 double.
Ritz: Av. Madero 30. Well known like other Ritz Hotels.
Murals by Covarrubias. Rates average $3 single, $4 double.
Regis: Ave. Juarez 77. Has an excellent night club Capri,
Paolo restaurant and “El Greco” bar. Rates average $3 and $5.
Prince: Luis Moya No. 12. Good central location, bar and fine restaurant. Rates average $3 single, $4.50 double.
Comee: Melchor Ocampo No. 323 in residential district, but good hotel with good food and music for dining. Rates average $4 single, $5.50 double.
Geneve: Londres 130, excellent. Similar rates to Ritz.
It is advisable to consult the Mexican Tourist Bureau for information on the above.
ARTS • • • There is so much art in Mexico that it is difficult to decide what to see first. Musts are: The National Palace, with frescoes by Diego Rivera; the Fine Arts Palace, with frescoes by
Rivera, Orozco and Siqueiros; the Palace of Justice, with frescoes by Orozco and George Biddle; the National Museum of Anthropology, in which all the many cultures of Mexico are represented; the Central Art Gallery, which houses most of the Mexican moderns; the Ministry of Education, which contains sculptures, frescoes by Carlos Merida and Rivera, and a self-portrait by the latter. The Palace of Fine Arts houses a theatre and a museum. The theatre has a curtain of Tiffany glass depicting two extinct volcanoes. The Palace also houses El Museo Nacional de Artes Plasticas. The Museum of Flora and Fauna is filled with exotic specimens from all parts of the country. Galeria Mexicana is devoted to the works of Rivera.
BALLET • • • Visiting companies of ballet show in Mexico occasionally. Opera in Spanish with ballet is presented at the Palace of Fine Arts each spring.
BANKS • • • Banco Continental in Guardiola Bldg. (English spoken); Banco de Londres y Mexico, 16 de Septiembre and Bolivar; Banco de Mexico, 5 de Mayo and Motolinia (National Government Bank); National City Bank of New York, Uruguay and Isabel la Catolica.
