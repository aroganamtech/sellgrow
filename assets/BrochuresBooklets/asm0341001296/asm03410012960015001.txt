When the airlines are attempting to usher in the Jet Age in the U. S., this vanishing profit margin becomes alarming.
The reason profits are disappearing is that while the price the airlines charge for their product remains almost fixed, the price they pay for supplies and services has gone up, and up, and up.
The industry has now reached the point where its ability to absorb increasing costs—while maintaining a fare level only 1.9 per cent over 1938—is at a dead end.
It’s no secret that consumer prices are up. Any housewife knows that. The airlines also pay more for the things that go into their market basket.
