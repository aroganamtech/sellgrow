When the Civil Aeronautics Act of 1938 set out the ground rules for the young airline industry, there were 23 airlines. Now there are 53 airlines. During the early years, they were helped—as were the barge lines, merchant marine and railroads before them—by government aid or subsidy.
However the trunk lines today, and the great majority of the international lines, are completely free of subsidy. As a matter of fact, the total amount of subsidy paid by the Federal Government since 1938 is only a little more than half as much as the total of Federal income taxes paid by the airlines and the transportation tax generated by carrying passengers.
