The heart of the new jet aircraft lies in its engine.
Like the plane itself, it is big. And it is powerful.
The jet engine is a blending of modern science with one of the oldest laws of physics.
Sir Isaac Newton could be called the Father of the Jet Engine because as far back as 1680 he set forth a basic law. It was: “For every action there is a reaction, equal in force and opposite in direction.”
This law can be clearly seen in a child’s balloon. Blow it up, release it, and watch it travel in jet-propelled flight.
The stream of air escaping from the opening is the “action”; the equal force pushing against the front wall of the balloon is the “reaction.”
