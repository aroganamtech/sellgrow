To take an example, when transport planes were bought 20 years ago, they cost $125,000 apiece. Now, the modern, piston-engine planes cost about $2.5 million. Tomorrow, the big new jet-powered planes will cost about $6 million, including engines and spare parts.
Another significant cost item is labor. Recently, a major airline signed an agreement under which senior pilots assigned to the new jets will earn about $27,000 a year, or $8,000 more per year than these same pilots are presently earning on piston-engine planes.
As a means of ending the profit squeeze, the trunk lines have asked the Civil Aeronautics Board for authorization to increase fares to permit earnings that would stimulate investments and stock purchases, and attract debt capital.
