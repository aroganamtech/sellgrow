Earlier this year, after lengthy delays, the CAB granted a temporary increase of 4 per cent, plus $1 per ticket. The inadequacy of this increase—even without the heavy financial burdens of the jet program—can be seen in a comparison of profit margins over the years.
Last year, the profit margin was 1.9 per cent. With the recent increase, it is estimated that the margin will be 2.61, a long way down from the 4.6 per cent of 1956 and the 7 per cent of 1952.
Perhaps the CAB’s reluctance to permit adequate fares stems from the Board’s approach to rate regulation.
The Board views the airlines as a monopoly industry in the classic form.
