The jet engine is as thirsty as it is powerful. It uses five times as much fuel per hour as present piston engines. The new planes have a fuel capacity equal to the amount that an average motorist would use operating his car for more than 22 years!
The most striking feature of the jets is the swept-back wing. The sweep angle, 30 to 35 degrees, tucks the large wing area close to the body. Why sweep? It means that a large thick wing— such as the jets employ—provides a relatively thin surface to the air at high speeds, thus cutting down “drag.” Also, a thicker wing allows for greater fuel capacity and aids in takeoff.
