In the sixth century B.C., India was invaded by Darius and two centuries later by Alexander the Great. Invasion followed invasion and the Mongol (who were called Moguls in India) invasion in the tenth century was one of the bloodiest. The Mogul period reached its climax in the reigns of Babur and his grandson, Akbar, who was the most celebrated and perhaps the most remarkable of India’s rulers. It was during Akbar’s reign that western contacts with India began to develop. It was the Moguls and their descendants who imported Mohammedanism into India.
Among the first to arrive were Portuguese Jesuits and Englishmen. The English came to trade and the first post of the East India Company was built at Surat in 1609. A century later India as an empire fell to pieces, and the powerful East India Trading Company gradually took over, ousting the French and Dutch. In 1857, as a result of the Indian Mutiny, the administration of India was transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown. By the beginning of the 19th century, Great Britain was firmly in control, and for the next 140 years the country enjoyed complete immunity from invasion. Not until World War II was there any serious threat in this direction. With the division of India into the Dominion of India and the Dominion of Pakistan, the year 1947 opened a long-awaited dream of Indian leaders —the dream of an independent people.
December 7, 1941, PAA ANZAC CLIPPER, en route from Noumea to Auckland, on receipt of news of Pearl Harbor, flew around the world to New York and later to San Francisco. This was the first round-the-world flight by a commercial aircraft.
