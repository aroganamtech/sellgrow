ROUTES EFFECTIVE OCTOBER 1,1947
This Settlement had complete powers of self-government, including police rule. Although for many years the Settlement was limited only to foreign merchants, during China’s revolution Chinese refugees were allowed inside its gates. Now Chinese residents constitute a majority of its population. In 1926 Chinese were admitted to the municipal council where they represented their people until the beginning of World War II. Shanghai was defended against the Japanese for three months during 1937, and in December, 1941, the International Settlement was occupied by Japanese troops.
Since the war all foreign concessions in Shanghai—British, French, and American — have been abrogated, and both the International Settlement and the French Concession have been returned to the jurisdiction of the Chinese. Today they can be considered only as districts of Greater Shanghai.
