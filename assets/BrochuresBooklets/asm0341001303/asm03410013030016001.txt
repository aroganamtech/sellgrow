CLIPPERS GO FULL CIRCLE —AROUND THE WORLD
World War II accelerated the development of Pan American. Facilities, equipment, personnel, and experience were all enlisted in support of the war effort of the Allied Nations. The established system was converted to the essential job to be done — whether it be transport of military personnel and war materiel, or the training of flight, engineering and maintenance personnel for the military services. Pan American was called upon to construct airports and air bases in the most forbidding corners of the earth. Whole route systems were built and flown. In war dress, Pan Am made 9,500 crossings of the Atlantic, 5,500 crossings of the Pacific, during the period.
The stress of war gave great impetus to aircraft and airport development. Land planes of great flying range were designed and built. With the return to peace, Pan Am converted to land planes — the Boeing Strato-Clippers, Douglas DC-4’s and Lockheed Constellations, then later to DC-6B’s and the DC-7C.
Having spanned the Pacific, and then the Atlantic, all that remained was for Pan Am to join eastern and western routes for full around-the-world service. On June 17, 1947, the Clipper “America”, a Lockheed Constellation, took off from LaGuardia Airport, New York — destination LaGuardia Airport, ’round-the-world.
