TOMORROW . . . AND TOMORROW
At this very moment, on the drawing boards of American airplane manufacturers, are projected plans of aircraft for the next stage in flight — the Supersonic commercial airliner. In possibly ten years, you may fly in a Supersonic Clipper not too unlike the Jet Clipper of today. But you will travel at speeds in excess of Mach 3, three times the speed of sound, or approximately 2,000 miles-per-hour. Such swiftness would mean that you could fly from New York to London in two hours, and across the Pacific in half a day.
If this kind of dreaming is not big enough, consider the probable air development beyond Supersonic transports. This will be in the area of Boost-Glide — using rocket propulsion for height and speed, a winged fuselage for gliding into destination.
Within your lifetime, the whole world can become as accessible as the nearest town. In all that happens in the future — as in the past — Pan American engineers, designers and technicians will have a hand in the planning. The Clippers of tomorrow will meet the same high standards of performance and dependability that Pan American has always imposed upon all aircraft added to its fleet.
