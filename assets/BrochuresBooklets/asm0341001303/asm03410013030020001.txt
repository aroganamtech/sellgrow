PAN AM ENTERS THE JET AGE
In July 1958 Pan American made the startling announcement that it was introducing Jet Clipper service to Europe in the fall of that year.
Here was a totally different mode of air transportation — made possible, first, by the Boeing 707 Jet Clipper, developed as a joint effort by Boeing and Pan American. The DC-8 Jet Clipper, later addition to the Pan Am fleet, also promises a new concept of travel comfort, and traveling range, to the public. These Jet aircraft have a flying speed close to 600 miles per hour. London in 6V2 hours, Paris in 7. Introduced in other areas, Jet flight means 4 Vi hours to Hawaii, Buenos Aires in less than half a day, 11 Vi hours Japan to the U. S. In Jet Clippers 150 passengers enjoy a degree of comfort never before known, or even dreamed of.
The implications of Jet travel to human living are as important as the technical accomplishment. The world is as small as air speed makes it. Aware of these things, Mr. Trippe recently said “Mass travel by air — made possible in the Jet Age — may prove to be more significant to world destiny than the atom bomb. For there can be no atom bomb more powerful than the air tourist, charged with curiosity, enthusiasm and good will, who can roam the four corners of the world, meeting in friendship and understanding the people of other nations and races.”
