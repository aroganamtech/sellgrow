1927—AN INTERNATIONAL AIRLINE 90 MILES LONG
The story starts in 1927. Juan Terry Trippe and eleven others invested $25,000 each, joined forces with two other air operation groups that had hopes for an international air service in the Caribbean, and Pan American Airways, Inc. was born. Juan Trippe was elected president.
Newly-formed Pan American had an air mail contract — Key West to Havana. In a scant few weeks a mudflat became an airfield. Stations and hangars were built, red tape cleared away. There were twenty-four employees, dedicated to the job to be done. The Pan American fleet? — three brand-new F-7 Fokker airplanes. The F-7 had three engines. It could fly on any two, a vital safety factor. Flight range, 100 miles. A ship of wood and fine stretched linen, with comforting flotation gear in the high-flung wings.
October 28, 1927 was the big day. Pan American Flight No. 1 took off from Key West bound for Havana — the first U. S. international air service, in scheduled operation. The distance — 90 miles. Flying speed — 90 miles-per-hour. Space for eight passengers, plus mail.
And it was only the beginning . . .
