OVER THE TOP OF THE WORLD — TO EUROPE
The shortest distance between the West Coast of the United States and Europe is over the top of the world. Pan American, in its Atlantic and Pacific route survey and exploration work, conducted in 1932 and 1933, was familiar with air operating technique in Polar regions. In 1957 it put this knowledge to further practical use — by establishing a Polar short-cut to Europe.
In preparation for this operation, a full-scale refueling and service base was built at Frobisher Bay in northeastern Canada, approximate half-way point. On September 10, 1957, Pan Am’s first scheduled Polar Route flight took place — San Francisco to London. The Clipper, a broad-winged Douglas DC-7C — flight range 5,000 miles, passenger capacity 100. In thirty years, Pan Am had come from a 90-mile hop to Cuba, to a full-scale world service, including U. S.-Europe scheduled service over the Pole.
