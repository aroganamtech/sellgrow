How much does the government pay Pan American in subsidy and what does it get in return?
The Civil Aeronautics Board had estimated that subsidy to Pan American for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1955 would be $26,388,000. This represented some 11.4% of Pan American’s total revenues. It now appears that, as a result of increased carriage of military mail, the actual subsidy will be lower.
Pan American provides transportation for 28.2% of all passengers traveling between the United States and its overseas territories and foreign countries (except Canada and Mexico) by all means, both air and sea. It carries 42.8% of those traveling by air and 62.2% of the U. S. airmail to the territories and foreign countries. It keeps in readiness 62,751 miles of routes and 93 overseas stations. Its fleet of 114 four-engined, long-range transports, along with the crews ready to man them and the mechanics ready to maintain them, could provide the capacity in a sudden emergency to move, as an example, from the United States to Europe, 8,000 troops or 1,800,000 pounds of cargo in one day. The cost to the Government of maintaining these and 14 twin-engined aircraft and the flight crews alone would amount to $90,000,000 a year. All but 12 of the fleet of 114 four-engine aircraft either have been modified or have been committed to modification in accordance with the Government’s Civil Reserve Air Fleet program. In addition 7 DC-7Bs currently in production will also incorporate modifications to military specifications. All of these aircraft are available for military use on 48 hours’ notice.
