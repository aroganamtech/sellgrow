Does the U. S* pay a higher rate per ton-mile for mail transportation than is paid by passengers?
No. The so-called “U. S. mail pay” figure includes not only payment for transportation of the mail as such, but also subsidy. By very definition, therefore, the total payment per ton-mile of mail, which includes subsidy, will be higher than that per ton-mile of passengers, which does not.
However, under President Eisenhower’s Reorganization Plan 10 of 1953, the Civil Aeronautics Board segregates this total figure of U. S. mail pay as between compensatory mail pay for carrying the mail, which is paid by the Post Office Department, and subsidy, which is paid by the Civil Aeronautics Board. Under this segregation Pan American now receives for carrying the mail an average of 53.9 cents per ton-mile. This compares with an average yield of 68 cents per ton-mile on passenger traffic and an average of 122 cents per mail ton-mile which the United States pays to foreign-flag carriers under the Universal Postal Union Convention. Pan American’s foreign competitors receive more from their governments, and from ours, for carrying the mail than the United States pays Pan American. This unjust discrimination is one of the factors enabling some foreign airlines to show a lower subsidy need.
