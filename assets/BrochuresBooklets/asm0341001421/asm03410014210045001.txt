Considering the amount of service rendered, does Pan American receive a di sproportionaie share of the total air transport subsidy?
The Civil Aeronautics Board estimates that of the total subsidy for the fiscal year 1954 for international, overseas and territorial air carriers - $43,104,000 excluding intra-Alaskan and intra-Hawaiian operations and international stub-end operations of domestic carriers - Pan Anerican received $27,991,000 or 65% of the total. However, Pan American rendered 67% of the service performed by this group of carriers, so that its share of the subsidy was somewhat less than its proportion of the service rendered. Pan American has much more than its proportionate share of the long, thin national interest routes - such as those to the South Pacific, South Africa, and up to the door of the Iron Curtain in Finland and Austria.
As shown in Table I, whereas Pan American’s Latin American services received 17.7 cents of subsidy for each dollar of commercial revenues earned, Braniff received 35.9 cents, or twice as much.
Similarly, Pan American received 17.1 cents of subsidy for each dollar of transportation revenue on its Pacific operations, while Northwest received 27.2 cents. On the Atlantic, Pan American’s subsidy for the area of operation in which it competes with TWA is slightly lower than TWA. However, when the long, thin national interest routes to Africa and Finland (which require substantially higher subsidy) are included, the Pan American subsidy in the Atlantic is slightly higher than TWA.
