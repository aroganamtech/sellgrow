What is the nature of Pan American’s foreign-flag competition?
Most of Pan American’s foreign competitors are government-owned. As a result they do not need to show earnings sufficient to attract private investment, and most of them have earned little or nothing.
The wages paid by these foreign lines are in accord with those paid in their countries generally. A study has been made of wage payments per employee in BOAC, Air France, KLM, SAS and Swissair. The weighted average of the wages in these companies is only 43% of those paid by Pan American. The disparity is shown on Chart No., ft. Certain potentially strong competitors, such as Japan Air Lirfes, have wage levels still lower.
Pan American’s flight crews produce no more mileage in a year’s time than do foreign pilots, although the quality of their work is unsurpassed and probably unequalled. Indeed, under U.S. Government regulation and union contracts, they fly less than flight crews from many other countries. There is no inherent advantage in the productivity of Pan American’s aircraft because the same machines can be and are bought by its foreign competitors. The annual aircraft utilization in certain foreign lines compares favorably with American practice. Although the American private enterprise system and the quality of American personnel bring about more productivity per employee than in government-owned foreign airlines, the difference is not sufficiently great to offset the labor differential. This is in marked contrast to certain industries, such as the manufacturing of consumer durables, where a large investment in mass production tooling enables American concerns to meet the competition of foreign producers.
