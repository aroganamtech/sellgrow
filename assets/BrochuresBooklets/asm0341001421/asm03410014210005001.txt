What does Pan American do?
Pan American carries passengers, cargo and mail by air between the Continental United States and all of its territories - Alaska, Hawaii, Guam, the Canal Zone, Puerto Rico and the Virgin Islands - and 64* foreign countries and colonies in all parts of the world - Central and South America, Europe, Middle East, Asia, Africa and Australasia. The routes are shown on Chart No. 1.
Pan American operates these routes under certificates of convenience and necessity issued by the Government under the Civil Aeronautics Act of 1938. Some of these certificates were issued pursuant to Congressional direction in recognition of Pan American’s pioneering activities between 1927 and 1938. The others were issued by the Civil Aeronautics Board, after complete public hearings and with the approval of Presidents Roosevelt, Truman and Eisenhower.
In accordance with the policies of the United States Government, Pan American was responsible, until the third term of the Roosevelt Administration,. for all of the overseas and international air services of the United States. For the same reason, it was not authorized to offer scheduled airline service within the Continental United States. Immediately after World War II, Pan American applied for a number of domestic routes; after hearings before the Civil Aeronautics Board, these applications were denied. To date, as in the past, Pan American is not authorized to render service between any points within the Continental United States. On the other hand, its principal U.S.-flag competitors abroad have very large domestic networks and numerous sales offices in the United States.
* Pan American’s affiliate, Pan American-Grace Airways, in which W.R. Grace & Co. is a 50% stockholder, serves eight countries - three of which are also served directly by Pan American World Ai rways.
