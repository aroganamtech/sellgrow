How have Pan American’s stockholders come out?
The return to Pan American’s stockholders has been altogether insufficient, in comparison with returns that have been available on other investments and in the light of the risks of international air transportation.
Over its entire history the Company has paid dividends that have provided an average yield of 3. 1% on the capital invested by the stockholders. During the post-war period, 1946-1953, dividends have provided a yield of 2.7%.
Over its entire history the Company has earned 6.2% on the capital invested by the stockholders. In the post-war years, 1946-1953, it has earned 5.7% and 53% of these earnings has been plowed back into the business.
From the beginning of operations to December 31, 1953, the Company has plowed back $35,500,000 of earnings (this being 51% of the total) into the business. Since 1945, it has invested approximately $230,000,000 in new aircraft. Over the next few years a sum approaching $100,000,000 will be needed for new aircraft.
These dividends and earnings compare unfavorably with those of American industrial companies, of gas and electric utilities, and of the domestic trunk airlines - despite the Company’s amazing growth and the added hazards of international operation which warrant a higher return. (See Chart No. 13.)
