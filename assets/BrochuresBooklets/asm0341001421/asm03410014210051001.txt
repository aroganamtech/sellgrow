Is it true that the Government gives subsidized air carriers the monies with which to pay their income taxes?
All of an airline’s revenues, including Government subsidy and everything else, go into its bank accounts. It draws on these accounts to pay all of its expenses - wages of its personnel, costs of fuel and other supplies, rentals of facilities, interest on its debt, and income tax. It is no more accurate to say that the Government subsidy pays Pan American’s income tax than to say that cargo shippers pay it.
The impression that the Government makes a specific grant to pay a carrier’s income tax doubtless derives from the fact that in computing the amount of subsidy required, the Civil Aeronautics Board first applies all other revenues to the payment of day-to-day operating expenses. Subsidy is then given, first to supply a deficiency below expenses, and then to supply a return to the stockholders on the capital which they have invested. In computing the amount needed for this purpose it is necessary, of course, to consider what income tax will be payable. Ibis is standard public utility regulatory practice.
As previously pointed out, some 43% of the subsidy returns immediately to the Government by way of income tax payments.
