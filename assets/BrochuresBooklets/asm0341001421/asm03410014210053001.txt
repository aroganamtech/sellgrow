What is the trend of Pan American’s subsidy and what hope is there for its elimination?
Since figures separating subsidy from total mail pay are available only commencing with the fiscal year 1951, comparisons with earlier years must be on the basis of total mail pay. Pan American’s total mail pay for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1954, was $38,879,000 compared with $25,688,000 for the fiscal year ended June 30, 1948, an increase of 51%. ‘However, the ton-miles of service rendered by Pan American during the same period increased from 162,880,000 to 291,636,000, an increase of 79%. Mail pay per unit of service rendered thus decreased 15% in spite of an increase in total mail pay and a decrease in revenue yield due to lowering of passenger fares and cargo rates and in spite of the inflationary spiral which greatly increased operating costs during the 1948-1954 period. (See Charts Nos. 11 and 12.)
Since the fiscal year 1951, Pan American’s subsidy for its transatlantic and transpacific operations, as estimated by the Civil Aeronautics Board, has decreased from $18,388,000 to $15,279,000, and will decrease still further as a result of large movements of military mail. However, subsidy for its Latin American and Alaskan operations has increased from $5,702,000 to $12,712,000 due largely to the introduction of excessive competition.
With the decrease in uneconomic duplication of service in line with the President’s Civil Air Policy Report, still further reduction in subsidy should be possible.
