Spanish has five basic vowel sounds: A (ah) E (eh), I (eeh), O (oh) and U (ooh). All are single sounds, with no slurring or gliding. Diphthongs and tripthongs are combinations of two and three vowels, which are always pronounced according to the individual letters. In the combinations ue and ui, when preceded by q or g, the u is silent.
The majority of the consonants are pronounced as in English. B and V, are pronounced almost alike in Latin American countries. C, before e or i is pronounced like s, as in the English words center and cistern; before a, o, u, it is pronounced like k. Ch, a separate consonant sound, as in chance. Spanish is spoken by syllables. There is only one combination of letters for each sound. Being almost a completely phonetical language, it is written as it is spoken. Spelling contests are unknown in Spanish-speaking countries where school children are given dictation exercises to achieve mastery of the written language.
