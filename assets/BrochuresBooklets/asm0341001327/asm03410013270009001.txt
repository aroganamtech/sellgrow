What does the future hold? Supersonic aircraft able to streak across the skies of tomorrow at speeds up to and above 2,000 miles per hour are no longer a dream of man. Within the next ten years you will be able to fly at Mach 2 (1,450 MPH) — twice the speed of sound . . . aboard Pan Am’s new Concordes. You may also expect to ride at Mach 3 (about 2000 MPH) in a new American built SST (Supersonic Transport). Aboard a new SST, you can start from New York and fly to Tokyo in just four hours and fifteen minutes; Honolulu in three hours; Mexico City in an hour and twenty minutes; Rome in two hours and forty-five minutes; London in two hours and ten minutes. In the future ... as in the past . . . Pan American engineers, designers and technicians will have a hand in aviation’s destiny. The Clippers of tomorrow will meet the same high standards of performance and dependability that Pan Am has always imposed upon all aircraft added to its fleet.
CONCORDE — Pan Am’s supersonic airliner of tomorrow. Scheduled for delivery in the early 1970’s, the Concorde will fly at speeds of about 1,450 miles per hour — twice the speed of sound.
