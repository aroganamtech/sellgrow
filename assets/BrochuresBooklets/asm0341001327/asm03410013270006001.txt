Later, as Pan Am’s technical adviser, Charles Lindbergh flew the route to the Scandinavian countries via Greenland and Iceland. This flight, made in a Lockheed Sirius is not too far removed from the path taken by Pan Am’s West Coast-Europe flights even today.
In 1934, the Sikorsky S-42 ... a flying powerhouse weighing 19 tons and equipped with four engines and a range of 2400 miles joined the Pan Am fleet. This giant Clipper was the first true over-ocean transport.
During the next year the Martin M- 130 also an over-ocean flying boat—was added to Pan Am’s flying fleet. Weather stations in the Pacific were now being set up; and out over the ocean Pan Am dispatched the SS North Haven with a crew of young, adventurous Americans and enough supplies to build bases thousands of miles apart over a string of deserted atolls. Islanders, many of whom had never seen or heard of an airplane, soon were marveling at the huge droning birds passing overhead. It was during this time that Pan Am’s first chief pilot, Captain Edwin C. Musick, joined hands with Gen. Charles A. Lindberg to courageously conduct Pan American's Pacific survey flights to Manila.
When testing was completed and bases opened, the famousChina Clipper-a MartinM-130 took off from San Francisco on November 22, 1935 and disappeared in the clouds. Twenty-two hours later the China Clipper arrived in Honolulu . . . then continued onto bases just constructed on Midway, Wake, Guam and Manila . . . the first flying boat to cross an ocean on a regular scheduled service.
