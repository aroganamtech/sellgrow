Destination Europe.Twenty-six hours later it touched - down to complete the first scheduled commercial passenger flight across the Atlantic—from New York to Lisbon.
With the Pacific and Atlantic conquered, the dream of full ’Round the World service, connecting Pan Am’s eastern and western routes became closer to being a reality.
On June 17, 1947, Captain Hugh H. Gordon, piloting a Pan Am Lockheed “Constellation” inaugurated the first ’Round-the-World civil air service from LaGuardia Airport, New York.
Pan Am continued to pioneer. In 1955, Pan Am opened the way into the commercial jet age by placing the first order for American-built jet airliners. On October 26, 1958, Captain Samuel H. Miller guided his Boeing 707 Jet Clipper down the runway at the then Idle-wild Airport and Pan American inaugurated American flag jet service with a flight from New York to Paris. Flying at speeds close to 600 miles per hour, the new Jet Clippers shrunk the world to half its size . . . bringing continents and people closer together.
Jet travel has changed the face of the world. As stated by Mr. Juan Trippe, “Mass travel by air — made possible by the Jet Age — may prove to be more significant to world destiny than the atom bomb. For there can be no atom bomb more powerful than the air tourist, charged with curiosity, enthusiasm and good will, who can roam the four corners of the world, meeting in friendship and understanding the people of other nations and races.”
