In the Fall of 1956, Pan American World Airways set the stage for the Jet Air Age by being the first U. S. airline to order a fleet of jets — 44 in all. And Pan Am will likewise be the first to operate U. S.-built jet airliners on scheduled flights.
Jet Clippers will fly high — seven, eight miles above the earth . . . too high for the longest fingers of weather to reach. You’ll float smoothly, serenely. The muted sound of the jets will fall quickly behind, as you travel almost at the speed of sound. Yet you’ll feel no sensation of speed or height, rather enjoy a sense of kinship with the intensely blue sky around you. The air outside your window may be 60 degrees below zero. The speed of these huge Jet Clippers will make possible round trips to Europe in a single day. It will be as simple to visit other countries as it is now to travel to neighboring states. You’ll lunch in Paris, have dinner in Manhattan ... or breakfast in San Francisco, lunch in Honolulu.
By giving such easy speed to travel, to communication and to commerce, Jet Clippers will contribute inevitably to the cause of world peace . . . through broadening acquaintance and understanding between people of all nations.
