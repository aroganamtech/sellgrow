THE ENGINE
SIKORSKY S-36
One of the landmarks along that trail was our Sikorsky S-36 . . . the world’s first practical amphibian transport. Capable of carrying 8 passengers at speeds up to 100 m.p.h. (161 kilometers), the S-36 was brought into service partially because it was found that early air travelers felt more relaxed in a plane that could set down on land or water.
But 1929 was the year of the big jump for Pan American from 251 route miles (404 kilometers) to 11,489 (18,500 kilometers). Puerto Rico, Central and South America were now opened up to air travel by planes like the Sikorsky S-38 . . . the first in commercial service to use the variable pitch propeller for added safety and maneuverability. Other Pan Am “firsts” that year included the introduction of multiple flight crews . . . radio operators and stewards, or flight cabin attendants as they were called in those days.
The four engines (JT3D-3W Fan Jet) each provide
18.000	pounds of thrust. This thrust is produced by the blowing of hot expanding gases out of the tail pipe . . . the force created thrusts the plane forward.
As you can see, we’ve come a long way since 1927 ... in the planes we fly and the places we fly them to . . . from ninety miles (145 kilometers) of routes to over
69.000	miles (112,573 kilometers). We’ve done it step-by-step . . . always growing . . . learning from our successes and our mistakes . . . blazing a trail for the advance of commercial aviation all over the world.
