Now, let’s follow these men on a typical long-range flight. In the Dispatcher’s office, the Captain gets the carefully prepared flight plan. He studies it, accepts the plan, and discusses general conditions with the Dispatcher.
Meanwhile, the Flight Engineer makes a pre-flight maintenance check of the airplane. Here, he is checking the fueling of the jet.
After accepting the flight plan, the Captain briefs the crew on weather, the flight plan, and other aspects of the flight. Emergency procedures are reviewed. . . . Flight Service crew is advised on the best conditions and times for serving meals.
In the cockpit, the Flight Engineer is joined by the
rest of the crew and a check and double-check of every instrument, dial, and switch is made, according to a printed check list.
After the passengers are on board, and the doors are closed, the engines are started, one by one.
The Jet Clipper taxis to the edge of the runway. Then, the Captain calls the Control Tower for permission to take-off. When permission is granted, the plane enters the runway, power is applied, and the take-off begins.
