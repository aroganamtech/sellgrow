PERSONAL RELATIONS
In addition to our official dealings with our customers, there are our personal dealings—an area of vital importance to all of us. This concerns the manner in which each Pan Am employee, regardless of his or her function, acts toward the customer so as to create the best possible impression.
Each customer, of course, is a separate individual . . . with his own personal desires, anxieties, needs and so on. But many of these needs and desires are basically the same for all. For instance, they all want—and are entitled to—convenience of transportation facilities . . . comfort during the flight . . . courtesy and competence on the part of Pan American’s personnel.
All of these elements can be summed up in one word: SERVICE. This is the kind of asset that can’t be carried on our books as an item with tangible value . . . but its value is tremendous. THE KIND OF SERVICE WE GIVE CREATES THE REPUTATION OF OUR COMPANY . . . and making sure that our customers get the best service available anywhere is the respon- * sibility of every Pan American employee.
IN YOUR HANDS
In these days of vigorous competition, transportation companies spend a lot of money to advertise what they have to offer . . . but there’s one kind of advertising that can’t be bought for money—“word of mouth” advertising that only you have the power to create . . . through the service you give. You know, from experience, that when you buy something and are particularly satisfied with it, you’ll go back to the same place to buy more . . . and you’ll tell your friends about it.
This kind of “word of mouth” advertising applies to our sales efforts, too. Our salesmen all over the world can tell prospective customers about Pan Am’s superb service features . . . but only you can make their descriptions come true. Pan American’s reputation for service is in your hands.
