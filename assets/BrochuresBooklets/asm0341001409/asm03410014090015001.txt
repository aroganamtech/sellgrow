SIKORSKY S-40
In 1932, we had the Sikorsky S-40, the first commercial four-engine flying boat. It could carry 36 people at 110 m.p.h. (177 kilometers). This plane established the world’s record, in 1932, for the longest over-water route . . . 600 miles! By the end of that year, we were flying out of 29 land airports and 26 marine bases.
SIKORSKY S-42
This is the Sikorsky S-42, America’s first true ocean
air transport capable of flying 2400 miles (3,864 kilometers). We introduced it to service in 1934. This was also the plane in which we made our survey routes for the trans-oceanic routes which we were planning.
CHINA CLIPPER, THE MARTIN M-130
This is the plane that opened up Pacific travel. It flew our first scheduled flights from San Francisco to Manila, in the Philippines. It was the largest transport of the time, capable of cruising 3200 miles (5,152 kilometers) non-stop at 150 m.p.h. (241 kilometers), carrying up to 32 passengers.
YANKEE CLIPPER, THE BOEING 314
This is the largest and the last of our seaplanes, the Boeing 314. It was used to inaugurate our trans-atlantic service in 1939. Like an ocean-liner, it had staterooms . . . including a bridal suite . . . and instead of regular seats, passengers could relax on overstuffed chairs and sofas.
