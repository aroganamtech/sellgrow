First of the Douglas series to join our fleet was the DC-2. This was the forerunner of the phenominal DC-3 the most well-known airliner in history. It is estimated that 500,000,000 commercial passengers have flown in the DC-3. The DC-2 made only a brief appearance in Pan Am and was replaced by the DC-3 which operated for many years in our Latin American Division.
DOUGLAS DC-4
This first pressurized land plane ever used in commercial service enabled us to make the change-over from seaplanes to landplanes, with their advantages of speed, capacity and wider choice of routes and destinations.
CONVAIR 240
After World War II, we used the Convair 240, especially suitable for Caribbean service because of its 1300 mile range (2,093 kilometers) and 40 passenger capacity.
DOUGLAS DC-2
In 1945, we also began flying the Douglas DC-4. It could cruise up to 260 m.p.m. (419 kilometers) with a payload of 55 passengers in first-class service ... or 70 in tourist ... as well as 8,000 pounds of cargo. LOCKHEED L-49 CONSTELLATION
