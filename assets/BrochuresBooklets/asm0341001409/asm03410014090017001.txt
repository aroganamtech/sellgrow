In the Spring of 1949, we took a giant step forward with the Boeing 377—the Stratocruiser. It featured such exclusives as a lower-deck lounge . . . completely private staterooms . . . and sleeperette service for all passengers. As a tourist plane, it could accommodate up to 86 passengers.
DOUGLAS DC-6B
In 1952, we started our fleet of Douglas DC-6B’s. This permitted the extension of tourist service throughout the world.
DC-7B
In 1955, we extended our pioneering with the addition of DC-7B’s. This was followed the following year with the larger DC-7C. The 7C, or Super-7, cruises at 353 m.p.h. (568 kilometers). It has an operating range of 5,600 miles (9,016 kilometers). It was the first commercial aircraft capable of non-stop trans-pacific flight and the first that could guarantee non-stop transatlantic service in both directions.
BOEING 707-321
In 1959, the Boeing 707-321 joined the 707-121 Jet Clipper fleet. It is the long-range version of the 707, with a range up to 5,000 miles (8,050 kilometers). It cruises at almost 600 m.p.h. (963 kilometers), and is in service on our Atlantic, Pacific and Latin American routes.
DOUGLAS DC-8
Also in our Jet Clipper fleet is the Douglas DC-8, powered by JT-4 Pratt & Whitney engines. It carries 23,300 gallons of fuel, and has about the same speed and range as the 707-321. It can carry from 106 to 168 passengers, depending on the classes of service in which it is used.
