In 1955 Juan Trippe led the U.S. airline industry into the jet age by placing the first order for the American-built jet airliners that soon dominated the air fleets of the world. On October 26, 1958 a Boeing 707-121 jet called "Clipper America" inaugurated American-flag jet service from New York to Paris.
Travel grew at a prodigious rate from the end of the war to the late 1960s. In five years of peace, by 1950, Pan Am was carrying a million passengers more than a billion-and-a-quarter passenger miles a year. From then, traffic doubled about every five years till in 1970 the airline carried 11 million passengers nearly 20 billion passenger miles.
Operating revenues, however, grew at a much lower rate, reflecting the ever lowering relative cost of international air travel achieved by more efficient aircraft and expansion of the air travel market base.
In April 1966, with traffic growth exceeding all estimates and airports becoming severely congested,
Pan Am ordered jets of a new order of productivity, the wide-bodied Boeing 747s, capable of carrying up to 490 passengers in all-economy configuration.
Pan Am's $600 million order for 25 of the 747s enabled Boeing to go ahead with construction of the new aircraft, which Pan Am put in service on January 22, 1970 with a flight from New York to London. In April 1976, Pan Am introduced the 747SP, a special performance airliner that for the first time gave commercial aviation nonstop range between New York and Tokyo. The first flight of the 747SP took place on April 25, 1976 when "Clipper Constitution" flew from Los Angeles to Tokyo.
