National was the logical choice. The routes of the two airlines were complementary and, for all practical purposes, non-competitive. Yet the two airlines shared a number of stations, which would lead to operating efficiencies. Their fleets were complementary; Pan Am had the aircraft for the long-haul routes, National had the planes for the medium and short-range routes. Also, the seasonality problems the two airlines had would be alleviated.
All in all, it was felt that Pan Am and National would make ideal partners.
More than 16 months were to elapse between the day Pan Am proposed the merger and the effective date of the merger. Finally in October of 1979 the Civil Aeronautics Board sent to President Carter its recommendation approving the merger.
On December 22, 1979 the President announced his approval of the merger.
