TRADE MAKES A WORLD OF NEIGHBORS
Foreign trade has been defined as the life-blood of international relations. On it, international and national well-being and progress depend.
Yet, in no sector of the economy is the postwar outlook more puzzling than in foreign trade. During the war, shipments to and from foreign countries were enormous. But what of the futute? What will foreign countries want to buy from the United States now and in the years ahead? How much will we import from other countries? Under what conditions will trade be conducted?
On these pages are presented certain facts and figures, together with pertinent quotations from a few leaders in the field of international trade, all bearing on this subject.
Businessmen desiring information on trade development are invited to consult the office of the Foreign Trade Counsellor, Pan American World Airways, 135 East 42nd Street, New York 17, N. Y.
