FrOM HER FARMS, orchards, vast wheat fields and cattle ranges America learned to feed herself (and, later, others); from her forests, mines and quarries she housed herself.
When new fuels were needed, oil fields mushroomed to power automobiles, trains and the planes that now give America the world’s greatest domestic and international air transport systems.
World PROGRESS has been made here—in the automobile, steamboat, airplane, electric light, motion-pictures, telephone, telegraph, medicine—and now in atomic power.
With material advancement, Americans have not neglected cultural achievements. This nation has developed its own literature, distinctive art and architecture, new forms of music and drama, and even (in a sense) its own language.
There are more than 700 universities and colleges, attracting students from every state, and foreign nations as well.
The world’s greatest cities, monumental power dams, Grand Canyon, Niagara Falls, the Great Lakes, Indians and their colorful customs, Wall Street, extensive river systems, mountain resorts, wide beaches—across the face of the land Nature and Man have carved unequaled attractions for the world to see.

PARTICULAR ATTRACTIONS FOR THOSE VISITING THE UNITED STATES
Skyscrapers and business centers, particularly in New York and Chicago. Entertainment and night clubs (stage and screen shows, etc.) in New York, Philadelphia, Boston, Chicago, San Francisco. Symphony orchestras, art galleries and museums in these same cities, plus St. Louis, Minneapolis, Detroit. Educational institutions—varied and numerous.
NEW ENGLAND: Lakes, mountains, forests, fishing, hunting, artists’ "colonies,” quiet villages, humming industrial towns, Boston—“Hub of the Universe.”
MIDDLE ATLANTIC STATES: Big industrial plants, centers of commerce, finance and learning. Washington—the Nation’s Capital.
SOUTH: Cotton and tobacco fields, pine forests, Shenandoah National Park. Once the Confederate States. "Way Down South in Dixie.” Florida—Winter resortland.
MIDDLE WEST: Cattle, corn and wheat lands. "Where the West begins.” The mighty Mississippi—Missouri River system; New Orleans, Mardi Gras.
SOUTHWEST: Indians, cowboys, rodeos. Grand Canyon, petrified forests. Oilwells, deserts. Memories of prospectors, the Pony Express and outlaws. Boulder Dam.
MOUNTAIN STATES: The towering Rockies, trail of the "49ers,” copper and silver. Great national parks such as Yellowstone.
THE PACIFIC COAST: Oranges, palm trees, Hollywood premieres, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Rose Bowl football tournament, the ancient redwood trees.
THE NORTHWEST: Lumbering, orchards, fisheries, Seattle, "gateway to Alaska.”
By Pan American World Airways System, tens of thousands yearly arrive to visit the United States of America.
