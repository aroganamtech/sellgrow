


New York is Pan Am’s home. Standing astride New York’s Grand Central Station, the company’s home office is the world headquarters for operations that stretch some 77,818 miles around the globe, touching six continents.
Better than one-third of the company’s entire labor force is based in New York City. In 1966, the 11,396 Pan Am employees earned $106,777,119.83 or better than $9,370 per person.
Pan Am is proud to be a part of New York, and the company strives to make New York a better place to live or to visit.
In the 12 months of 1966, Pan Am concentrated much of its efforts in promoting New York. For the current year, these efforts are highlighted by a good will tour of major European cities by New York's Summer Festival Queen, Nancy Davison.
Accompanied by an executive of the New York Convention and Visitors Bureau, Summer Festival Queen Nancy flew aboard Pan Am to 11 cities in Europe where she met with 239 travel agents, tour organizers and other travel industry officials in order to interest them in promoting New York as a place to visit.
In the following pages, this booklet will illustrate how this and other promotion efforts undertaken by Pan Am on behalf of New York, helps to support the city's second largest industry—the tourist industry.
