Pan Am is now able to grant stop over privileges in New York to its round-the-world passengers or to other passengers who pass through New York along their route, as a result of a decision by the Civil Aeronautics Board.
In addition, because New York is the hub of the air transport industry in the United States, Pan Am carries many passengers from Latin America and the Caribbean to New York enroute to Europe or points in the United States or Canada.
These passengers arrive or depart aboard one of the 547 flights that utilize the Pan Am terminal at John F. Kennedy airport each week. In the past year, Pan Am carried 355,719 persons to New York City from Europe alone.
The New York Convention and Visitors Bureau estimates that 930,000 visitors from overseas stopped off in New York during a visit to the United States in 1966. Of this number, Pan Am carried some 230,000.
In addition to the tour of the Summer Festival Queen, two recent promotions have also made it easier for foreign visitors to gain an understanding of New York.
Pan Am is preparing for distribution at United States Travel Service offices and Pan Am ticket offices abroad, over 10,000 copies of city maps of New York City. These maps, printed in five languages, are being distributed in the nations where those languages are spoken.
Each year Pan Am gives hundreds of newspaper writers from various nations authoritative information on New York City during their visits to the United States. These foreign journalists come to New York for first hand experience in viewing the wonders of the New World. Pan Am also brings hundreds of travel agents and Interline airline representatives to New York. In 1966, some 1,000 of these persons were brought to New York to stimulate foreign travel here.
