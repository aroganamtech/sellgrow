CHECKING IN FOR A CHECKUP, new shift at Hangar 14 is briefed on all work to be completed on Jet Clipper receiving routine maintenance going-over. Since as many as 100 maintenance men may be working on a plane at the same time, scheduling is an important factor in the massive operation. All work is planned so that everyone finishes at the same time, permitting the Clipper to be placed back in service as quickly as possible.
The object of Maintenance at Pan Am is to keep everything working perfectly all the time. Parts are inspected frequently, on a regular schedule, so that any necessary repairs or replacements can be accomplished before malfunctions occur. Pan Am has more than 8,400 highly-trained mechanics and technicians at airports around the world to keep its Clippers flying safely and swiftly.
At Kennedy International Airport alone, Pan Am has more than 3,500 trained employees in its Maintenance Department. More than 2,800 of these are mechanics and servicemen, under the direction of 300 supervisor-experts licensed by the United States Federal Aviation Agency.
The FAA is the government agency which sets the rigid standards for airplanes and airline personnel, and Pan Am is proud to say that in many cases its standards are even higher than those of the FAA.
Most people know that you have to keep an automobile in good mechanical condition if you want it to give you good service and get you where you want to go without time-and-money-consuming trouble. Cars need gas, oil, grease and water; they must have good tires and brakes, and windshield wipers that work when you need them. And they must have a motor that gives reliable performance. But most people don’t pay much attention to the mechanical workings of their cars until something goes wrong... until something breaks down and the car won’t go.
