DOUGLAS DC-7C
The last of the piston-engine Clippers to join the Pan Am fleet, the DC-7C is the longest-range, most modern piston-engine aircraft in airline service. With an operating range of over 4,000 miles, and fuel capacity of 7,860 gallons, it is capable of nonstop flight across the Atlantic in either direction in any wind conditions. The DC-7C has a maximum takeoff weight of 143,000 pounds. Pan Am also operates a fleet of DC-7B’s, an earlier model, and a large fleet of DC-6B’s, for many years the backbone of Pan Am’s long-range services.
BOEING 707
Pan Am was the first airline to order and operate U.S.-built commercial jet aircraft. The Boeing 707 Jet Clipper made its maiden flight on October 26, 1958 and today dominates the world’s air routes. Pan Am operates several types of Boeing 707 in passenger service—the 707-100, the first model built; the 707-300 Intercontinental; the 720B, powered by fan-jet engines; and the fan-jet 707-320B, which has a range of 6,000 miles, making it capable of flying longer and further than any other aircraft in aviation history. The 707-100 aircraft, refitted with fan-jet engines, also have extremely long range. 707-300 Intercontinental Jet Clippers have a wing span of 142 feet, five inches; and are 152 feet, 11 inches long. They have a fuel capacity of 21,160 U.S. gallons, giving them a range of 5,300 miles. Maximum takeoff weight for the 707-300 is 312,000 pounds. They can carry as many as 187 passengers and a crew of ten.
