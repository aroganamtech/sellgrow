For every hour a Jet Clipper spends in the air, it receives 33 man-hours of maintenance. There are often as many as 100 mechanics working on the same plane at the same time.
The varied equipment they maintain is the best that money can buy. And it’s expensive. Here are a few examples: Each jet engine costs $250,000. Each tire costs $350, and each plane has ten. These are recapped about once a month, and each recapping costs $80.
Each DC-8 seat costs $4,400, a Boeing 707 seat $1,250. The reading light, stewardess call button, air inlet and emergency oxygen supply system are mounted on the rear of each DC-8 seat, whereas in the Boeing 707 they are located in the aircraft ceiling, accounting for the difference in the cost of the seats. Carpeting in the cabin costs $1,500. In fact the “living room” in a DC-8 arranged for first-class passengers is worth almost $100,000. And the two galleys (or kitchens] on a Jet Clipper cost $8,000 apiece.
