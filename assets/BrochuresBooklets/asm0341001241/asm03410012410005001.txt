QUANTITY, quality and variety all await your pleasure in Southern California theatres this month. Such hits as Dear Ruth, Snafu, Blithe Spirit and Blackouts of 1945 tread the boards; the brilliant San Francisco Opera Company visits its sister city for 10 days, and motion picture theatres offer glittering premieres. Following are scheduled theatrical highlights for November. Bookings often change at the last minute, so it’s a good idea to check with the box-office before venturing out. Phone numbers are included for your convenience.
BLACKOUTS of 1945. A fast-moving revue now enjoying its fourth popular year. Smooth-talking Ken Murray and smooth-looking cinemalovely Marie Wilson co-star. At the El Capitan Theatre, 1735 N. Vine (GRanite 1147).
BLITHE SPIRIT. A slick drawing-room comedy with typical Noel Coward wit. Provocative plot has our hero, a widower, remarrying and then playing bewildered host to an uninvited guest-—the ghost of his first wife. Ensuing entanglements are hilarious. Nov. 14 through 25. At the Pasadena Playhouse, 39 S'. El Molino, Pasadena (RYan 1-6418).	M|
DEAR RUTH. A happy comedy about a bobby-soxer who writes letters to an Air Corps lad and signs her big sister’s name. Lad comes home to amusing and romantic complications. Through Nov. 21. At the Biltmore Theatre, 520 W. Fifth (TUcker 7131).
ELSA LANCHESTER AND THE YALE PUPPETEERS.
A series of comedy marionette plays by the Yale Puppeteers on one stage, then coffee at intermission and turn about for a musical performance starring Elsa Lanchestfer. No performance Monday. Indefinite run. At the Turnabout Theatre, 716 N. LaCienega (CRestview 6-1005).
JANSSEN SYMPHONY ORCHESTRA. Under the celebrated
direction of Werner Janssen, this deservedly popular orchestra presents an evening of music by Beethoven on Nov. 18. At the Wilshire-Ebell Theatre, Eighth and Lucerne (WYoming 7095).
LAS POSADAS. The annual holiday production of the Mexican Players opens Nov. 21 and continues throughout the month. In connection with the play, picturesque Yuletide customs of old Mexico are revived. At Padua Hills Theatre, Claremont (CLaremont 6981).
LAY OF THE LAND .Farce-comedy about two sailors on furlough. Alane Miller stars and Warren Mills shines in a comedy role. Indefinite run. At the Beaux Arts Theatre, 1701 W. Eighth St. (FItzroy 9300).
UNDO MEXICO. Title means beautiful Mexico, and a program of Mexican songs and dances in typical costumes proves the veracity of the title. Through Nov. 17. At the Padua Hills Theatre, Claremont (CLaremont 6981).

MOTEL WIVES. Fairly amusing farce-comedy concerning a pair of “unmarrieds” posing as “marrieds.” In this oft-told tale, it’s the housing shortage and patriotism that bring about this indecorous conduct. Margie Bennett is the comely wench, Sidney Melton the handsome hero. Indefinite run. At the Musart Theatre, 1320 S. Figueroa (PRospect 6644).

SNAFU. In case you’re wondering, the title is Army parlance interpreted as “situation normal—all fouled up.” Diverting nonsense arises out of complications via the return of a 16-year-old GI after a year of Jap-killing in the Pacific. The actors do a good job. Through Nov. 21. At the Pasadena Playhouse, 39 S. El Molino, Pasadena (RYan 1-6418).
THE BLACKGUARD. New-fashioned melodrama a la Olsen and Johnson. Impersonations, beer and peanuts part of the fun. Indefinite run. At the Gateway Theatre, 4212 Sunset (NOrmandy 9495).
THE DRUNKARD. In its 12th year and still holding its glow. You enjoy beer and pretzels in a music hall atmosphere while hissing the villain and applauding Neely Edwards, jolly emcee. Indefinite run. At the Theatre Mart, 4049 Clinton (NOrmandy 2-1121).
VAUDEVILLE. First-run motion pictures in addition to name vaudeville acts. This is the last home of vaudeville in Los Angeles. At the Orpheum Theatre, Ninth and Broadway (Michigan 6272).
