On the evening of the 10th, ice hockey gets under way at the Pan-American Auditorium with games planned for every Wednesday and Saturday night throughout the Winter.
The Southern California Open Golf Tournament comes off at Fox Hills Country Club on Nov. 2, 3, and 4, and Thanksgiving week-end the Los Angeles Yacht Club is sponsoring a regatta—the Fall season for Star boats in Los Angeles Harbor.
Oh, yes, and about that skiing—there is the possibility of some Winter sports in the high (and nearly high) Sierras where, as early as October last year, there was excellent skiing, particularly in the vicinity of Big Bear. Better check Van Dergriff’s Ski Hut, 717 W. Seventh St. (VAn Dyke 6275) for day-to-day snowfall reports.
For indoor diversion you have wide choice, whether you’re feeling frivolous or culture-conscious.
The San Francisco Opera Company will hold forth at the Shrine Auditorium from Nov. 1 through the 10th, with Salome, La Boheme and Carmen some of the popular operas to be presented. Gaetano Merola will wield the baton for L’Heure Espagnole and Aida. The Philharmonic Auditorium has Thursday evening and Friday afternoon pairs of symphony concerts penciled in the date books, as well as the Don Cossacks Chorus on the 13th and Vladimir Horowitz, the pianist, on the 16th. The New York stage hit, Dear Rath, is the Biltmore Theatre offering for the first three weeks in November.

PARKS
A S IS to be expected of a city with such beautiful natural •^•settings, Los Angeles abounds in parks. The following list is for the aid of sun and nature lovers. Most parks are open from 7 in the morning until dusk.
ARROYA SECO PARK, Arroya Seco Parkway. There are 276 acres in a narrow strip that follows the Arroya Sec, or dry riverbed, with tennis courts, bowling greens and miles of bridle trails in wooded, natural settings.
CABRILLO BEACH PARK, San Pedro. Municipally operated beach park, with children’s playgrounds, free dressing rooms, boathouse and marine museum.
ECHO PARK, Glendale Blvd. at Temple. Interesting for its tropical and semi-tropical trees, and an 8-acre lake with an island with picnic tables and free gas stoves for picnic parties. There are swans and water fowl, and a fine display of pink and white water lilies.
ELYSIAN PARK, main entrance, N. Broadway, at Los Angeles River. There are 600 acres of wooded preserve, public picnic grounds, winding trails and vantage points for a panorama of the city below.
EXPOSITION PARK, S. Figueroa St. between Exposition Blvd. and Santa Barbara. Sunken rose gardens have 15,000 bushes and 118 varieties. There are 2 museums—the Los Angeles County Museum of History, Art and Science with its Anthropology Hall and collection of prehistoric mammal skeletons from La Brea tar pits, and the State Exposition building. Other attractions: Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum which seats 105,000 spectators, State Armory Building and Los Angeles Swimming Stadium, with grandstand to seat 5000.
FOREST LAWN MEMORIAL PARK, Glendale Blvd. and San Fernando Rd. Privately owned—its chapels, Wee Kirk o’ the Heather and Little Church of the Flowers, are favorite marrying places for the movie stars. Interesting statuary and landscaping.
GRIFFITH PARK, Los Feliz Blvd. and Riverside Dr. More than 4000 acres, mostly in its natural state, with zoo, playgrounds, Greek theatre, observatory and planetarium, 3 public golf courses, archery ranges, bridle paths, picnic grounds and provisions for most sports.
HANCOCK PARK, Wilshire Blvd., near Fairfax. Site of La Brea tar pits, world’s greatest source of Pleistocene Age and Glacial Age animal relics.
LAFAYETTE PARK, Alvarado and Wilshire. Nicely landscaped small park which adjoins through a tunnel under Wilshire Blvd., the larger MacArthur Park (formerly Westlake).
LINCOLN PARK, Valley Blvd. and Mission Rd. Picnic and playgrounds.
MacARTHUR PARK, Alvarado, Sixth to Wilshire. This has been famous on picture postcards for many years as Westlake Park. There’s 1	a lake for boating, and miles of walks and lawns for enjoying the fine
trees and flower gardens.
PERSHING SQUARE, between Hill and Olive, Fifth and Sixth Sts., in heart of downtown Los Angeles. Its benches are palm-shaded and there is always a crowd—especially since the war and the erection of various service men’s headquarters.
SYCAMORE GROVE, Figueroa and Ave. 49. Favorite spot for most of the State society meetings and annual outdoor picnics. There are accommodations for 28,000 persons in the park’s landscaped 15 acres.
