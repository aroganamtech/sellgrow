TTERE is a list of some of the places visitors most frequently ■*"®"ask to see. or that residents like best to recommend.
CHINATOWN. Old Chinatown, near Alameda St. This is the original which was partially torn down to make room for Union Depot a few years ago. Has some of the best places in which to buy nice old Chinese things. New Chinatown, N. Broadway and College St. Run by the younger generation Chinese, it is modern and showier, with neon-lighted pagodas to give it a carnival air. There’s Chinese food in several good restaurants, curios and gimcracks in the stores It’s liveliest at night. China City, bounded by Ord. Main, Macy and New High Sts., is popular with visitors too.
CITY HALL, Civic Center. Is the city’s tallest building—464 feet. Observation balcony on twenty-fifth floor affords a good view of the city and surroundings. Open weekdays 9:30 to 4. Admission free.
EXPOSITION PARK, Figueroa, between Exposition and Santa Barbara. Includes Memorial Coliseum where 105,000 spectators watch football and special events, the county museum and art galleries (including Anthropology Hall’s collection of prehistoric animals from La Brea tar pits) and the permanent collections of the State Exposition Building. Other attractions: Sunken Gardens with 15,000 rose bushes, extensive bowling greens, landscaped walks, tennis courts, swimming stadium and playground. University of Southern California campus adjoins the north side.
FOREST LAWN MEMORIAL PARK, 1712 Glendale Ave. An
unusual 220-acre burial ground which substitutes beauty and art treasures for tombstones. Its Wee Kirk O’ the Heather and Little Church of the Flowers are favorite wedding chapels—particularly used by radio and movie stars.
GRAUMAN'S CHINESE THEATRE, 6925 Hollywood Blvd.
A fantastic Oriental structure famous for its star-spangled movie premieres and interesting to visitors for the slabs of concrete in its forecourt where stars have impressed foot and other prints in soft cement on gala openings. Among the prints are Joe E. Brown’s mouth, the Barrymore profile and Betty Grable’s legs.
GRIFFITH PLANETARIUM, in Griffith Park. Has a mammoth
Zeiss-built machine which projects the starry heavens on a huge convex dome, with accompanying lectures. Open Friday, Saturday and Sunday 2 to 10. Admission: adults, 500, service personnel and children 25^. A science museum and observatory are housed here as well.
HOLLYWOOD BOWL, Highland and Cahuenga. A 59-acre natural amphitheatre and home of Symphonies Under the Stars, operas and similar events. The great sound shell on its stage lends itself to spectacular lighting effects. Symphonies and popular concert attractions are scheduled throughout the summer months.
OLVERA STREET, opposite the old Plaza, between Macy and Marchessault Sts. This is a block-long bit of Old Mexico atmosphere. There are candle-dipping and Mexican handicraft shops, concessions with bright native hats, shawls and shoes. Two cafes, La Golondrina and the Caliente, feature Mexican music, liquor and food. Afternoon and evening the place is lively with crowds in high fiesta mood.
