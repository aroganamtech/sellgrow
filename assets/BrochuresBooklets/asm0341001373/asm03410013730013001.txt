In most cases whoever does the inviting for drinks or lunch picks up the check. Use your own judgement in extending invitations. But if you are the guest, don't attempt to pay the bill .. . your host may be offended. Simply reciprocate by inviting your host to be your guest next time. Drinks taken at the bar before or after dinner are usually totaled on a single check. If you are the host, you will be expected to pay the entire check. If you are the guest, your host will pay — but you may wish to offer to share the cost.
SELLING YOURSELF
Businessmen in the U.S.A. may appear to conduct their affairs at a much faster pace than you are accustomed to. They do not trouble themselves with details, especially during preliminary talks. Rather they are interested in the "big picture” — and with what may seem to be undue swiftness,they are usually anxious to come to an agreement. For this reason, you must be prepared to step-up your own business pace ... to present your case quickly and be ready for rapid-fire questions that often may probe deeper than you would like.
Small things often make a big difference in the impression you are making. For instance, having a knowledge of your host's own business — and complimenting him about it. If you are fortunate enough to be invited to an executive’s home either overnight or for a weekend, show an interest in his hobby — most Americans have one of which they are intensely proud. Be willing to participate in active weekend activities, many of which may be more zestful than you'd like . . . golf, bowling, tennis, swimming . . . and that popular American activity, the "cookout” or barbecue.
