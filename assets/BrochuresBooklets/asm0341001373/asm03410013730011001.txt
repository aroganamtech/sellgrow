Depending on the establishment, 150 to 250 is the standard tip for checking your hat and coat. At a hotel, the doorman receives 250 if he obtains a taxi for you — bellboys expect 250 per piece of luggage. In a barbershop, tip about 350 for a haircut. It is not necessary to tip a chambermaid in a hotel unless you stay for an extended period; then 250 per night on departure is proper. See Pan Am’s New Horizons USA for variances in these general rules according to sectional customs.
CLOSING AN AGREEMENT
If you are unsure of important legalities relating to contract agreements and procedures, you may wish to have a lawyer supervise your new business arrangements. Your own lawyer may be able to recommend one or more lawyers in the United States. If not, you may inquire at your National Consulate or Embassy.
INTERPRETERS
You will find few American businessmen who are multi-lingual — or at least few who are able to carry on a productive business discussion in a language other than English. If you have need of an interpreter, the Pan Am office nearest to the area you are visiting will direct you to a licensed interpreter, (A bi-lingual stenographer, too, if you need one.)
THE SOCIAL SIDE OF BUSINESS
Although it varies with individuals, American businessmen are more inclined to mix business with pleasure than their counterparts in other parts of the world.
The business luncheon, for example, can be an elaborate affair — complete with a number of cocktails, a heavy meal and lingering discussions over coffee.
