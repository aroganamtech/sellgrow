Many firms, especially those involved in the sale of retail goods, are open for business six days a week — but you rarely find an upper echelon executive in his office on Saturday. The weekend is a cherished time for rest and relaxation for most Americans, so you should probably plan to “do as the Romans" — set Saturday and Sunday aside for your own enjoyment. Banking hours are generally 9 to 3, Monday through Friday.
BUSINESS TITLES
The most common form of address in the United States is the simple “Mister" prefixed to the surname. Titles such as “Director" or “President" are almost never used in conversation. Unless the individual concerned is a current or former political or military figure (Senator Smith or General Jones), titles are considered unnecessary — even pompous — in most business circles. Some PhD's, of course, prefer to be addressed “Doctor" — but these are rare. Members of the Medical Professions, on the other hand, should always be called “Doctor." As for first names — you may be surprised at the American willingness to drop formalities quickly. You may hear “Call me George ... or Henry" after only a few minutes conversation. But do not call a businessman by his first name unless he asks you to. If he does, it is a good sign that you have made a favorable impression. Your business prospects are bright!
BUSINESS DRESS
Don't burden yourself with an elaborate wardrobe. Travel light, bearing in mind air-travel baggage limits and the pace of your business-hopping around the United States. Pick up any needed extra items of clothing and accessories as you go along.
