But your host will undoubtedly initiate the gesture. You’ll find that some executives prefer to merely nod or indicate where you should be seated. Generally, an interview is closed with a handshake — and in this case, it is up to you to make the first move. Should you meet an individual several times during the same day, a handshake is not expected at each meeting and parting . . . simply a word of greeting or farewell. One does not usually shake hands with a woman in a business situation. But if you are a guest in her home, the American woman will expect a gentle handclasp at first meeting.
BUSINESS CARDS
Take a large supply of business cards to the United States with you. The cards should be simple, preferably engraved, printed in easily-readable type. Your name centered and your title, firm name and address in the lower left is all that should appear on the card. Do not put anything of an advertising nature on it, except perhaps the year your firm was founded. A long-established business commands respect in the United States. Providing secretaries and receptionists with your business card is a good practice; it will help them when the time comes to introduce you to their employers. And don't be too surprised if an American business executive takes several seconds to study your card closely. Strangely enough, even though the U.S. is the “great melting pot” made up of descendants of practically every national group, some Americans find unfamiliar names difficult to pronounce.
