A plane for all weather
The Douglas DC-7B — which joined Pan Am in 1955 —was built for long hauls and unpredictable weather conditions.
It could cover 3800 miles nonstop ... with 50 to 72 passengers, plus mail and baggage. For extra safety, it carried reserve fuel for another 1,000 miles. Its sister ship, the "Super-7" (or DC-7C) was even more powerful. It could take the Atlantic or Pacific in one nonstop hop in either direction, regardless of weather or headwinds — being the first commercial plane that could! Its cruising range — at 353 MPH — was 5000 miles.
Its four revolutionary Wright turbocompound engines captured and used
the energy of escaping gases. The fuel supply, 7,860 gallons, would power the average car 6 times around the world. Its heating system would warm 60 average-sized houses.
