Off to war!
Soon after World War II broke out in Europe in 1939, Pan Am Clippers were crowded to capacity with diplomats, refugees and thousands of pounds of high priority mail.
When the U.S. entered the war, only 11 great trans-oceanic Clippers, nine Boeing 314s and two Martin 130s were available to carry men and supplies to battle fronts and build-up areas. (Altogether, Pan Am at this time had 148 planes of varying size, but only 11 transoceanic.) Doing the biggest overseas flying job of any airline contractor to the Armed Services, Pan Am flew a total of 346,000,000 miles!
For Pan Am, there were many moments of excitement, pride and satisfaction. For example, the Dixie Clipper flew President Roosevelt to Africa for one of the most momentous conferences of the war. Another highpoint was the legendary round-the-world flight of the Pacific Clipper. With a 10-man crew, she left San Francisco on December 2,1941, destined for Auckland, New Zealand. She was to return to San Francisco in two weeks, but December 7th and Pearl Harbor intervened. It was a year and 5 months before the Pacific Clipper returned home, after a history-making circle of the world.
