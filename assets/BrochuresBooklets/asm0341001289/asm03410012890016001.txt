Warehouse in the sky
The Douglas DC-6A all-cargo Clipper could move anything from orchids to elephants. In addition to a capacity larger than a railroad freight car, it boasted huge loading doors, fore and aft. The cabin was temperature-controlled and pressurized to protect delicate cargoes such as plants and baby chicks.
Quick-change artist
This DC-6B (or "SUPER-6") passenger version of the DC-6A had a convertible cabin. It could be changed in a few hours from an 88-passenger tourist plane to a deluxe 44-passenger plane with lounge and bar. All in all, a big boon to Pan Am's expanding worldwide tourist service.
Vital statistics: wing span — 117V2 feet; length 105V2 feet; gross weight 107,000 pounds. Four Pratt and Whitney 2500-horsepower engines gave it a cruising speed of 313 miles-an-hour, an operating range of 3700 miles, and a normal operating altitude of 16,000-22,500 feet.
