Getting the jump on the Jet Age
Delivery in 1958 of the first Boeing 707 Clippers — and Douglas DC-8 Clippers (in 1960) — gave Pan Am the country's first commercial jet transports ... as well as a big jump on the Jet Age.
Almost overnight, a number of longstanding performance records were shattered. For instance, cruising speed shot up to 575 miles-an-hour. Operating altitude, to somewhere between 25,000-35,000 feet. Range, to over 5000 miles. Passenger capacity, to over 140 (over 145 for the 707).
By 1959, a long-range version of the 707 — designated the Boeing 707 Intercontinental Jet Clipper — had joined the
Pan Am fleet. With beefed-up engines of 15,800 pounds thrust apiece, the "Intercontinental" stretched the 707's operating range to 5500 miles. It carried up to 177 passengers.
Three years later, Pan Am brought in the Super Intercontinental Jet Clipper — longest-range commercial jet aircraft of its day. Powered by four turbofan engines —18,000 pounds of thrust apiece — this Boeing 707 had a range of 6000 miles and cruised at 575 MPH.
No doubt about it, the new turbofan engines delivered more power than any of their predecessors, were more efficient and reliable. They could carry greater payloads, take off from shorter runways.
And because the new engines had more cruise thrust per gallon of fuel, turbofan-powered Jet Clippers were ideal for both long and short trips.
