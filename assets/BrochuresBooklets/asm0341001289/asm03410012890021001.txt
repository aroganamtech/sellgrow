The mechanics of fast cargo handling
As aircraft speed has increased, so has the need for rapid handling of cargo become vital to practical operations. To that end, Pan Am has been developing ever-more-efficient means of handling cargo on the ground and of loading and unloading aircraft.
The two top photos below illustrate some of the means of speeding cargo through Pan Am's automated terminal at Kennedy International Airport in New York. In the upper left photo, a pallet-load of cargo (loaded for a particular destination and compatible with standard truck beds and with highway requirements of the major countries served by Pan Am)
proceeds along an automated runway. The giant console at right controls the speed and direction of all the automated runways that carry cargo through the terminal.
The lower left photo shows a pallet-load of cargo being lifted to cargo Clipper deck height by a scissor-lift equipped with a roller transfer platform. Smoothly, effortlessly, the pallet is glided aboard, rolled into position along the roller and ball-bearing deck of the Jet Freighter (right photo) to be secured in place with quick-snap locks. Loading an entire Jet Freighter, with as much as 40 tons of cargo, takes less than an hour.
Unloading operations are much the
same, with a total result of significantly reduced ground time, an absolute necessity with today's tightened schedules.
