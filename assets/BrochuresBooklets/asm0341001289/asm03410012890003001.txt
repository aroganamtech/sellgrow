Magnificent and invincible, the sailing Clipper ships made their debut during a bitter world struggle for trade. And from the China coast to the mainland of Europe, they turned the 19th Century seas into a crossroads for American traders.
With their high, raking masts, long black hulls and knife-sharp bows, the Clippers violated all the established rules of ship design. Yet they literally raced away with the lion's share of world commerce.
Nothing could touch them. They could carry more trade faster than any of their rivals. In fact, some transoceanic Clipper records have not to this day been surpassed by any type of sailing vessel.
The Clipper story was to be repeated within a century... this time in the arena of international air commerce. The time? The 1930's.
Pan American, having initiated Caribbean air service (to Havana) in 1927, aimed at developing a fleet of air Clippers comparable to the sailing ships of yesteryear ... at building a truly international airline.
And from the launching of the "American Clipper" to the present day, Pan Am® Clippers have become as well known around the world as their famous namesakes.
This is the story of history's second great Clipper fleet... and how it came to be.
