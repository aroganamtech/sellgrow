A turbine in the exit path, turning like a water wheel, taps energy from the escaping exhaust gases, and transmits this energy forward through a shaft to drive the compressor. The fan, serving as the front element of the compressor, handles all the air taken into the engine. Some of this air passes aft to the combustion and turbine sections, but the greater portion is partially compressed and immediately ejected through rearward-facing ports near the front of the engine. This relatively cool fan discharge air represents the greater thrust-generating potential of the turbofan engine as compared to its predecessor, the turbojet. Thus, for longer flight range, greater cruising speeds, and shorter take-off distances, the trend is TO THE TURBOFAN.
