*’Twas the month of July, as I recall,
In the summer of thirty-six When they shoved her into the hangar,
A plenty tough job to fix.
The graybeards moaned and shook their heads At that shambles of rusty steel,
’Til suddenly up stepped a lad By the name of Herman Thiel.
“I’ll fix her, Sirs,” he bravely cried,
“I’ll make the blamed thing go.
By the Great Horn Spoon and Alice the Goon And the seven winds that blow.
“You get me four new Hornets,
And I’ll call my laddies gay,
And we’ll build you a blooming airplane If it takes ’til Judgement Day.”
So he gathered his lads about him At that heap of whitened bones.
There was Fisher and Chapman and Sawyer And Swazey and Jerry Jones.
There was Mertens and his boy Sammy, Humdinger and Teddy Mayes.
Month in, month out, they slaved and toiled In divers and sundry ways.
The lads all grew to manhood As proper young men do.
And they sent their children’s children To work on 822.
Out of the East at morning,
The rays of the sun were cast
And they wore a ditch in the hangar floor
Where their daily shadow passed.
The slaves were freed and Lincoln shot And the “horseless carriage” came.
Like a winged sphinx 822 She sat there just the same.
And some day Fate may smile on me And send Romance my way With wedding bells, a blushing bride And monthly bills to pay.
And I won’t send my son to sea To sail the bounding blue I’ll send him down to Dinner Key To work on 822.
Ye End—By, Anonymous
Note to Mr. Anonymous:
Now I’m just an old broken down pilot And can’t crack so wise as you But this poetical stuff is the kind of guff That delayed NC Eight-Twenty-Two.
By H. W. T.
