We ducked into the hills to see Squaw Valley, famed home of the 1960 Olympics Games, and did a fly-over the north shore gambling places—Cal-Neva, Crystal Bay, and Nevada Lodge—half hidden in the trees. A look south down the point of land on which those casinos are situated, letting the eye travel the length of the lake, one can see the imaginary line which marks the boundary between California on the west and Nevada on the east. The gambling spots at both shores are safely over the line into Nevada.
On the way back to the airport we passed Fallen Leaf Lake, the northern part of which is bounded by thick evergreen forests, and the southern portion is buttressed by Mount Tallac.
Again, the flight had taken about an hour, charges based on $25 an hour.
The last plane flight was out of Redding Municipal Airport, when we made arrangements with the Shasta Flight Service to fly us over the Shasta Lake region. And again our plane was Cessna.
One look from “upstairs” convinces you that the only way to see Shasta Lake is from the air. It is multi-armed, with many fingers and scores of coves. By boat it would have taken us days to see it, and we still wouldn’t have comprehended the topographic layout as we did from a plane.
In addition to the Lake, we had close-up air views of Shasta Dam, Castle Crags, and Mt. Shasta, and off to the west the Trinity Alps.
Once more it had taken just about an hour, with the plane fare priced at $22 an hour.
Out of the three week’s vacation trip we had flown en route over three choice sections, at a cost just under $75. It had been well-worth the expense.
