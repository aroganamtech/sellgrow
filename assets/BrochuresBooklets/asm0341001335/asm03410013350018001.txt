REMEMBER
When we started flying the Sikorsky S-42 four-engine aircraft in 1934. Remember the good ship, NC-822M and how she was featured in the news media, travel literature, advertisements, news reels, etc. She was j really the latest word in air travel. But there was another story, unheralded perhaps, about NC-822M that still rings a bell with many of us.
In those days, overhaul of a flying boat was practically rebuilding her from the keel up. Skin and stringers were replaced, struts and fittings machined or replaced, equipment completely refurbished, overhauled engines installed and she went out like a new ship. It was a time consuming task and “standby” was a word not yet in our vocabulary, for when things got slack, you just went over and started to work on the spare airplane—in this instance, good old 822M!
Someone who preferred to remain nameless (particularly after an “ad lib” by the then Division Manager) » wrote a poem. It may not go down in literature as a classic of the time, but it has helped to keep the memory of old NC-822M alive. The story can best be told by the attached:
YE BALLAD OF 822
