HAPPENING IN HUNTINGTON
The Management Club of New York held its Annual Fall Dinner Dance at the Huntington Town House on Long Island on December 5, 1969. An estimated 1,200 Pan Am’ers and their spouses danced to the great music of the Wayne Foster Band.
A new dance sensation was introduced at the affair called the “Pan Am.” Judging from the response received that evening, it’s sure to be an overnight success.
The evening was highlighted by a unique presentation made by Dick Robinson, Check Engineer—JFK. Accepting for Pan Am was Vice President-Maintenance, Jim Weesner. The presentation was a tribute to Pan Am “in appreciation for continuing gainful employment in my twenty-fifth year of service.” It should be noted that Mr. Robinson in addition to being one of Pan Am’s finest Check Engineers is a very talented sculptor. Working in many mediums, his particular specialty is abstract and junk metal art. His talent has won him 1st place at the Pan Am Art Exhibit—JFK for the second year running. His gift was a globe-like metal structure which was his interpretation of Pan American and its World Route Dominance. It was eight months in the making between trips. The structure will be on display either at the Pan Am building or Mr. Weesner’s office until its permanent home, at the new Jet Center, is completed.
Certainly a very unusual yet inspiring way of saying “Thanks” to the Company he has served for the past twenty-five years.
