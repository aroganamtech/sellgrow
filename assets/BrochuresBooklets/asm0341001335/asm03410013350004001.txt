THE FIRST CHALLENGE
On January 10, 1970, at 2:40 a.m., our 3rd Production 747 NC 735 touched down at JFK International Airport.
This aircraft was selected to take some 200 co-emps and their guests to London on a Proving Flight. Announcement of the Proving Flight scheduled for January 12, 1970 at 6 p.m. was heralded around the world, by the news media. All Pan Am departments were humming in preparation for the big day. When all of a sudden, a problem developed on #4 engine while enroute from the UTB to Hangar 14. The problem was further diagnosed and an engine change was deemed necessary.
Now, an engine change on any aircraft is no simple task, but given a fair amount of time and a replacement engine it’s no real problem either.
Here’s where the plot thickens. The only engines at JFK were undergoing buildup, at Hangar 17. To further complicate matters, the engines we had on hand were for NP not NC aircraft. We were confronted with the task of modifying an NP engine on hand by cannibalizing what was needed from the failed engine and using our technological expertise to get the job done. All stops were pulled, Foreman and Mechanics were awakened from their beds and asked to report for work. The task force worked through the day and night and day again but they did it!
Even though a two hour delay was incurred, it’s quite evident to us all that without the brilliant display of Team Work and second effort on the part of our Maintenance Personnel there would have been no Proving Flight of the 12th.
