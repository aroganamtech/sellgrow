Which sights should you see from the air? The scope of your trip, type terrain, your own personal trepidation and that of your family, and the amount of air fares you choose to spend all come into consideration. Obviously, you will select sights that are best seen from the air, sights which will stand out and be meaningful —outstanding cities, lakes, and mountains. There would be no point in flying over long stretches of flat country shoreline, or desert ... or over ground sights that you could hardly make out from 1,500-2,000 feet. And the sights should be interesting enough to warrant the expenditure of localized flights.
Expenses need not be prohibitive. They could be modest, even low-budget. You can fly any portion of an hour. A four-place plane will accommodate pilot, and three passengers, a six-place plane (approximately $10 more an hour) will carry pilot and five passengers.
My wife and I took a low-budget vacation— $150 for three weeks for the two of us, and that included everything—gas, oil, food, camping, sightseeing, and the localized plane flights.
Similar car-plane trips could be made in the Puget Sound country, in the Great Lakes area, through sections of the New England states, and on down the east coast, with Boston, New York, and Washington as the principal cities to see from on-high. There are dozens of other regions where combinations of ground and air travel would make a logical itinerary.
Something to think about, for your next vacation or holiday weekend.
—Howard Jackson
