EATING AND ENTERTAINMENT
Best restaurants are the Dell’Arte, Brasserie, and Silhouette Supper Club, all in the Inter-Continental Hotel (tel: 230561); the Grill Room of the Park Hotel (tel: 230571); the old Briickenkeller (Schiitzenstrasse 6—tel: 284238); da Bruno (Elbe-strasse 15—tel: 233416); the Schloss Hotel Kronberg (Kronbeg/Taunus, Hain Strasse 25—tel: 06173/7011); Alter Haferkasten (Neu-Isenburg, Lowengasse 23—tel: 06102/36059); Heylands Weinstuben (Kaiserhof-strasse 7—tel: 284840); and Zum Grauen Bock (Grosse Rittergasse 30 in Sachsenhausen—tel: 618026). A favorite drink in Frankfurt is Apfelwein (apple cider—ebbelwoi in the local dialect), which can be sampled at taverns in Alt-Sachsenhausen district.
Good bars include the Prolog Bar at the Inter-Continental Hotel; Frankfurter Stubb and Lipizzaner Bar (with dancing) at the Frankfurter Hof; Jimmy’s Bar at the Hotel Hessischer Hof. Nightclubs includeCoupt (Salzhaus 4) and, for jazz, the Jazz-Haus (Kleine Bockenheimer Strasse 12). For typical Bavarian atmosphere try the Maier Gustl’s Bayrisch Zell (Munchener Strasse 57). The weekly Frankfurter Wochenschau gives current theater, sports, and tourist news (in English).
WHAT TO SEE
Frankfurt is equally proud of its international trade fairs and of Goethe, wdiose birthplace is now a museum (Grosser Hirschgraben 23). The highlights of the old part of town are the steeply gabled Gothic buildings on Romerberg Square, including the Town Hall with the Emperor’s Coronation Hall, the Cathedral with its tall 15th-century bell tower, St Leonhards and St Nicholas’ churches, and the Carmelite Monastery, which houses a museum. Frankfurt Zoo is one of the world’s greatest, afternoon concerts and plays are presented here.
Wiesbaden, an international spa in beautiful natural siftroundings, has an active social life and lively casino. The major event is the May Festival, when first-rate opera, ballet, and drama are performed. The baroque castle of the Dukes of Nassau stands on the shores of the Rhine.
