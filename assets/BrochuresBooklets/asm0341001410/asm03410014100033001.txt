Pan American was forging ahead in Latin America, our foreign flag rivals were not resting on their oars. They were rapidly extending their air services to the rich markets of the World. The European airlines had a comparatively easy overland route through Persia and India to the Far East. On the other hand, the United States was separated from Hong Kong, Shanghai, and the markets of the East by the largest ocean in the world — 8,000 miles of the Pacific and three weeks’ travel time. 5 5 5 The ground organization for a PAA trans-Pacific service, however, was already under way. An expeditionary ship sailed from California in 1935, carrying a million separate items to build complete air bases on the desert atols of Midway and Wake and at Guam and Manila. 5 5 5 Nine months later, PAA’s China Clipper was poised on her ramp in San Francisco Bay for take-off. She was the first four-engined aircraft produced anywhere in the world, designed to fly an ocean route. Under the command of PAA Captain Edward
Musick, she departed on schedule, landing at Honolulu, Midway, Wake and Guam, and arrived safely at Manila. 5 5 5 Less than four years later, in 1939, the North Atlantic was bridged.
