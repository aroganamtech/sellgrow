Pan Am’s airline terminal of the future is under way for New York International Airport. Ovalshaped, glass-encased ... with an umbrella-like roof, it will enable passengers to go between car, bus, or taxi and planes without exposure to weather. Curtains of air will replace heavy doors. 1800 cars and taxis can come and go every hour. The terminal will be able to handle a 160-passenger Jet Clipper eveiy fifteen minutes. It is the first of many terminals which Pan Am will build for the Jet Age.

The Phoenicia-Intercontinental, in Beirut, Lebanon (above). The San Salvador, in El Salvador (at left). Hurried by the approach of jet-age travel, Intercontinental Hotels Corporation—a wholly owned subsidiary of Pan American—is building, breaking ground for, or drawing plans for many hotels abroad. This company was founded 10 years ago when a postwar upswing in travel abroad caught many of the world’s leading cities without adequate hotel accommodations. IHC operates exclusively outside this country providing modern comfort for airline passengers and for surface travelers as well.
