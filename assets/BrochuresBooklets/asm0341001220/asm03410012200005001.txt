The elected officials of the districts and cities may have Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, Malayan, English, Yankee, or Polynesian backgrounds—their popularity is due entirely to their worth. The present Mayor of Honolulu, John Wilson, is an Islander with Irish, English, Scotch, Spanish, and Polynesian blood in his veins—he is a scholar, an aristocrat, and is dearly loved. He has been the Territorial Representative to Washington, D. C., has influential connections throughout the world, and heads all the charities and the progressive movements in the Islands.
His wife, a full blooded Polynesian of gracious gifts was very close to the Royal Family, and is often referred to as the, “First Lady” of Hawaii. Mayor Wilson has recently discovered on his farm volcanic clay deposits that bake to an adobe as hard and strong as cement, and is
promoting mass production of this into building blocks, giving all the deposits on his property to this purpose. It should prove to be the best building material the Islands have ever known. Wood is scarce and impractical because of the termites, and the volcanic shale is brittle and rapidly disintegrates.
The Islands are entirely volcanic and are really the tops of a mountain range that reaches 13,800 feet above sea level with more than 10,000 feet below sea level to the base of the range. There are five major islands, five lesser islands, and fifteen islets. They swing in an arc from northwest to southeast a little way below the Tropic of Cancer in mid-Pacific.
