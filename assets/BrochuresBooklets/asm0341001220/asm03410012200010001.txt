The “show place” residences of Honolulu are in the beautiful suburbs around Diamond Head promontory and in the hills that rise back of the city. The financial district with its many banks, insurance and investment offices, and the steamship and airways offices, is as handsome and solid as a corner of London. The architecture of Honolulu is not indigenous or distinctive, but expresses Spanish, English, and American influences. The most thoroughly Hawaiian building is the Academy of Art built only a decade ago, and it has influenced much of the building that has been done since.
The Crossroads is exactly that with a congregation made up of as many Orientals as Occidentals. Church life in Hawaii has become a social factor in the meeting of the races. The Hawaiian festivals derived from the Polynesian traditions are being revived, and the luau is a super duper picnic with the whole pig roasted in a trench of earth lined with palapalai leaves.
In short, life in Hawai takes up where it left off in 1941 with a broader concept of the brotherhood of man than ever before. It is a place of business opportunity and unexcelled recreation, and it is no farther away from us now than the nearest vacation resort was a few years back.
