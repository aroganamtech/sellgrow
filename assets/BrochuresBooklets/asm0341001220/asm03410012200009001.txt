The collections of Oriental art, Hawaiian crafts, painting and sculpture are a surprise to one who has not heard of them. Among the generous donors, the names, Cook, Spalding, Peck, and Allerton are conspicuous. The installation and lighting of the collections is the last word of good taste and modern engineering. A room of 20th Century French painting contains fine examples of most of the modern masters. A Mediterranean Room has outstanding old masters, and the Chinese objects particularly from the Cook Collection are without peers.
In one of the splendid city parks is a recreation pavilion with outstanding mural
paintings by the well-known American painter, Robert Eskridge, who now lives permanently in the Islands. The parks of Honolulu, with their swimming pools and sea bathing facilities available every day of the year are splendidly operated and free to the people.
Waikiki Beach
The life of Honolulu concentrates at Waikiki, with its year round hotels of every class, from modest rooming houses and seaside restaurants to the most fashionable of hotels.

LUAU. In Hawaii the word for picnic is luau. Instead of fried chicken, the guests are served cuts from a whole roast pig, along with pineapple, papaya and other Hawaiian delicacies. The luau is of ancient Polynesian origin, and differs from a picnic in the U. S., in that it generally is staged as a ceremonial feast, or as a party in honor of a distinguished guest.
