Grass skirts are only for the common dances, the green ti leaf skirt is the official one for the ritualistic dances.
For many of the most inspired and noble dances a long trained evening gown is worn. Always leis, generally of orchids or plumaria are hung in abundance over the shoulders, flowers adorn the head, the arms are bare but for bracelets, and the feet are always bare, even with the long trained gowns. The music is generally made by the men in traditional Polynesian costumes, with hand made instruments and much singing.
There is a rhythmic swaying of the hips and a curious movement of the feet, but the dance is concentrated in the arms and hands which are used to describe in fascinating pantomimic motion the story of each song. HULA. The hula is more than a dance with intricate footwork and swaying hips. The words, music and dance together tell a story, and much of the story telling is accomplished through movements of the hands. These movements are formalized, so that each position is immediately intelligible to one familiar with hula tradition.

Hula Dancing
Here one also sees the best hula dancing. The Kona families who perform these dances are the same that did them for the royal courts. Adult and corpulent women are considered as lovely in the hula as the young girls, and often the perfect grace of the dance is not attained short of many, many years of practice.
In fact, to be a good hula dancer, one should be rotund with an abundance of feminine pulchritude; never has well fed beauty had such a perfect opportunity.
