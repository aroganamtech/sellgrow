Pick a nice low fare
Before you sit down to talk with your Agent, think out where you want to go in a general way. You’ll get ideas from this booklet. . . from the many books on travel at your public library . . . from Pan Am’s “New Horizons World Guide.”
Then visit your Travel Agent or the nearest Pan Am office and lay your cards on the table. Time and money limitations ... and how you want to spend both. Not just by naming specific countries and cities, but also by describing the kind of trip you want. Casual. Sporty. Sporting. Or some of them all.
Many times he’ll have been to places you didn’t know about — that would just fit. (That’s another nice part of his business). Naturally, they’re only suggestions for you to think about. But from a man who does this kind of planning every day of the year.
When you’re finished talking, give him some time to work out an itinerary for your approval. You haven’t spent a cent... and you’re armed with concrete information for making your final plans.
Which won’t be hard. If you live in Chicago, for instance, you can travel by Jet all the way to the beach at Waikiki ... and when you’ve had enough sun, return the same way for a total of just $380. From the West Coast Pan Am’s Thrift Class Jet fare is only $200 round trip.
It’s the old truism: the world has shrunk. And with it the cost of getting around to anywhere. Tokyo. Singapore. New Zealand. Tahiti. Now they’re all within easy reach.
What’s more, Pan Am makes it possible to fly to a place like Hong Kong ... and stop off in exotic places along the way — at no extra fare. This means you can pick a destination and then sample the world between here and there. Your trip takes on a new dimension of universality. More about this a little later.
The important thing to remember right now is that you can widen your scope of planning to include all the places you really want to see. Pan Am has seen to it.
