FORTY QUESTIONS

These questions were prepared not only to show the actual knowledge gained by the student, but to encourage individual thought and judgment. By comparison with familiar things, the student will also gain a better understanding of other nations and peoples.
Factual Questions
1.	What language is most generally used in Europe?
2.	Name at least three mountain barriers in Europe. Locate them.
3.	Where are some of the oldest traces of civilization found in Europe? Tell about them.
4.	Locate the Lowlands of Europe. In which country are they found?
5.	Why is the coastline of Europe excellent for commerce? Locate some of the most important harbors.
6.	Name and locate on a map the following:
a.	Great rivers
b.	Capital cities
c.	Important industrial centers.
7.	Locate some important battle fields and invasion points of the last war.
8.	What are some of the important exports to America?
9.	What wild animals are to be found in Europe?
10.	What is the largest city, the smallest country?
11.	Where are the following grown?
a.	Flax.
b.	Olives.
c.	Grapes.
d.	Sugar beets.
12.	Name at least two great art museums. Tell what you might expect to see there.
13.	Explain the meaning of “Buffer States.” Give the names of three nations that come under this meaning.
14.	Name the ocean and seas that touch the coastline of Europe.
15.	Name the highest mountain peak in Europe.
Thought Provoking Questions
1.	Did you enjoy your imaginary trip? Why?
2.	If you could actually fly to Europe, which country would you like to visit? Why?
3.	In what way has the airplane affected life in Europe?
4.	Do you think the airplane will bring about a better understanding between United States and Europe?
5.	What effect did the war have on the relationship between our country and Europe? Do you think we can better this relationship in any way?
6.	How has flying the Atlantic been made safe?
7.	Would you like to study abroad? Do you think the schools are good? What subjects are taught?
8.	What do you know about the United Nations Organization? How can it bring about a better understanding between nations?
9.	How can you yourself help towards a better understanding between the peoples of other nations?
10.	Are the nations of Europe friendly among themselves?
11.	Tell something of the history of Europe and its bearing on our country.
12.	In what way do modern European politics, finances and unrest affect America and the rest of the world?
13.	Which country do we understand best? Why?
14.	Would you like to live in Europe?
15.	Discuss European art and music and its effect upon American culture.
Questions of Comparison
1.	Compare the climate of Europe to that of the United States. Point out on maps sections of this country that are similar to sections of Europe. Different. Explain why.
2.	Compare farming in Europe and America. How does the lack of modern farm machinery effect Europe?
3.	Compare the forests of Europe to those in this country.
a.	Trees
b.	Lumbering
c.	Undergrowth
d.	Preservation
4.	Compare land in general.
a.	Erosion in Greece and this country. What is done about it in both countries?
b.	Mountains of Europe and America.
c.	Population.
d.	Cultivation and waste lands.
5.	Compare the difference between
the lives of American children to those of Europe.
6.	How does the standard of living
vary in each nation and the United States?
7.	Compare the housing situation in various countries to that in the United States.
8.	Do we enjoy the same sports as do the people of Europe? Discuss the following:
Rugby or Soccer vs. Football Cricket vs. Baseball
9.	Compare former modes of travel to the airplane. Tell about the advantages of airplane travel.
10.	Is democracy as practiced in England any different than that of the United States? Compare other governments with ours.
