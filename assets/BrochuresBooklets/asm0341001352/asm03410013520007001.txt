GROUND PERSONNEL are at your service
On the ground, Pan Am personnel operate a world-wide network of radio, radio-telephone and teletype facilities . . . keep in constant 2-way contact with all Clippers in the air. Through PAT (Pan American’s Teleregister) — a central electronic brain — you get instantaneous confirmation of both passenger and cargo reservations to more than 100 global destinations.
You never see Pan Am’s meteorologists, yet ’round the globe, ’round the clock, they’re gathering wind and weather information to furnish the data needed to plot your flight and make it the finest and fastest you ever enjoyed.
Important, too, are Pan Am’s mechanics — who give every Clipper two hours of maintenance for every hour in the air, including complete nose-to-tail overhauls at frequent intervals. And, also “behind the scene,” Pan Am’s engineers work closely with plane manufacturers to give you the greatest comfort, highest operating efficiency and utmost dependability. Right now, they’re concentrating on the Jet Clippers you’ll be flying tomorrow — just as they concentrated on the Clippers you’re flying today.
Pan Am’s Flight Crews are SUPERBLY QUALIFIED
In addition to undergoing training to rigorous U. S. standards — none more exacting — many of the present Clipper captains have flown more than 3 million miles. And Pan Am’s cabin attendants, recruited from all parts of the world, undergo intensive classroom and practical training before they first set foot aboard a Clipper. During the past 30 years, Clippers have carried more than 18 million passengers over a billion swift, comfortable and pleasant miles. And in doing so, Clippers have flown more than 65,000 Atlantic crossings . . . 40,000 Pacific crossings . . . 3500 trips Round the World.
A record without equal.
