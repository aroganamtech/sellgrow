Another step in the ladder towards mass travel had been climbed. Further progress was being made.
With the availability of additional aircraft, services were further expanded between the Mainland and Honolulu as schedules to the South Pacific were inaugurated in 1940.
Constellation Clippers Ordered in 1940
Still looking ahead with vision and courage while continually striving to develop finer aircraft which would provide better and more frequent services at lower costs, in 1940 Pan American ordered 40 Constellation Clippers. The first of these were due for delivery in 1942. Eleven were to be assigned for Pacific operations replacing all other equipment.
The foresighted plans, as approved by the Company in 1940, called for two daily schedules between California and Honolulu, twice weekly services between California and Hong Kong, and twice weekly between California and the South Pacific. The effect of this plan would have been to provide in 1942, 18 weekly departures both ways between the Mainland and the Hawaiian Islands.
The Parade of Progress was moving steadily ahead.
To provide further additional services prior to the availability of the Constellation Clippers, San Francisco and Los Angeles to Honolulu and return schedules were inaugurated in 1941 in addition to those to the Orient and the South Pacific.

BOEING CLIPPER—1939
Japs Jolt Parade
The Parade of Progress was suddenly jolted when on December 7, 1941, the Japs attacked Pearl Harbor; the United States was at war in the Pacific and the Atlantic. All efforts within and without the country were turned to defeat the enemy. The Constellation Clippers, scheduled to begin operations in 1942, were assigned for war needs. All aircraft and facilities of Pan American World Airways in the Pacific, as well as in all other parts of the world, were turned over to the armed forces. For the next four years Pan American’s operations in the Pacific were under contract to the Naval Air Transport Service. When these were terminated in the middle of November, 1945, Admiral J. W. Reeves, Jr., USN, Commanding Officer, Naval Air Transport Service said, in part: the Navy Department
“wishes to extend a ‘well done’ to the Pacific-Alaska Division of Pan American Airways System for their contribution toward meeting the Navy’s urgent need for air transportation both during and after world war two. As a part of the Naval Air Transport Service, the Pacific-Alaska Division aided materially in accomplishing the enormous task which was thrust upon us and thereby provided its share of which it can be justly proud in the overall war effort.”
On November 16, 1945, commercial services were resumed on daily schedules with extra sections as often as available aircraft permitted, with the best aircraft available, the Boeing 314 Clippers which had so distinguished themselves in their war services.
