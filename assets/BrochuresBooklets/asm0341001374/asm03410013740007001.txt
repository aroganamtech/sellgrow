special	rules on' ’SWING-BACK” trips
If your route involves a “swing-back” into the United States, consult your nearest Customs officer so that you won’t inadvertently lose your exemptions. For example, suppose you’re taking a Caribbean Tour, and on your way to the Virgin Islands you fly from Mexico to San Juan. You’ll have to go through U.S. Customs there, and if you use your exemptions on this part of the trip you’ll have to pay duty on later purchases when you return to the U. S. mainland.
If your family lives at the same address and makes the trip with you, exemptions can be combined and used to cover the total value of articles the family buys on your trip. One member of the family can declare for the whole group.
can you prove PRIOR POSSESSION?
Naturally you don’t want to pay duty on personal possessions you took with you from this country. To be safe, register any questionable article with Customs before beginning your trip. Personal and household effects, professional books, tools of your trade or occupation, or any valuable article (such as a watch or camera of foreign
manufacture) should be registered. In addition special regulations cover wild bird plumage or articles made with such plumage. Your Customs House will be glad to help you with any questions.
