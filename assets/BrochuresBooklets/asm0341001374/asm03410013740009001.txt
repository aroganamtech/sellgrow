Be sure you know exactly what you can bring into the United States. There are some things which no one can bring into the country. Some other articles always require payment of duty or the internal revenue tax.
Many kinds of fruits, vegetables, plants, meats and pets cannot be permitted through the customs unless they meet the requirements of the U. S. Department of Agriculture or the U. S. Public Health Service.
The advice of your Customs officials is helpful when you want to bring back articles bearing a trade mark — especially watches, perfumes, and accordions.
If a trade mark is registered with the U.S. Treasury Department, you must obtain written permission either from the trade mark owner or his American distributor before Customs will clear the article.
There is one exception. Usually each trade mark owner sets up definite export limitations on his product . . . allows you to bring back only a certain number or amount or value. However, if your purchase does not exceed these limitations — and you agree to have the label and other identifying marks removed — you may bring it in without formal permission.
Otherwise customs must hold the item — but will do so only for 30 days — after that they must return it to the seller or destroy it. See why we say it’s a good idea to inquire about any questionable purchase before you leave on your trip?
