If you’re a U.S. resident, your Uncle Sam gives you certain privileges as a returning tourist. For instance, you don’t have to pay duty on many articles you buy abroad. But that doesn’t mean you can just cram your suitcases and cruise through the customs. There are declarations to make. There are time limits to watch. And there’s the matter of what an article is worth. You’ll find most of these rules explained below.
WELL I DECLARE . . .
You’ll have to declare in writing anything acquired abroad which (1) you are bringing in for someone else at that person’s request; (2) you intend to sell or use in business; (3) you are bringing home for personal or household use or as a souvenir or curio; (4) all alterations and any repairs to articles taken abroad, other than necessary repairs to restore an article to the condition it was in when taken abroad.
But what about gifts or other articles you’ve sent on home ahead of your arrival ... or arranged to have shipped later? To include such articles under exemptions, you must declare them with the rest.
