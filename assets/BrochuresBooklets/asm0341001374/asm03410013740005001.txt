This is true when the total value of articles you acquired abroad (including cost of alterations or dutiable repairs to articles you took abroad) is not more than $25 ... if the articles are for your personal or household use and if you have the articles with you.
At any U.S. port of entry, a written declaration will probably be required to speed you through customs. In all cases you’ll have to make a written declaration if duty or internal revenue tax is assessed on some article you have with you.
To the customs, the value of an article is its current sales price. Your sales slips or receipts can be your proof. If bringing back something that was given to you, put down as closely as you can its fair market value. But in any case, don’t undervalue anything you acquired abroad! You’ll find your customs officer an authority on all kinds of merchandise, including art objects and souvenirs.
