In 1848, during the California Gold Rush, the oceans became a clippers racing its foamy lengths and 90,000 souls — "landlubbers” mostly — a voyage around the Horn. To the tune of salty sea chanties, Donald McKay’s "Flying Cloud” skimmed her way from Boston to California in the record time of 89 days!
How appropriate that the clipper should be called, "The Darling of the Seas.” No other ship in all maritime history so completely captured the imagination and affection of an applauding civilization. In the space of a few decades were written thrilling chapters in the saga of the most colorful ships that ever pointed lovely, graceful bows across the rolling seas. The clipper ship has become a symbol of an era when American ships, and the men who sailed them, led the way.
