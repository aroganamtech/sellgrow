BUILDING A

AIR TRANSPORT SYSTEM
ROM the 90-mile airline of 1927 between Key West and Havana, Pan American World Airways has vastly expanded. Leaving Miami in 1928, Pan American’s transports crisscrossed the Caribbean and soon served both coasts of South America.
In 1932, regular Pan American service was begun within Alaska, and in 1940 this huge territory was air-linked with the United States. In 1935, a Flying Clipper soared across the Pacific to establish the first regular air service to the Orient. Service to New Zealand was begun in 1940; to Australia in 1947.
In 1939, the Flying Clippers began scheduled trans-Atlantic service to Europe. Just prior to the War, Africa too was joined to Pan American’s global network.
By establishing service to 68 nations and territories, Pan American W orld A irways System has flown more than half a billion miles, and carried nearly six million passengers.
