Put yourself in his shoes . . .
If a patron seems irritated, impatient or unreasonable, put yourself in his shoes. Try to see his point of view and you’ll find yourself more than half way toward the goal of clearing up the difficulty. With your fellow workers it makes for smooth, pleasant teamwork. And in dealing with the public it’s a MUST for good public relations.
Little words do big jobs
“Please” . . . “Thank you” me” ... “I beg your pardon” these little words of courtesy do a big job in building better public relations.
Let’s form the habit of using them generously.
