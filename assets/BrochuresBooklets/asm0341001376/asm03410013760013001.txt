Distance doesn’t make the heart grow fonder . . .
Each added inch away from the phone equals extra miles your voice must travel to be understood. Speak directly into the mouthpiece, with your lips half an inch away.
W rite it down . . .
If a caller has a message for you to deliver—or asks you to do something—get it right by writing it down. Keep a pad and pencil beside the phone at all times.
Slurred words, dropped syllables and use of slang not only cause confusion that may take hours to correct, but are a sure way to lose friends and influence people to use some other means of transportation. If a caller’s
name is known, use it. Anyone appreciates this courtesy. Otherwise use “Sir” or “Madam.”
The art of hanging up . . .
Don’t spoil a pleasant conversation by breaking it off abruptly. Say “Goodbye” in your most pleasant voice. And to be sure the caller has finished, let him say “Goodbye” first.
