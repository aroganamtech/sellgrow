Lend a hand

. . . to the new employee. He’s probably as bewildered as you once were. The treatment we give him at the start may color his whole attitude. A sympathetic and helpful reception goes far toward making him a valuable member of our airline team.
The pros and cons . . .
. . . of a debate may sharpen your mind. But arguments only sharpen your temper and dull your influence with others. There’s no need to be a “yes-man” and agree with everyone. But arguing merely wastes time and convinces nobody.
A pat on the back . . .
. . . is worth two in the mind. If you see a fellow worker doing a good job, why not tell him so? And tell others. Because praise pays two ways. It encourages fellow workers to do their best, and it helps you get ahead as an agreeable, constructive member of the airline team.
Dependability . . .
. . . is the most important of all virtues on an airline. Without it the public loses confidence . . . and the airline team becomes lost in confusion. There’s one person from whom you should never accept an excuse—yourself. The worker who constantly makes excuses or passes the buck soon finds himself by-passed.
