80,000 letters—that’s the number of letters contained in a ton of air mail . . . And a ton of mail is a normal part of a Clipper cargo. Many a Clipper has carried much more than that since war began . . . 80,000 letters—80,000 or more separate ideas, or instructions or official orders connected with winning the war aboard each transoceanic Clipper.
Passengers are important, of course,—but so also is mail, particularly V-mail for our fighting men.
And Pan American not only carries V-mail, PAA also helped develop it. The photographic process was devised by the Eastman Kodak Company. Its continued development for use in air mail was pioneered by Pan American Airways, Imperial Airways (now British Overseas Airways), and Eastman Kodak Company.
On board a great, 42-ton trans-ocean Clipper goes the U. S. Mail. In a single year, such air mail shipments run into ^thousands of tons.
