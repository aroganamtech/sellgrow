An airline’s most precious possession is not its planes ... It is the "know-how” of operations— the whole scientific background of such things as flight training, radio, meteorology, engine overhaul and, above all, navigation.
Even before the U. S. was at war—in 1940—Pan American placed its whole fund of technological information, built up by millions of miles of overocean flight, at the disposal of the Army and the Navy . . . "Flying classrooms” for the Army and Navy flying cadets were inaugurated at Miami. . . In 1943, Pan American also opened a Navy Flight Mechanics School at La Guardia Field.
Three hundred and twenty-five graduates of these schools had been decorated for gallantry in action up to January 1, 1944 . . . And many more have been decorated since.
