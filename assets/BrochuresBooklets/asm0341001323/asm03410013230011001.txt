“OVER THE HUMP’-TOUGHEST AIR ROUTE IN THE WORLD
The peaks, capped with ice, are 20,000 feet high. Deep jungles cover the valleys below . . . Yet two American CNAC pilots walked out of this country alive from a forced landing!
On this page appear two very rare photographs taken by CNAC (China National Aviation Corporation) pilots. They show you how the 20,000 foot "Hump” looks to the men who fly it—when they can see it . . . Fifty per cent of the flying over this route is done "blind” on instruments.
Here, flying on oxygen four miles above sea level, CNAC’s unarmed transports are often menaced by Jap fighters . . . Five CNAC planes have been shot down with loss of both American and Chinese flight crews.
When CNAC pioneered this "lifeline”, most of this country was uncharted . . . Much of it still is. But Chinese troops, and such materials as tin ingots, mercury, tungsten and antimony are flown out regularly, and gasoline, badly-needed spare parts and much war material (some manufactured in the U. S.) are flown in regularly. This supply route was pioneered by CNAC before Pearl Harbor.
