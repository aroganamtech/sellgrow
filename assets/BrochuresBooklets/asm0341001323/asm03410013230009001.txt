THOUSANDS OF TONS OF AIR CARGO
GO OVERSEAS
Red Cross Supplies — Iry Clipper. Here, at La Guardia Field, 1,810 pounds of medical sup-plies go aboard.
How many tons? . .. Well, in a single year—1943— Pan American Airways System carried 84,545,010 pounds of air cargo. This compares with 26,459,057 pounds in 1942 and 6,157,255 in 1940.
But the quantity, often, is less important than the speedy—not to mention dependability, because air cargo moves out of reach of the submarine. Serum or penicillin or plasma is needed to save lives . . . Vital radio tubes or a radar part is needed in a hurry!
"Send it by Clipper”, the order goes out.
And overnight, it is in Honolulu, or England, or Alaska, or well on its way to Africa, the Middle East, India, or—(westward), on its way to the South Pacific.
Vital War Materiel goes aboard a huge Coronado operated by PAA. Teamed PAA Flight Crews fly ships like this all over the world.
