GOVERNMENT OFFICIALS -CIVILIANS-ON WAR MISSIONS
Diplomacy, politics, economic warfare and the publicizing of facts about the U. S. and the United Nations are all part of the business of winning a war. Here, too, the swift, sure wings of the giant Clippers have played an important role.
On wings, presidents, princes, production chiefs and Ambassadors Extraordinary can get together fast! In certain cases (like Lisbon, Portugal) where a bomber would be interned immediately, the unarmed Clipper, representing a peacetime commercial organization, can land in a neutral country and depart without hindrance.
Of the 98,582 miles of air routes pioneered by Pan American World Airways before Pearl Harbor, some 82,000 miles are still being maintained on regular, scheduled flight despite the loss of bases due to the war in the Pacific.
Goodwill Mission took Vice-President Henry A. Wallace to Latin America in 1943. Here he was snapped boarding a PAA Clipper at Miami, Florida. On this flying trip he visited seven Latin American republics.
