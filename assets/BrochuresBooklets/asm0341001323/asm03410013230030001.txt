THE LIFE-LINE THAT HELPED CHASE ROMMEL OUT OF AFRICA
That great correspondent, the late Raymond Clapper went out across Africa early in 1942—a short time after Pearl Harbor. He wrote: 'T went out across the new air supply route that Pan American Airways was throwing across the heart of Africa to transport spare parts and light supplies and personnel to the Middle East, India and China. That airline was an amazing demonstration of what could be done quickly by air.'’’’
Five different uniforms . . . America, New Zealand, Australia, Britain and South Africa, meet on their way to fight Rommel over the PAA-built Africa route . . . Man at table is checking passenger list.
"That airline”—at least the trans-Africa portion of it—was completed before Pearl Harbor. Scheduled flights began on it 61 days after President Roosevelt had authorized its construction. . . . A continent was spanned and a vital supply route established through to Alexandria, Egypt. Over this route went some of the supplies which helped chase Rommel out of Africa for good!
Surface temperatures of airplanes sometimes reached 180°. PAA mechanics had to wear gloves and boots to handle metal and walk on wings.
Everything but the waiters was brought from the U. S. A. Note air-conditioning machines (black) and refrigerators (white) along the walls. By keeping its men jit, PAA finished its construction fob in 61 days.
