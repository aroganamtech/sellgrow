COFFEE FOR THE ARMY AND RUBBER -
FLOWN BY CLIPPER
Coffee, like fine wines, is better some years than others. There is no way to tell which beans are best until you taste the coffee brewed from them.
So when the U. S. Army wanted to buy huge quantities of the best Brazilian coffee, they wisely insisted they had to taste it first . . . Samples were flown by Clipper to the U. S., saving at least two weeks in transit . . . The order was cabled back.
Everybody knows what happened to our rubber supply when the Japs took the East Indies and the Malay peninsula. Some people know that the best synthetic rubber tires cannot be made without adding some natural rubber. But few people realize that Pan American has been flying latex (natural rubber) from Brazil to the United States.
In-going Clippers, operated for the Rubber Development Corporation, carry men, equipment, and medical supplies . . . Out-going Clippers carry latex from Manaos (Brazil)"to Miami.
Enough for an Army! In the tropical sunshine of Fazenda dos Palmeiras Luiz Pinto, Brazil, a worker turns the drying beans—"making coffee while the sun shines.'’'’
4-engined Clipper, built by Sikorsky, loads latex at a pier on the Amazon. This latex will go into jeep tires, tank treads and a thousand other places in U. S. war equipment.
The man way down there at the fool of all those coffee bags is poking a "fryer” into the bag held by the worker . . . Thus he will get a sample and know the quality and age of the beans.
