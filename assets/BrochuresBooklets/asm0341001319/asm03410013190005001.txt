He also decided that henceforth the first pilot would be called "captain” and the co-pilot "first officer.”
In 1931 the first four-engine aircraft ever flown commercially, the graceful Sikorsky S-40 flying boat with a range of 800 miles and room for 40 passengers, went into service for Pan Am. The first one was christened American Clipper® and from that time until today all Pan Am multi-engine aircraft have been called Clippers—a name with a proud heritage.
During 1934 Pan Am flew more than 100,-000 passengers. It was now operating 85 aircraft over a network that had expanded to almost 32,000 miles throughout Central and South America, the Caribbean and Alaska.
Those were truly exciting days, and the opening of a new route was always the occasion for a celebration. When the first Pan Am plane arrived at a new destination, it would be met by cheering crowds and bands playing and speeches welcoming "nuestros amigos de los Estados Unidos". The people had good reason to be joyous. The arrival of Pan Am into many remote areas quite literally linked them, for the first time, to the rest of the world.
