Incredible as it seems, only two decades had passed since Pan Am started operations with a two-plane fleet, 24 employees and one 90-mile route between Key West and Havana. Now, as it celebrated its 20th anniversary, it had 19,000 employees, including 129 pilots who had flown over a million miles, 20 who had flown two million miles. In 20 years Pan Am had flown more than seven million passengers, and was now serving 62 countries on every continent.
In 1948 Pan Am led the way to lower air fares by introducing tourist class service between New York and San Juan. The fare was cut from $133 to $75. In time, tourist class would be offered on all routes — a big step toward making air travel possible for everyone.
That same year Pan Am went to the rescue of West Berlin by participating in the Berlin Airlift. Adding its planes and crews to those of the U.S. military, it helped save the city from starvation by making more than two thousand flights carrying food and other supplies from West Germany.
