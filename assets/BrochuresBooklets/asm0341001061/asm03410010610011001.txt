RUBBER. Brazilian rubber took on heightened importance during World War II when supplies from the East Indies were cut off, and Brazilian rubber often was flown out from the Amazon region on flying “rubber specials”. Here a worker smokes the latex (the milky juice secreted by the rubber tree). As the smoke congeals the rubber, additional layers are added by pouring fresh latex onto the growing ball.

Brazil is one of the most air-minded Republics in the Western Hemisphere. Its aviation both commercial and military has made great strides forward during the last ten years. Its air transport system not only services the country well but also other nations and continents. Panair do Brasil, which was the first national company to inaugurate night flying in South America, is the first in all Latin America to have inaugurated service over the South Atlantic on a route which used to be flown only by European companies before the war. Now great airplanes link Rio de Janeiro with Lisbon, London and Paris, branching off from Portugal to Italy and Egypt. Pan American World Airways flights from New York to Lisbon make
connections with the flights of its affiliate from Lisbon to Rome.
Domestic air service in Brazil is ample and complete. There is for instance Panair’s Amazon River service which starts in Belem goes through Manaos and ends in Iquitos, Peru, nearly three thousand miles inland. Also the Rio-Sao Paulo-Matto Grosso-Paraguay service, or the Rio-Minas Gerais-Goyaz service, or the Belem-Rio coastal and direcLroutes, or the Rio-Sao Paulo-Porto Alegre service.
