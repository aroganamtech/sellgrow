“Flight,” a three-story-tall sculpture, created by the famous artist Richard Lippold, adorns the Vanderbilt Avenue lobby. This majestic gold wire sculpture suggests the performance and shapes of modern aircraft and the excitement and exultation of air travel as visualized by the artist. Consisting of seven cones containing 113 wire strands, the sculpture extends 31 feet for a total of 24,521 feet of gold wire. It is best viewed from the balcony.
The work of Gyorgy Kepes can be seen on display as you enter the Pan Am Building from 45th Street. Suspended over the second floor balcony level and covering a span of 40 feet, Mr. Kepes’ work is composed of 2 screens of aluminum hovering on stainless steel bars.
The Josef Albers mural in the lobby above the entrance of Grand Central terminal is 28 feet high and 55 feet wide. Outspoken in form, this mural is based upon the exploration of universal phenomena. Using laminated material in black, medium red, and white, the artist has created a design of optical mixtures.
Sculptor Robert Berk’s bust of the late Erwin S. Wolfson, originator and builder of the Pan Am Building, is situated in the main lobby.
Some of the other interesting features of the building include three international dining facilities and an attractive shopping area on the mezzanine floor.
Some quick statistics on the world’s largest
commercial office building:
•	45,000 tons of steel were used in the structural framework.
•	Workers ride on the world’s fastest high-rise bank of elevators, which move at 1,750 feet per minute.
•	More than 80 miles of cable were used in the elevator complex, which is the largest single internal transportation system ever installed in a building.
•	There are 302,000 square feet of Venetian blinds.
•	3,500,000 square feet of masonry went into the building.
•	Approximately 7,500 persons in 75 different trades were involved in the construction.
•	The architects and designers were Emery Roth & Sons, Walter Gropius (The Architects Collaborative), and Pietro Belluschi.
•	The building cost nearly $100,000,000.
