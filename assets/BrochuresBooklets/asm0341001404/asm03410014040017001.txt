PROGRAM PLANNING
OBJECTIVES. Any objective that involves competition between individuals is a logical candidate for a travel incentive contest. A direct increase in sales (the most common), increased production, improved plant and office safety, reduced tardiness and absenteeism, better customer and public relations . . . note that all bear on over-all corporate profit.
BUDGET. Common procedure is to estimate how much more business could be developed with a good incentive . . . determine how much this would mean in added profit . . . decide how much of the added profit to allot to the incentive travel program. A realistic appraisal of the potential market is essential.
QUOTAS. In a sales incentive program, aim at motivating your average producer. Experience shows top producers will respond, poor producers won’t, in any case. Average producers are most capable of improvement. And being your largest group, their improvement will result in the greatest total increase.
Quotas set too high result in a race between top producers, demoralize the bulk of your sales team. Because everyone can win, an unlimited or “open end” program allowing an undetermined number of “qualifiers” to win produces better results than a plan which sets a specific number of winners. In the latter, top producers always win.
Make sure quotas are fair. When establishing individual quotas keep territory, accounts, experience in mind. One solution — set each man to competing with his own previous record.
Finally, choose your trip (or trips) to fit your quotas, rather than vice versa.
MECHANICS OF SUCCESS. Most incentive travel contests run from 30 to 90 days. For trips to distant destinations, contest length is sometimes extended to six months.
Start from the day of the kickoff meeting. Take full advantage of initial enthusiasm.
