Flying with Pan American
Travel-comfort and dependable transportation for its patrons is the sole object of Pan American passenger service. This means all the conveniences of established surface transportation, operating on schedule, with a speed that cuts days to hours, and months to weeks. Aboard Pan American Airliners, and throughout the System, all personnel are guided by one purpose—your ease and security. In flight, a steward serves refreshments—observes your every want and need. At all stations, offices, in airport Aerocars, as well as en route, this policy prevails. Customs officers check you through almost without your attention; your trip will be carefree.
The cabins of airliners are appropriately and restfully finished, and there is plenty of room to move about. In winter they are pleasantly warm; in summer they are cooled by the crystal clear upper air. The ships are immaculate, inside and out.
Distinguished and interesting people travel with Pan American. It's the smart way. Why not enjoy the fellowship of a trip with them?
Liners of the Air
Pan American's 99.7 per cent perfect performance record is the result of three prudent operating principles: First, multi-motored equipment is used exclusively for passenger service; second, the loads carried are limited to fully 20% less than Department of Commerce regulations, thus insuring at least one engine in reserve —passengers and every pound of mail pass over the scales; and third, the most elaborate, thorough, and expensive equipment maintenance system known to air transportation is employed. All airliners en route communicate by two-way radio, constant weather reports and other operating information. Each ship is in charge of a pilot with more than 2000 hours experience. Pilot, co-pilot, radio operator, and steward make up a ship's crew of four men.
Pan American, Miami Terminals
Visitors are always welcomed. They may enjoy the observation terrace, tea in the Spanish Garden, overlooking the field, dine in one of the little "national" dining rooms, or accept the services of a guide who will conduct them through the hangars and shops. The Pan American International Airport at Miami stands out among the fine Air Terminals of the world. Sweeping across 640 acres of smooth land, six miles of coral runways parallel the prevailing winds. Lawns and flowers lend color to the impressive station building and hangars. Great Airliners, perfectly groomed and gleaming, land and depart at short intervals. A tall radio tower rises in the distance sending out its electric voice, to which half a world is listening.
Visitors are likewise welcome at Dinner Key, the Pan American Airways marine airport where the giant flying yachts leave for Nassau, Jamaica and Panama.

IMPORTANT
Mail deposited after closing hour will be delayed from one to several days. Be sure to check with your Postmaster the closing hours for air mail to Latin American destinations. Inquiries may also be mailed or phoned to Pan American offices at New York, Miami, or Brownsville, Texas.
The U. S. Post Office Department requires that the correct amount of postage be affixed to each article to be sent by air mail to the above countries. Carelessness in this matter may cause serious delay to your mail.

BAGGAGE ALLOWANCE
For Havana, Nassau, West Indies or Central America (via Miami or Brownsville): 30 lbs. (or 14 kilograms) free on full-fare ticket (on half-fare ticket 15 lbs. or 7 kilograms). Excess baggage of 20 lbs. carried when Capacity of plane permits at ■/j of 1% of standard tariff per pound. Within Mexico or on Pan American-Grace Airways, Inc., free allowance is 15 kilograms; half-fare allowance as above; excess allowance of 10 kilos when space available; in Mexico excess rate is at 1% of standard tariff per kilogram. Because of limited weight allowance for baggage, only the most necessary articles should be carried and the bag itself should be as light as possible.
AUTOMOBILE TRANSPORTATION
On Pan American Airways, Inc., or on Cia. Mexicana de Aviacion, S. A., a charge will be made to the passenger for automobile transportation to or from airports wherever necessary, exceptions being at overnight airway stops in the case of passengers in transit through that point; also except in the case of passengers transferring at Miami or Brownsville to or from a through connection at those points. Charges will be made according to the locality.
Pan American Airliners of various types equipped with two-way radio, full crew and complete facilities may be chartered for special trips or cruises. Send your inquiries to any Pan American Airways office or leading travel bureaus.
Clothing
Light weight clothing should be carried for the tropics, but in the high altitudes of Mexico and Guatemala, and during winter season in Southern countries of South America, heavy clothing should be carried.

HEAVY BAGGAGE
Trunks and other articles handled as baggage by Rail Service under effective tariffs will be checked to or from Miami upon presentation of through rail tickets. Such baggage may be rechecked at Miami through P. A. A. which has made arrangements for the handling of such excess baggage at the excess baggage rates of the F. E. C. Ry. and P. & O. S. S. Co. to Havana and the Munson S. S. Co. from Miami to Nassau, or, southbound baggage may be checked through to Havana from point of origin provided all charges are collected at point of origin. For Mexico, heavy baggage can be checked to or from Brownsville by rail to and from points in the United States, and will be shipped by rail express between Brownsville, Tampico and Mexico City.
TICKETS, PASSPORTS, CUSTOMS CLEARANCES
Tickets will contain the following clause:
"Purchaser must be in possession of a regular passport with proper visas where the same are required by the laws of any country to be flown into. The purchaser must arrive at the airport of departure early enough before the scheduled time of departure to enable passport inspection and baggage customs to be completed."
LIMITS — RESERVATIONS — STOP-OVERS — CANCELLATION
Airways tickets are not transferable and will be limited to date and plane on which space has been reserved. Space reserved must be paid for when confirmed. Stop-over tickets are sold only at the point-to-point rate.
Tickets may be cancelled only on formal notice received by the Traffic Department of Pan American Airways System five days previous to date of flight. Without such notice, refunds on tickets will be made only if the total capacity of the plane shall be sold out including the space cancelled.
