Flight By Night
Everything takes on a different aspect at night, and flying is no exception. Those flashes from the engines, for instance. They’re merely the exhaust, and were there during the day, too — but invisible because of the sunlight. Should there be a mist, or a light rain, the whole side of the Clipper may seem to light up in a flash of red, or green, depending on the side you’re seated on. St. Elmo’s fire? No, merely our running lights, reflected from the drops of moisture.
In Preparation For The Improbable
The chances that your Clipper ever will have to make a water landing are so negligible that they’re hardly worth discussing. But we’re completely in accord with the Boy Scout motto, “Be Prepared”, so let’s talk about it a bit.
Actually, a Clipper could remain aloft on any two of its four engines, and it has an almost unlimited reserve of fuel on every flight. So you can see how unlikely the prospects of a water landing are. But if you should have to come down on a liquid surface, you might like to know that Clippers are made of sturdy stuff. They’ll withstand the slight shock of landing without difficulty. And remain afloat for many hours after they have touched the water.
As far as safety equipment goes, there’s a raft of it, if you’ll pardon the pun. Hundreds of pounds of the latest gear, in fact, and all of it tested before you boarded the plane.
