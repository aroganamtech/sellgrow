Doesn't the Pilot Know
Where He's Headed?
He most certainly does, but sometimes during day flights he may bank and turn from one heading to another, in quick succession. It’s just a way of finding out wind direction and velocity called a “multiple drift sight”.
Do The Engines Sound Different?
That shift in the tone of the engines may be due to the pilot’s changing the pitch of the propellers. “Pitch” regulates the size of the bite they take from the air. Or perhaps he’s reducing power after reaching his proper altitude. Or it could be that he’s synchronizing his engines.
Are You Sure My Baggage Is Aboard?
Yes, indeed. It’s stored below in the cargo hold, to insure proper weight distribution. And when you deplane, it will be only a matter of minutes before you can claim it with your check. However, during flight or at transit stops, baggage stowed in the cargo hold is inaccessible, except in cases of real emergency.
