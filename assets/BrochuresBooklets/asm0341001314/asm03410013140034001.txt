W hen you pick up a telephone, anywhere, and call Pan American Reservations, either directly or through your favorite Travel Agent, you have at your command one of the best-integrated service systems in all the world. You can get a round-trip seat, and have it confirmed — even around the entire world — in five minutes or less.
Smooth functioning of Pan American Reservations is due to a unique method of operation, plus a global network of communications— by telephone and Teletype. Pan American's world is divided up into a number of control points — cities at which Clipper flights originate. Visual boards at these control points show seat availability on flights. Reservations are received, by wire, from Pan American offices over the entire system.
All stations are notified immediately when seats for a given flight are sold out, within

Pan American Reservations is a busy department — more extensive than most people would ever believe. In New York Reservations alone as many as 22,000 telephone calls per week are handled. New York, Miami, and San Francisco are central Teletype distributing centers in the United States, with direct connections with all Pan American offices and stations in this country and abroad. Amsterdam is the Teletype distributing center in Europe. The chattering Teletype room in New York Reservations has handled 45,000 Teletype messages in a single 24-hour period.
When you call Pan American Reservations you're in touch with the world — wherever you want to go — whenever you want to go.
