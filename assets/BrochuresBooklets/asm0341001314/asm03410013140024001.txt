Radio Network—with
Radio, communications, electronic flying aids—these are things that aid in making travel by Clipper anywhere so dependable and sure, At tremendous expense, and through great engineering effort, Pan American has now extended Radiotelephone communications around the world. This means that whether over ocean or land, your Clipper Captain is in continuous two-way voice communication with the ground — with no codes or translation necessary. As a single example of the pioneering involved, Pan American installed a powerful Very High Frequency station atop 10,000-foot Mount Zugspitze in the Bavarian Alps, a new and important link in a globe-circling Radiotelephone chain. Pan American’s combined radio, Radiotelephone, and Teletype facilities have indeed created a “One World” in communications that is invaluable in every phase of its operation.
This same kind of pioneering has been applied in the case of electronic flying devices and navigational aids. Over land areas, Clippers use the conventional Radio Beam and Omni-Directional direction finder. Over water, Pan American was first to use war-developed Loran (Long Range Air Navigation)—an electronic marvel, related to radar, for establishing position. To make all landings fair weather landings, Pan American has been a leader in the development and adoption of the best-known technical aids. This was the first airline to use GCA (Ground Control Radar Approach) and has regularly used ILAS (Instrument Low Approach System). If there’s anything new in the science of flying, Pan American knows about it. If it’s tested and wholly dependable, Pan American is using it.
