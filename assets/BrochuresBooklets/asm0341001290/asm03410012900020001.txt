B an American crossed the threshold of the J et Age with the delivery of the first Boeing 707 Jet Clipper. The 707-121 was America’s first commercial jet transport.
Powered by four Pratt & Whitney JT-3 engines of 13,000 pounds thrust each, it has a cruising speed close to 600 miles an hour at altitudes between 25,000 and 40,000 feet. Maximum cruising range is about 4,400 miles. In “dual configuration,” each 707-121 carries 40 First Class President Special passengers and 71 Economy Class passengers.
Joining the Clipper fleet in 1959 was the Boeing 707-321 Intercontinental Jet Clipper. This long-range version of the 707 is powered by four Pratt & Whitney JT-4 engines, each developing 15,800 pounds thrust. Its range is up to 5,300 miles, and its cruising speed is almost 600 miles an hour. The Intercontinental, which can carry 120 First Class or 177 Economy Class passengers or a combination of both, is now in ’round the world service.
The longest range commercial jet aircraft in the world, the Boeing 707-321B Super Intercontinental Jet Clipper, made its first flight January 31,1962. Powered by four Pratt & Whitney JT3D-3 turbofan engines, each developing 18,000 pounds thrust at takeoff, the 321B has a range of 6,000 miles and cruises at around 600 miles per hour.
The latest development in Jet aircraft propulsion, the turbofan engine offers the proven reliability of the turbojet engine plus substantially greater power and operating efficiency. Because they can carry greater payloads, take off from shorter runways and their engines deliver more cruise thrust per gallon of fuel, turbofan-powered Jet Clippers are ideal for both short and long trips.
Super Intercontinental J et Clippers fly 20 per cent farther with a full payload of 125 passengers and five tons of cargo than the longest-range jets now in operation. They are capable of flying non-stop between New York and Buenos Aires, London and Los Angeles, New York and Moscow or Honolulu and Sydney.
