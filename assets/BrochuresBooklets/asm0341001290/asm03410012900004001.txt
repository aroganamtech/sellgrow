FOKKER F-7
In 1927, the year of its birth, Pan American began flying mail and passengers over a 90-mile route between Key West, Florida and Havana, Cuba.
Since there were no seaplanes in the United States that filled the requirements of overwater flight, a land plane was chosen. It was the FOKKER F-7, powered by three Wright 220-horsepower engines, any two of which were enough to keep it in flight in case of
failure of the third. The F-7 cruised at 90 miles-an-hour, a respectable speed in those days. The wing was made of plywood, and was fitted with special compartments so that it would remain afloat if forced down at sea. It carried 8 passengers, if none brought luggage along.
