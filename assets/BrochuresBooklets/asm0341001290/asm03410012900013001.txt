BOEING 307
One of the first land planes was the BOEING 307, the “grand-daddy” of the Boeing Strato-Clippers. It was the first pressurized cabin landplane ever used in regular commercial flying; the first able to fly passengers comfortably and safely above troublesome weather conditions. It was powered by four Wright Cyclone 1,100-horsepower engines,
and could cruise at 185 miles-an-hour at an altitude of 15,000 feet. The wingspan was 107 feet, 3 inches, length was 74 feet, 4 inches, and had a gross take-off weight of 45,000 pounds. The 307 had an 1,100 mile range, and consumed 165 gallons of gasoline an hour. It could carry 33 passengers.
