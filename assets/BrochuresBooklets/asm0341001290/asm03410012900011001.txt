BOEING B-314, THE YANKEE CLIPPER
The BOEING B-314, PAN AM’S YANKEE CLIPPER, was the last and largest of the seaplane fleet. It inaugurated the first scheduled trans-Atlantic service in 1939. Weighing 42 tons, the Yankee Clipper had a wing span of 152 feet, and a length of 106 feet. The four Wright engines delivered 1,500-horsepower each. The engines were the first that were
accessible to the Flight Engineer for repair while in flight. The B-314 showed that flying had come of age from the standpoint of comfort, luxury and utility. Like an ocean liner, it had staterooms . . . including a bridal suite ... and passengers could relax in overstuffed chairs and sofas. It could carry 70 passengers.
