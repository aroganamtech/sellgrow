SIKORSKY S-42, AMERICAN CLIPPER
S-42 used flush riveting to reduce air drag, wing “flaps” to reduce landing speed, and constant-speed propellers. Because of the length of the over-water flights, a flight engineer was added to the crew. The S-42 was assigned to regular service in the Caribbean and on such routes as New York - Bermuda. As the American Clipper, an S-42 surveyed the routes across the Pacific and over the Atlantic to Europe.
By 1934, Pan Am was ready to cross the oceans. In that year, the SIKORSKY S-42 joined the fleet. This was America’s first true ocean transport. Built to rigid specifications which required a plane to fly 2,400 miles with a safe fuel reserve, and operate with one engine out of commission, the S-42 broke all performance records for flying boats.
