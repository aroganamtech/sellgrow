In 1959, Pan Am began taking delivery of its fleet of Douglas DC-8’s. These aircraft, like the 707-321 “Intercontinental”, are powered by Pratt & Whitney engines of 15,800-pound thrust, and have a cruising speed close to 600 miles-an-hour. The cruising altitude is between 25,000 and 40,000 feet and the range is over 5,000 miles.
The DC-8’s passenger capacity is 109 President, 141 Tourist, or 168 Economy, or a combination “dual configuration.”
This is the first commercial airplane to eliminate overhead or sidewall utilities, with all the passenger utilities, such as: stewardess call buttons, adjustable air inlets, reading lights, folding tables, first aid or emergency oxygen, etc. built right into the “Unit-ized seat”, offering “finger tip” luxury to the passenger.
Jet transports are now the true mass transportation airplanes. One Pan Am Jet Clipper, for example, because of its great capacity — almost double that of the DC-7C — and its more than nine mile-a-minute speed, can carry as many trans-Atlantic passengers in a year as the Queen Mary.
Few cities are more than half-a-day away by Jet Clipper. The “Intercontinental” now flies around the world in less than 40 hours flying time.
These planes are proof that, whatever tomorrow may bring to the field of commercial aviation, Pan American’s planning, pioneering and progress will be a vital part of it.
