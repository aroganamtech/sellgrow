SIKORSKY S-36
By the end of 1928, Pan Am’s routes were expanded to 251 miles, across the Caribbean and to the north coast of South America.
These areas were new to flying, many of the over-water crossings were beyond the range of the aircraft being used and, there were very few airports in the area. It was obvious that a new type plane had to be developed ... one which could land on water
as well as on land.
The result was the SIKORSKY S-36 ... the ancestor of Pan Am’s long line of famous flying Clippers. Thirty-four feet long, and with a wingspread of 72 feet, the 105 mile-an-hour plane was powered by two 200-horsepower engines. It carried 8 passengers.
