BOEING 377, STRATO-CLIPPER
In 1949, Pan Am took a giant step forward with the BOEING 377 ... the double-decked Stratocruiser, also called the Strato-Clipper. It featured a lower-deck lounge . . . completely private staterooms . . . sleeperette service for all passengers. As a standard first class flight, it accommodated 61 passengers.
A modified version, the Super Stratocruiser, went into service in 1954. Technical improvements such as reducing propeller revolutions per minute while keeping the same
3,500-horsepower of each of the four Pratt & Whitney engines; combined with the new turbo-superchargers, and extra wing-tip fuel tanks, made possible non-stop service from New York to London or Paris regardless of weather.
Wing span was 141 feet, 3 inches and length was 110 feet, 4 inches. Its weight was 145,800 pounds. The 377 had a cruising speed of 320 miles-an-hour, with an operating range of 4,500 miles, and a normal operating altitude from 15,000 to 25,000 feet.
