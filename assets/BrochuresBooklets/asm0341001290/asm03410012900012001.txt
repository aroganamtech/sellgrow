^/\lfter war broke out in Europe, the Clippers were crowded with diplomats, refugees and thousands of pounds of high priority mail.
When World War II struck, the 11 great trans-ocean Clippers, nine Boeing 314s and two Martin 130s, were the only planes capable of carrying big loads of men and supplies to the battle fronts and the build-up areas. (Altogether, Pan Am at this time, had 148 planes of varying size, but only 11 trans-ocean.) Doing the biggest overseas flying job of any airline contractor to the Armed Services, Pan Am flew the staggering total of 346,000,000 miles.
While the Clippers were operating over war-time routes, Pan Am continued to pioneer with new equipment and changed over from flying boats to land planes . . . with all their advantages in speed, capacity, and a wider choice of routes and landing fields.
