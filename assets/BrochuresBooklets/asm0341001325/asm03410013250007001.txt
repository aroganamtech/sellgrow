“AIRLIFT”—new word, new idea

As a rule, airlift means a pooling of commercial and military air transports to meet an emergency.
Perhaps the most famous airlift was the “Cannonball Express” to Africa and India in World War II. Then there was the historic “Berlin Milk Run”. . . and the current Korean airlift. Pan American has taken a major part in all three.
Under the direction of the Military Air Transport Service, the Korean airlift went into operation only four days after the invasion. Ten airlines provided
45 four-engine airplanes with crews. Pan American World Airways operated 24 aircraft, more than half of which came from its own Clipper* fleet.
Through these airlifts, Pan American is co-operating with the Civil Aeronautics Board in executing one of the vital tasks assigned to it by the Congress: “ . . . the encouragement and development of an air-transportation system properly adapted to the present and future needs ... of the national defense.” Pan American World Airways, New York 17, N. Y.
